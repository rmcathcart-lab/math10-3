The Gear Ring

Five gears, A to E, sit in a ring. Each gear’s teeth fit into the teeth of the two gears beside it.

You turn gear A clockwise (the arrow).

Which way does gear E turn?

A clockwise
B counterclockwise
C it can’t turn at all
C: the whole ring is stuck! Two gears that touch always turn opposite ways. Go around the ring: A clockwise, B counter, C clockwise, D counter, so E clockwise. But E also touches A, and A is clockwise too. Two touching gears can’t turn the same way, so nothing moves. With 4 or 6 gears in the ring, it works. Strategies for Spatial Puzzles

A spatial puzzle is a puzzle about shapes and space: fitting pieces together, turning them, flipping them or folding them. A strategy is a plan for solving a problem. Good puzzle solvers know several strategies and pick the one that fits.

Key idea   Problem-solving strategies
Draw or modelSketch the puzzle, or use real pieces that you can move around.
Guess and checkTry something. If it does not work, change it a little and try again.
Make a systematic listList every case in an order, so you do not miss one or count one twice.
Look for a patternSolve small cases, see how the answers grow, and use that to predict.
Simplify the problemSolve a smaller or easier version first.
Eliminate possibilitiesRule out the choices that cannot work, so fewer are left to try.
Work backwardStart from the end and undo the steps (you will use this for games in Lesson 2).
Try another approachSolve it a second way to check (verify) your answer.
Four steps for any puzzle: 1. Understand the rules. 2. Plan: pick a strategy. 3. Solve. 4. Check that your answer follows the rules.

The Tangram

A tangram is a very old puzzle from China. A square is cut into 7 pieces: 2 large triangles, 1 medium triangle, 2 small triangles, 1 square and 1 parallelogram. (A parallelogram is a four-sided shape whose opposite sides are parallel, like a slanted rectangle.) The goal is to use the pieces to make a given shape. Its outline, filled in, is called a silhouette.

Key idea   Tangram rules:   Lay the pieces flat.   Pieces touch but do not overlap.   You may turn and flip any piece.   Use exactly the pieces the puzzle asks for.

Try it: the 7 pieces

Drag a piece to move it. Tap it to turn it 45°. Double-tap it to flip it over. Corners snap together.

4 small triangles cover a large triangle; 2 cover the medium triangle, the square and the parallelogram. Fraction = area \div 16.
(b) What is the area of the whole square, in small triangles? Check by adding all 7 pieces.
Whole square: 16 small triangles. Check: 4+4+2+1+1+2+2=16 ✓ (Fractions check too: \tfrac14+\tfrac14+\tfrac18+\tfrac1{16}+\tfrac1{16}+\tfrac18+\tfrac18=1.)
(c) A tangram is cut from a square of card 20 cm by 20 cm. Find the area of each kind of piece.
Whole square: 20\times20=400 cm². Small triangle: 400\div16=25 cm². Medium triangle, square, parallelogram: 2\times25=50 cm² each. Large triangle: 4\times25=100 cm².
Estimate first: 400 cut into 16 equal parts is a bit more than 400 ÷ 20 = 20. On the TI-30XIIS: 20 x² ÷ 16 ENTER gives 25.
!
Watch out. The square, the medium triangle and the parallelogram look very different, but they have the same area (2 small triangles each). Same area does not mean same shape.

Making Shapes with Tangram Pieces

Use your cut-out pieces (the cut-out page is at the end of the lesson in your booklet). These tips use the strategies from Section 1.

Key idea   Tangram tips
  1. Check the area first (eliminate possibilities). The pieces you use must add up to the area of the silhouette.
  2. Place the biggest pieces first. They fit in the fewest places, so there are fewer guesses to try.
  3. Look at the corners. A square corner (90°) of the silhouette needs the square corner of a triangle, a corner of the square piece, or two sharp (45°) corners side by side.
  4. Guess and check. If a gap is left that no piece fits, move one piece and try again. Remember you may flip pieces.

Pentominoes: Finding All 12

A polyomino is a shape made of equal squares joined along full sides. A domino has 2 squares. A shape made of 5 squares is a pentomino.

Key idea   Two pentominoes are the same if one can be turned (rotated) or flipped over (reflected) to fit exactly on top of the other. So all four of these are the same pentomino:
Example 5

Pentomino or not? Same or different?

(a) Is each shape a pentomino? Write yes or no. If no, say why.
(i) yes   (ii) no: it has only 4 squares   (iii) no: two squares touch only at a corner   (iv) no: the top squares sit on half a side   (v) yes
(b) Is each pair the same pentomino or different? If they are the same, say whether you turn or flip it.
(i) same: turn. Turn the first one halfway around (a half turn) and it fits on the second. (ii) different. The bump is on the second square in one and on the end square in the other. No turn or flip fixes that. (iii) same: flip. Flip the first one over (like a mirror image) and it fits on the second.

One pentomino, many looks

Turn a pentomino 4 ways, then flip it and turn it 4 more ways. That is 8 ways to lay it on a grid, but it is still one pentomino. Some pentominoes look the same after a turn or a flip, so they have fewer looks.

Think The I pentomino is 5 squares in a row. How many different looks does it have?
2: lying across or standing up. A half turn or a flip gives the same picture again.
Example 6

Find all the pentominoes

Find all the different pentominoes with a systematic list.

Simplify first. With 3 squares there are only 2 shapes: 3 in a row, or a bent one. (With 4 squares there are 5. You will find them in the assignment.)

Sort by the longest straight row. Start with all 5 in a row, then 4 in a row, then 3, then 2. Before you draw a new one, check that it is not a turn or flip of one you already have. Draw each one on a grid below. The number of grids tells you how many to find.

How many pentominoes are there in all? 12
1+2+8+1=12 different pentominoes. A pentomino can never have only 1 in a row (the squares would only touch at corners), so the list is complete.

Each pentomino is named by a capital letter that it looks a bit like. Draw your 12 on the grid paper on the cut-out page, write their letters, and cut them out.

Math History1984

Tetris began as a pentomino puzzle

In 1984, Alexey Pajitnov worked at a computer lab in Moscow. He loved a store-bought pentomino puzzle and tried to turn it into a computer game.

Twelve pieces of 5 squares were too complicated for a fast game. So he simplified the problem: pieces of 4 squares. He named the game Tetris, from tetra (Greek for four) and tennis, his favourite sport.

A Tetris field is usually 10 squares wide and 20 squares tall. Each piece covers 4 squares. How many pieces fill it exactly?
Check the area: 10\times20=200 squares. 200\div4=50 pieces.

Pentomino Puzzles: Open Boxes and Rectangles

An open box is a cube with no top. It has 5 faces, so a pentomino might be a net for it (a flat pattern that folds up into the box, as in Unit 3, Lesson 10). But not every pentomino works.

Key idea   Will it fold into an open box? (draw or model)
  1. Step 1. Pick one square to be the bottom.
  2. Step 2. Fold the squares that touch it straight up: they are sides.
  3. Step 3. Fold any other squares up against the sides.
  4. Step 4. If two squares land on the same face, it does not work. Try a different bottom square before you give up.
Quick eliminations: a 2 by 2 block of squares never folds (four squares meet at one point, but only three faces of a cube meet at a corner). Five in a row wraps all the way around and overlaps.

Try it: the folding machine

Pick a pentomino. Tap a square to make it the bottom. Then press Fold it.

Why does a 2 by 2 block never fold?

Look at the point in the middle of a 2 by 2 block. Four squares meet there. At a corner of a cube, only three faces meet.

Think Why does 5 in a row never fold into an open box, no matter which square is the bottom?
A row folds up like a belt. Four squares already go all the way around, so with 5 in a row one square always lands on a face that is already used. Try the I in the folding machine.
Example 7

Will it fold into an open box?

Does each pentomino fold into an open box? Write yes or no. If yes, shade the square that becomes the bottom. Check with your cut-out pieces.
(a) X yes: the middle square is the bottom and the four arms fold up as the sides. (b) L yes: the column of 4 wraps around to make the 4 sides, and the extra square closes one end: it is the bottom. (c) U no: put the bottom in the middle of the long row. The two end squares fold up as the left and right sides, and the two tips both land on the back. They overlap and the front is missing. (d) P no: it has a 2 by 2 block, so it is eliminated right away.

Tiling a rectangle

To tile a rectangle means to cover it completely with pieces, with no gaps and no overlaps. Pieces may be turned and flipped.

Key idea   Tiling tips
  1. Check the area: each pentomino covers 5 squares.
  2. Fill a corner or an edge first, where pieces fit in fewer ways.
  3. Eliminate: if you close off a gap whose number of squares is not a multiple of 5 (5, 10, 15, …), that try cannot work. Undo the last piece and try again.
Example 8

Tile a 3 by 5 rectangle

Tile this 3 by 5 rectangle with the L, T and Y pentominoes. Draw the pieces on the rectangle and label them.
Area check: 3\times5=15 squares =3\times5, so 3 pentominoes is the right number. The T is 3 long in both directions, so it fits standing up at the right end. The L and Y are 4 long, so they must lie along the long sides: the L across the top, the Y across the bottom. Guess and check the flips until they lock together.

Example 9

Owen’s box, and a closed box

Owen says: “The P pentomino has 5 squares and an open box has 5 faces, so P must fold into an open box.”

(a) Identify and correct Owen’s error.
Having 5 squares is needed, but it is not enough: the squares must also land on 5 different faces. P has a 2 by 2 block, and when you fold it, two squares land on the same face. So P does not fold into an open box (Example 7).
(b) Create a variation: a closed box (a cube) has 6 faces. Add one square to the X pentomino to make a net of a closed cube. Describe your strategy.
X folds into an open box with the middle square as the bottom. The open top is across from the bottom, so the new square must fold over to be the lid: add it to the end of any arm (shown on top).

Key ideas

Strategies: draw or model, guess and check, systematic list, look for a pattern, simplify, eliminate, work backward, try another approach. Then check the answer follows the rules.
Tangram areas (small triangle = 1): large 4, medium 2, square 2, parallelogram 2, small 1. Whole square = 16, so a large triangle is \tfrac14 of it.
Building shapes: check the area first, place the biggest pieces first, look at the corners, guess and check (flips allowed). Reuse a puzzle you already solved.
Pentominoes: 12 of them (a turn or flip is not new). An open box needs 5 different faces: a 2 by 2 block never folds. Tiling: the area must be a multiple of 5.
