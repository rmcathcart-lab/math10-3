Puzzles and Games

Example 1

Take-away games and the Tower of Hanoi

(a) Two players take turns removing 1, 2 or 3 counters from a pile of 15. Whoever takes the last counter wins. You go first. How many should you take? What do you do after that?
Work backward. If you leave 4, the other player takes 1, 2 or 3, and you take the rest. So leaving 4 wins, and so does leaving 8 or 12: multiples of 4.
15-12=3, so take 3 and leave 12.
After that, take 4 minus what the other player took (they take 1, you take 3), so every round removes 4.
(b) Change the rule: still take 1, 2 or 3, but whoever takes the last counter loses. The pile has 18. What should the first player do now?
Now you want the other player to be left with 1 counter. Work backward: leave 1, and before that 5, 9, 13, 17 (one more than a multiple of 4).
18-17=1: take 1 and leave 17. Then take 4 minus what the other player took.
(c) In the Tower of Hanoi puzzle, you move a stack of discs to another peg, one disc at a time, never putting a bigger disc on a smaller one. Fill in the table. Then find the fewest moves for 6 discs.
Discs123456
Fewest moves137153163
Pattern: each number is double the one before, plus 1: 15\times2+1=31, 31\times2+1=63.
Rule: 2^n-1. Check: 2^6-1=64-1=63 ✓ 6 discs take 63 moves.
Powers on the TI-30XIIS: 2^6−1ENTER gives 63.

Think: in (a), what if the pile had 16? (16 is a multiple of 4, so whatever you take, the other player can leave 12. Go second if you can!)

(a) A loonie has 11 equal sides and 11 equal angles. Find the sum of its angles. Then find the size of each angle, to the nearest tenth of a degree.

True Bearings

Example 4

The float plane

A float plane is tied up at a dock, D, on a lake in northern Alberta. A is a fishing camp and B is a cabin.

(a) What is the true bearing of the camp, A, from the dock?
Measured clockwise from north: 062°.
(b) What is the true bearing of the cabin, B, from the dock?
From north clockwise to south is 180°, then 25° more: 180^\circ+25^\circ=205^\circ.
(c) The plane flies to the camp. On what bearing does it fly back to the dock?
062° is less than 180°, so add: 062^\circ+180^\circ=242^\circ.
(d) On what bearing does it fly from the cabin back to the dock?
205° is more than 180°, so subtract: 205^\circ-180^\circ=025^\circ.
(e) Sketch a bearing of 300° on the north arrow. Which referent helps?
300^\circ=360^\circ-60^\circ, so the line is 60° to the west of north (two referents of 30°, or one of 60°).

Copying and Bisecting Angles; Perpendicular Lines

Example 5

The goalie, the carpenter and a 58° angle

(a) A goalie stands on the line that bisects the angle between the puck and the two goal posts. From the puck, the lines to the two posts make a 14° angle. What angle does the goalie’s line make with each post line?
Bisect means cut into two equal angles:
14^\circ\div2=7^\circ on each side.
(b) A carpenter must copy the angle where a sloped ceiling meets a wall onto a board. Name two ways to copy the angle.
Any two: measure it with a protractor and draw the same size on the board; set a sliding T-bevel on the angle, lock it and trace it; trace it on paper; or copy it with a compass and straightedge.
(c) Bisect this 58° angle with a compass and a straightedge. Write your steps. Check with a protractor.
1. Compass point on B: draw an arc that crosses both arms, at P and Q.
2. Compass point on P: draw an arc inside the angle. Keep the same width and do the same from Q. The arcs cross at R.
3. Draw a ray from B through R. It bisects the angle.
Check: each half measures 58^\circ\div2=29^\circ.
(d) Sort each pair of lines as parallel, perpendicular or neither. Give a reason.
the two side rails of a ladderParallel: they never meet.
a wall and the floorPerpendicular: they meet at 90°.
the two straight edges of a pizza sliceNeither: they meet at the tip, but at a small angle, not 90°.
Ladder rails: parallel. Wall and floor: perpendicular. Pizza edges: neither.

Example 8

The pool table and the two streets

On a pool table, the two long cushions are parallel and the end cushions are perpendicular to them. A ball leaves the bottom cushion at 32° and rolls straight into the top right corner pocket.

(a) Find x, the angle between the ball’s path and the top cushion.
x=32^\circ: alternate interior angles are equal (the long cushions are parallel).
(b) Find y, the angle between the path and the end cushion.
The end cushion meets the top cushion at 90°. y=90^\circ-32^\circ=58^\circ (complementary to x).
(c) Find z.
z=180^\circ-32^\circ=148^\circ (supplementary: a straight line)
(d) Two streets are crossed by a diagonal road. At one corner the angle is 86°. At the other street, the corresponding angle is 88°. Are the two streets parallel? Explain.
No. If the streets were parallel, the corresponding angles would be equal. 86^\circ\ne88^\circ, so the streets are not parallel (they will meet somewhere, far away).

Key ideas

Puzzles and games. Name your strategy. Work backward from the end: leave a multiple of 4 (last one wins). Tower of Hanoi: 2^n-1 moves. A flip or a turn is not a new shape.
Angles. Estimate with referents first. Protractor: centre on the vertex, 0 on one arm, read the scale that starts at 0. Reflex = 360° − angle. Polygon sum: (n-2)\times180^\circ.
Bearings and constructions. From north, clockwise, 3 digits. Back bearing: ± 180°. Bisect: two equal halves. Perpendicular: exactly 90°. Parallel: never meet.
Partners and parallel lines. Complements add to 90°, supplements to 180°, around a point to 360°. Vertically opposite: equal. With parallel lines: F, Z and alternate exterior angles are equal; same-side angles add to 180°. Always give a reason.
