The Flashlight Bridge

Four friends finish a late shift at 1 a.m. They must cross an old footbridge in the dark.

Only 2 people fit on the bridge at once. They have one flashlight, and anyone crossing needs it, so it must be carried back each time.

Ana takes 1 min, Ben 2 min, Cam 5 min, Dee 10 min. A pair walks at the slower one’s speed.

What is the fewest minutes it takes to get all four across?

?
Stuck? Ana could walk each friend over and come back: that takes 19 min. Can you beat 19?
17 minutes! The trick: send the two slowest across together, so their slow times overlap. Ana + Ben cross (2) · Ana back (1) · Cam + Dee cross (10) · Ben back (2) · Ana + Ben cross (2): 2+1+10+2+2=17. Today: the extra money workers earn for late shifts, good service and hard jobs.

Extra Earnings: Tips, Bonuses and Shift Premiums

Many jobs pay extra money on top of the wage or salary: tips from customers, bonuses for good work, and more per hour for late shifts or hard jobs. Today we find each one and add it all up to get the gross pay.

I can…
  • find a tip and a tip percent, and estimate a tip in my head
  • share a tip pool by hours and find a tip-out
  • find a bonus: a lump sum, a percent, or an amount per item
  • find pay with a shift premium, danger pay or isolation pay
  • add every kind of pay to find the total gross pay
N2 Demonstrate an understanding of income, including: wages; salary; contracts; commissions; piecework to calculate gross pay and net pay.
Today: extra earnings that are part of gross pay.
1

Tips

Many jobs pay extra money on top of the base wage or salary. All of this extra money is part of your gross pay (your pay before deductions).

A tip (also called a gratuity) is money a customer gives to thank a worker for good service. Servers, bartenders, hairstylists, taxi and delivery drivers often earn tips. A tip is usually a percent of the bill. In Canada, 15% to 20% is common. In this booklet, the tip is found on the bill before tax.

In Alberta, a server must still be paid at least minimum wage ($15.00/h as of 2026; rates can change). The tips are extra, and they belong to the workers, not to the owner.

Key idea   Tips
tip = bill × tip rate    tip percent =\dfrac{\text{tip}}{\text{bill}}\times100
gross pay = wage pay + tips   (wage pay = hours × hourly wage)
≈
Estimate. Round the bill. Find 10% by moving the decimal one place left. Then 15% = 10% + half of 10%, and 20% = 2 × 10%.
Example 1

Dinner and a haircut

(a) A family’s dinner bill in Calgary is $64.80 before tax. Estimate a 15% tip in your head. Then find a 15% tip and a 20% tip.
Estimate: $64.80 is about $65. 10% is $6.50, half of that is $3.25.
\$6.50+\$3.25=\$9.75, so about $9.75.
15%: \$64.80\times0.15=\$9.72
20%: \$64.80\times0.20=\$12.96
(b) A customer leaves a $9.00 tip on a $52.00 haircut. What percent tip is that? Round to the nearest tenth of a percent.
tip percent =\dfrac{9.00}{52.00}\times100
=0.17307\ldots\times100\approx17.3\%
The customer tipped about 17.3%.
Tip percent in one line: 9 ÷ 52 × 100 ENTER shows 17.30769…, about 17.3%.

Wage pay plus tips

A server’s gross pay for a shift is the wage pay plus the tips. Dividing by the hours shows what the shift was really worth per hour.

Example 2

Noor’s Friday shift

Noor is a server at a restaurant in Lethbridge. She earns $15.50/h. On Friday she worked a 6-hour shift. Her customers’ bills added up to $1240.00, and her tips were 16% of the bills.

(a) Find Noor’s gross pay for the shift.
wage pay =6\times\$15.50=\$93.00
tips =\$1240.00\times0.16=\$198.40
gross pay =\$93.00+\$198.40=\$291.40
Noor earned $291.40 for the shift.
(b) How much did she earn per hour, with tips?
\$291.40\div6=\$48.566\ldots\approx\$48.57
With tips, she earned about $48.57 per hour, more than three times her wage.

Working backwards: from the tips to the bills

Sometimes you know the tips and the tip rate, and you want the total of the bills. Work backwards: divide the tips by the rate.

Key idea   Working backwards   bill = tip ÷ tip rate
$30 is 15% of the bill:  \$30\div0.15=\$200
!
Watch out. Tips change from day to day. A busy Saturday and a slow Tuesday can be very different, so workers often talk about their average tips.
Example 3

Mateo’s deliveries

Mateo delivers food in Airdrie. Last weekend he got $86.40 in tips. His tips were 12% of the orders he delivered, on average. What was the total of his orders?
orders =\$86.40\div0.12=\$720.00
(Or find 1% first: \$86.40\div12=\$7.20, then 100\%=100\times\$7.20=\$720.00.)
Check: \$720.00\times0.12=\$86.40 ✓
Mateo’s orders totalled $720.00.

Pooled Tips and Tip-Outs

In many restaurants, all the tips go into one tip pool. At the end of the shift, the pool is shared. It can be shared equally, or by hours worked, so a person who worked longer gets more. Sharing by hours is sharing in a ratio, as in Unit 1.

Servers often also give a part of their tips to the kitchen staff, dishwashers or bussers. This is called a tip-out.

Key idea   Sharing a tip pool by hours
Step 1. Add everyone’s hours to get the total hours.
Step 2. Find the share for 1 hour: pool ÷ total hours.
Step 3. Multiply each person’s hours by the share for 1 hour.
Step 4. Check: the shares add up to the pool.
Example 4

The café’s tip pool

Four servers at a café in Cochrane share a tip pool of $612.00 from Saturday.

(a) How much does each server get if they share the pool equally?
\$612.00\div4=\$153.00 each
(b) The café shares the pool by hours worked. Complete the table.
ServerMiaJonPriyaSamTotal
Hours865524
Share$204.00$153.00$127.50$127.50$612.00
Step 1: 8+6+5+5=24 h
Step 2: \$612.00\div24=\$25.50 for each hour
Step 3: Mia 8\times\$25.50=\$204.00; Jon 6\times\$25.50=\$153.00; Priya and Sam 5\times\$25.50=\$127.50 each
Step 4: \$204.00+\$153.00+\$127.50+\$127.50=\$612.00 ✓
Example 5

The bar’s tip-out

The bar at a Red Deer restaurant collects $825.00 in tips on a Friday night. First, 30% of the tips go to the kitchen staff. The rest is shared equally by the 3 bartenders. How much does each bartender get?
kitchen =\$825.00\times0.30=\$247.50
left for the bartenders =\$825.00-\$247.50=\$577.50  (or \$825.00\times0.70)
each bartender =\$577.50\div3=\$192.50
Each bartender gets $192.50.

The tip-out, step by step

Take the kitchen’s part off the top first. Then share what is left.

Bonuses

A bonus is extra money paid for good work, for reaching a goal, or for agreeing to stay with a company. A bonus can be:

A lump sumone set amount, like $500
A percentof your salary, wages or sales, like 4% of your yearly salary
Per sale or per itemlike $15 for each phone plan you sell

Year-end bonuses, signing bonuses (for joining or coming back) and referral bonuses (for bringing in a new worker) are common. A bonus is part of gross pay.

Key idea   Bonuses
percent bonus = amount × bonus rate    per-item bonus = number of items × bonus per item
Example 6

A percent and a per-item bonus

(a) Kyle is a parts manager at a car dealership. He earns $46 800 a year. His year-end bonus is 4% of his salary. Find his bonus.
4\%=0.04
bonus =\$46\,800\times0.04=\$1872.00
Kyle’s bonus is $1872.00.
(b) A phone store pays a bonus for each plan sold: $8 for a Basic plan, $15 for a Plus plan, and $30 for a Premium plan. In March, Erin sold 12 Basic, 7 Plus and 4 Premium plans. Find her bonus.
Basic: 12\times\$8=\$96
Plus: 7\times\$15=\$105
Premium: 4\times\$30=\$120
total =\$96+\$105+\$120=\$321.00
Erin’s bonus is $321.00.

Which choice is better?

When you can choose between a lump sum and a percent, work out the percent in dollars. Then compare.

Example 7

Rosa’s holiday bonus

A hotel offers its housekeepers a choice of holiday bonus: a lump sum of $750, or 3% of their pay for the year. Rosa earned $28 600 this year. Which bonus should she choose, and how much more is it?
3% bonus =\$28\,600\times0.03=\$858.00
\$858.00-\$750.00=\$108.00
Rosa should choose the 3% bonus. It is $108.00 more than the lump sum.

Shift Premiums, Danger Pay and Isolation Pay

Some hours or some jobs are harder, so employers pay extra for them.

Shift premiumextra pay per hour for evenings, nights or weekends (nurses, health-care aides, security guards, factory and warehouse workers). Paid only for the hours in that time, for example every hour after 6:00 p.m.
Danger pay(or hazard pay) extra pay for risky work, such as fighting wildfires or working on power lines.
Isolation payextra pay for working far from towns, such as at a northern mine or an oil sands camp. It can be per hour, per day or per month.

Extra pay can be a dollar amount per hour, or a percent more per hour.

Key idea   Extra pay per hour
$ per hour: premium pay = premium hours × premium per hour  (add it to the regular pay)
percent: new hourly rate = wage × (1 + rate)   e.g. 30% more: wage × 1.30
!
Watch out. A shift premium is paid only on the hours inside the premium time, not on the whole shift. Count those hours carefully on the timesheet.
Example 8

Sana’s evening premium

Sana is a health-care aide at a seniors’ home in Calgary. She earns $21.60/h, plus an evening premium of $2.50/h for every hour she works after 6:00 p.m. Her breaks are paid. Complete her timesheet and find her gross pay for the week.

No day is over 8 h and the week is under 44 h, so there is no overtime.
regular pay =31\times\$21.60=\$669.60
premium pay =14.5\times\$2.50=\$36.25
gross pay =\$669.60+\$36.25=\$705.85. Sana’s gross pay is $705.85.

Only the premium hours get the premium

A common mistake is to add the premium to the wage for every hour of the shift.

A percent more per hour

“30% more per hour” means the new rate is the wage plus 30% of the wage: wage × 1.30. The danger pay is the difference between the new pay and the regular pay.

Example 9

The fire line and the mine camp

(a) A wildfire crew member earns $24.00/h. For hours on the fire line, she gets danger pay of 30% more per hour. How much does she earn for an 8-hour day on the fire line? How much of that is danger pay?
new rate =\$24.00\times1.30=\$31.20/h
day’s pay =8\times\$31.20=\$249.60
without danger pay: 8\times\$24.00=\$192.00
danger pay =\$249.60-\$192.00=\$57.60
She earns $249.60; $57.60 of it is danger pay.
(b) A cook at a northern mine camp earns $5850 a month. He can choose isolation pay of $650 a month, or 10% of his monthly pay. His contract is 4 months long. Which choice pays more, and by how much?
flat: 4\times\$650=\$2600.00
10%: \$5850\times0.10=\$585.00 a month;
4\times\$585.00=\$2340.00
\$2600.00-\$2340.00=\$260.00
The $650 a month choice pays $260.00 more.

Putting It All Together: Total Gross Pay

ExtraJobs that often get itGood for the workerNot so good
Tipsservers, bartenders, stylists, driversgood service can mean a lot more moneytips change from day to day; slow days pay less
Bonussales, office jobs, trades, hotelsa reward for working hard or stayingoften not promised; can change each year
Shift premiumnurses, aides, guards, factory workmore pay per hourlate hours are hard on sleep and family time
Danger or isolation payfirefighting, power lines, mines, campsmuch higher payrisky, or far from home for weeks

To find total gross pay, find each kind of pay on its own, then add. A table keeps it organized.

Key idea   Total gross pay
Step 1: Regular pay = regular hours × wage. Overtime pay = overtime hours × 1.5 × wage (Lesson 3).
Step 2: Add each extra: premiums, tips, bonuses.
Step 3: Add everything to get the gross pay.
!
Watch out. Overtime is 1.5 times the base wage. A shift premium in dollars per hour is added on top, the same amount for every premium hour (overtime or not), unless the question says something else.
Example 10

Diego’s pay summary

Diego is a cook at a hotel in Banff. He earns $19.00/h. This week he worked 46 hours, and never more than 8 hours in a day. He gets a night premium of $1.50/h, and 20 of his hours were night hours. His share of the kitchen tip-out was $142.75. Complete his pay summary.

Type of payWorkAmount
Regular pay44\times\$19.00$836.00
Overtime pay2\times\$28.50$57.00
Night premium20\times\$1.50$30.00
Tip-out share$142.75
Gross pay$1065.75
No day is over 8 h, so use the weekly rule: 46-44=2 h of overtime, and 44 regular hours.
overtime rate =1.5\times\$19.00=\$28.50/h
gross pay =\$836.00+\$57.00+\$30.00+\$142.75=\$1065.75. Diego’s gross pay is $1065.75.
✓

Key ideas

Tip = bill × rate. Tip percent = tip ÷ bill × 100. Estimate: 15% = 10% + half of 10%; 20% = 2 × 10%. Gross pay = wage pay + tips.
Tip pool by hours: pool ÷ total hours = share for 1 hour; × each person’s hours; check the shares add to the pool. A tip-out comes off the top first.
Bonus: a lump sum, a percent (amount × rate) or per item (items × bonus). To choose, find both in dollars and compare.
Premiums: only the premium hours × premium per hour. Percent more: wage × (1 + rate). Total gross pay: find each part on its own, then add.
