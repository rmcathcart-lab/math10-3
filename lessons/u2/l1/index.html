When Do the Hands Meet?

At 12:00 noon, the hour hand and the minute hand point the same way. One is right on top of the other.

Then the clock keeps ticking, all afternoon and evening.

From noon until just before midnight, how many times are the two hands right on top of each other?

?
Most people say 12. Are they right?
Only 11 times! At 12:00, then about 1:05, 2:11, 3:16, 4:22, 5:27, 6:33, 7:38, 8:44, 9:49 and 10:55. There is no meeting in the 11 o’clock hour: the next one is exactly at 12:00 midnight. The hands meet about every 1 h 5 min (a bit more, really). In a whole day they meet 22 times. Today is all about hours and minutes.

Time Math: Hours, Minutes and Timesheets

Pay is worked out in hours, but clocks show hours and minutes. Before we work out any pay, we review how to write minutes as part of an hour, add and subtract times, read both kinds of clocks, find elapsed time, and total the hours on a timesheet.

I can…
  • change minutes to decimal hours and back (7 h 45 min = 7.75 h)
  • write a time as a mixed number of hours, and change an improper fraction of hours to a mixed number
  • add and subtract hours and minutes, regrouping at 60
  • change between the 12-hour and 24-hour clock
  • find elapsed time by counting up, even past midnight
  • complete a timesheet with unpaid breaks and total the hours

Parts of an Hour: Fractions and Decimals

Pay is worked out in hours, but clocks show hours and minutes. So you need to write minutes as part of an hour. There are 60 minutes in 1 hour, so 15 min is \tfrac{15}{60}=\tfrac14 of an hour.

The shaded part is 45 min. It is 3 of the 4 quarters of the clock, so

45\text{ min}=\tfrac{45}{60}\text{ h}=\tfrac34\text{ h}=0.75\text{ h}

The same amount of time can be written three ways:

7 h 45 min=7\tfrac34\text{ h}=7.75 h

Minutes → decimal hours: divide the minutes by 60, then add the whole hours.7 h 45 min: 45\div60=0.75, so 7.75 h
Decimal hours → minutes: multiply only the decimal part by 60.3.4 h: 0.4\times60=24, so 3 h 24 min
Benchmarks: 15 min =\tfrac14\text{ h}=0.25\text{ h} 30 min =\tfrac12\text{ h}=0.5\text{ h} 45 min =\tfrac34\text{ h}=0.75\text{ h}

Math Historyc. 2000 BCE – 1795

Why does an hour have 60 minutes?

About 4000 years ago, the Sumerians and then the Babylonians (in today’s Iraq) counted in groups of 60. Nobody knows for sure why. But 60 splits evenly into 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20 and 30 equal parts. That is why 15, 20 and 30 minutes are such friendly fractions of an hour.

Astronomers kept using 60s. In about 150 CE, Ptolemy split each degree into 60 small parts, and each of those into 60 again: our minutes and seconds. In 1793, France tried a day of 10 hours, each with 100 minutes. People didn’t like it, and it was dropped in 1795.

A French decimal day had 10\times100=1000 minutes. Our day has 24\times60 minutes. Which minute was longer?
Our day has 24\times60=1440 minutes. The same day cut into only 1000 pieces gives bigger pieces: one French minute was 1440\div1000=1.44 of our minutes (about 1 min 26 s).

Changing between hours and minutes and decimal hours

!
The digits after a decimal point are not minutes. 7.30 h is not 7 h 30 min. 7 h 30 min =7.5 h, and 7.30 h = 7 h 18 min (because 0.3\times60=18).
On the TI-30XIIS: for 5 h 50 min, key 50÷60+5ENTER. The screen shows 5.833333333. The calculator divides before it adds, so you don’t need brackets.

Mixed numbers and improper fractions of an hour

Time is sometimes written as a fraction of an hour. A mixed number, like 2\tfrac34, has a whole number and a fraction. An improper fraction, like \tfrac{11}{4}, has a top (numerator) that is bigger than or equal to its bottom (denominator).

Improper fraction → mixed number: divide the top by the bottom. The answer is the whole number; the remainder goes over the bottom. Then simplify the fraction if you can.\tfrac94: 9\div4=2 remainder 1, so \tfrac94=2\tfrac14
Mixed number → improper fraction: multiply the whole number by the bottom, add the top, keep the bottom.1\tfrac12=\tfrac{1\times2+1}{2}=\tfrac32 (an hour and a half is 3 half-hours)

Adding and Subtracting Hours and Minutes

Add hours to hours and minutes to minutes. An hour has 60 minutes, not 100, so you regroup at 60.

Adding: add the hours, add the minutes. If there are 60 min or more, trade 60 min for 1 h.7 h 45 min + 6 h 30 min = 13 h 75 min = 14 h 15 min
Subtracting: if there are not enough minutes on top, borrow 1 h and add 60 min.5 h 10 min − 2 h 40 min → 4 h 70 min − 2 h 40 min = 2 h 30 min
!
Don’t type 7.45 + 6.30 into your calculator. 7 h 45 min is not 7.45 h. Either add the hours and minutes by hand, or change each time to decimal hours first (7.75+6.5=14.25 h).

The 12-Hour and 24-Hour Clock

The 12-hour clock counts from 12 to 12 twice a day. a.m. times run from midnight to noon. p.m. times run from noon to midnight. 12:00 a.m. is midnight and 12:00 p.m. is noon.

The 24-hour clock counts the hours from 00 to 23 and has no a.m. or p.m. It always uses two digits for the hour: 07:30 is 7:30 a.m. and 19:30 is 7:30 p.m. Hospitals, airlines, the military, transit schedules and many work schedules use it.

12-hour → 24-hour: a.m.: keep the hour (write 2 digits): 9:15 a.m. = 09:15. p.m.: add 12 to the hour: 4:20 p.m. = 16:20.The two 12 o’clocks are different: 12:40 a.m. = 00:40 (just after midnight), 12:40 p.m. = 12:40 (just after noon)
24-hour → 12-hour: hours 13 to 23: subtract 12, p.m. Hour 12: p.m. Hour 00: write 12, a.m. Hours 01 to 11: a.m.18:05 = 6:05 p.m. 00:30 = 12:30 a.m.

Elapsed Time

Elapsed time is how much time passes from a start time to an end time. You can’t just subtract the clock times on a calculator, because the minutes go up to 60, not 100. Instead, count up from the start time in friendly jumps.

Count up: ① jump to the next whole hour ② jump the whole hours ③ jump the minutes left ④ add the jumps

From 8:40 a.m. to 1:25 p.m.: 20\text{ min}+4\text{ h}+25\text{ min}= 4 h 45 min.

Or use the 24-hour clock and subtract:13:25 − 08:40 → borrow 1 h: 12:85 − 08:40 = 4 h 45 min
Across midnight: find the time up to midnight, then add the time after midnight.10:00 p.m. → 2:00 a.m.: 2 h + 2 h = 4 h
!
The time from 8:50 to 10:15 is not 10.15-8.50=1.65. There are 60 minutes in an hour, so count up: 10 min + 1 h + 15 min = 1 h 25 min.

Night shifts and bus trips

(a) A security guard works overnight from 10:45 p.m. to 7:15 a.m. How long is his shift? Give the answer in hours and minutes and in decimal hours.
10:45 p.m. → 11:00 p.m.: 15 min
11:00 p.m. → 7:00 a.m.: 8 h (1 h to midnight + 7 h)
7:00 a.m. → 7:15 a.m.: 15 min
total: 8 h 30 min = 8.5 h
His shift is 8 h 30 min, or 8.5 h.
(b) Farah takes the bus from Calgary to Edmonton. It leaves at 13:50 and the trip takes 3 h 35 min. When does it arrive? Give the time in both clocks.
13 h 50 min + 3 h 35 min = 16 h 85 min
85 min = 1 h 25 min, so 17 h 25 min
The bus arrives at 17:25, which is 5:25 p.m.

Timesheets and Unpaid Breaks

A timesheet (or time card) records when you start work (in) and when you stop (out) each day. Your employer uses it to work out your pay, so it must be right. You are not paid for an unpaid break, such as a 30 min lunch, so subtract it. Some jobs have a split shift: two blocks of work in one day, with time off in between. Find the time for each block, then add.

Hours worked in a day = (time from in to out) - unpaid break
Hours for the week: add the days in hours and minutes. Change the total to decimal hours at the end.
i
Did you know? In Alberta, a worker must get a break of at least 30 min during a shift of more than 5 hours in a row. The employer decides whether the break is paid. (Rules as of 2026; rules can change.)
Example 8

Hours worked

(a) Priya works at a grocery store from 8:30 a.m. to 5:15 p.m. She takes a 30 min unpaid lunch. How many hours does she work? Give the answer in hours and minutes and in decimal hours.
8:30 a.m. → 9:00 a.m.: 30 min; 9:00 a.m. → 5:00 p.m.: 8 h; 5:00 p.m. → 5:15 p.m.: 15 min. Total: 8 h 45 min
Subtract the break: 8 h 45 min − 30 min = 8 h 15 min
15\div60=0.25, so Priya works 8 h 15 min = 8.25 h.
(b) Mr. Dhillon drives a school bus. He works a split shift: 6:45 a.m. to 9:10 a.m., and 2:50 p.m. to 5:05 p.m. How many hours does he work? Round decimal hours to 2 places.
Morning: 6:45 → 7:00 is 15 min, 7:00 → 9:00 is 2 h, 9:00 → 9:10 is 10 min: 2 h 25 min
Afternoon: 10 min + 2 h + 5 min = 2 h 15 min
Total: 4 h 40 min =4\tfrac23 h (40\div60=0.666\ldots)
He works about 4.67 h.

✓

Key ideas

60 minutes = 1 hour. Minutes → decimal hours: ÷ 60 (7 h 45 min = 7.75 h). Decimal hours → minutes: decimal part × 60 (3.4 h = 3 h 24 min). 7.30 h is not 7 h 30 min.
Add or subtract hours with hours and minutes with minutes. Regroup at 60: 75 min = 1 h 15 min; borrow 1 h = 60 min.
24-hour clock: p.m. → add 12 (4:20 p.m. = 16:20). 13 to 23 → subtract 12, p.m. Midnight is 00:00; 12:40 a.m. = 00:40.
Elapsed time: count up (next hour, whole hours, minutes left). Timesheet: in to out, minus the unpaid break; total in hours and minutes, then change to decimal hours at the end.
