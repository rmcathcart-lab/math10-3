Trees Along the Path

A tree planter plants spruce seedlings along a straight path that is 20 m long.

She plants one at the very start, then one every 2 m. The last one is at the very end.

How many seedlings does she plant?

?
Got a number? Test your idea on a tiny path first: 4 m long.
11 seedlings, not 10! 20\div2=10 gives the number of gaps. There is always one more seedling than gaps. On a 4 m path: seedlings at 0, 2 and 4 m make 3 seedlings and 2 gaps. Twist: around a circle 20 m long, the start and the end are the same spot, so it is just 10.

Piecework: Paid for Each Piece

People earn money in many ways. You already know two: an hourly wage (paid for each hour) and a salary (a set amount per year).

MethodYou are paid …Jobs that often use it
Hourly wagefor each hour you workcashier, cook, labourer, lifeguard
Salarya set amount per year, split into pay periodsteacher, nurse, office manager
Pieceworkfor each piece or task you finishtree planter, fruit picker, flyer carrier, sewing worker
Contractone set price for a whole jobhouse painter, roofer, landscaper, web designer
Commission (Lesson 5)a percent of what you sellcar salesperson, real estate agent
Tips and bonuses (Lesson 6)extra money on top of your payserver, hairstylist, hotel staff

With piecework, you are paid a set amount, the piece rate, for each item you make or each task you finish. The more pieces you finish, the more you earn.

Key idea   Piecework pay = piece rate × number of pieces
2 350 seedlings at $0.16 each: 2\,350\times\$0.16=\$376.00
2350 × 0.16 ENTER shows 376. Write it as money: $376.00. Estimate to check: about 2 000 trees at about 20¢ is about $400.

What is piecework worth per hour?

To compare piecework with an hourly job, divide the pay by the hours worked. This is the effective hourly rate: what the pay works out to for each hour (like the hourly equivalent of a salary in Lesson 2).

Key idea   Effective hourly rate = pay ÷ hours worked
$
Minimum wage. In Alberta, most employees who are paid by the piece must still earn at least the minimum wage, $15.00 per hour, for the hours they work (as of 2026; rates can change). If the piecework pay is less, the employer must add the difference.
Think A planter earns $0.16 per tree. About how many trees an hour must she plant to earn the $15.00 minimum wage?
\$15.00\div\$0.16=93.75, so about 94 trees an hour. Fast planters do far more than that.

Working Backwards with Piecework

Sometimes you know the pay and want the piece rate, or you know the pay you want and need the number of pieces. The formula can be turned around.

Key idea   pay = rate × pieces   ·   rate = pay ÷ pieces   ·   pieces = pay ÷ rate
Check by multiplying: rate × pieces should give the pay.
!
Watch out. When you find how many pieces you need to reach a goal, round up. You cannot get paid for part of a piece, and rounding down leaves you short of your goal.
120 ÷ 4.25 ENTER shows 28.235…, so 29 flats.
Example 4

Work backwards

(a) A florist is paid $382.50 to make 30 table centrepieces for a wedding. How much is she paid for each one?
rate = pay ÷ pieces
\$382.50\div30=\$12.75
Check: 30\times\$12.75=\$382.50 ✓
She is paid $12.75 per centrepiece.
(b) Tomas translates documents for $0.12 per word. He was paid $258.00 for one document. How many words long was it?
pieces = pay ÷ rate
258.00\div0.12=2\,150
Check: 2\,150\times\$0.12=\$258.00 ✓
The document was 2 150 words long.
(c) Sana picks strawberries for $4.25 per flat. How many flats must she pick to earn at least $120.00?
120.00\div4.25=28.23\ldots
28 flats: 28\times\$4.25=\$119.00 (not enough)
29 flats: 29\times\$4.25=\$123.25
She must pick 29 flats.

The employer’s side

An employer who pays by the piece then sells the pieces for more. The difference pays for the employer’s other costs (land, tools, rent) and profit.

Follow one basket of raspberries: where does the $9.00 go?

Example 5

The berry farm

A berry farm near Red Deer pays pickers $3.50 for each basket of raspberries. The farm sells each basket at its market stand for $9.00. One day the pickers fill 240 baskets, and the farm sells them all.

(a) How much does the farm pay the pickers?
240\times\$3.50=\$840.00
The farm pays the pickers $840.00.
(b) How much money does the farm take in, and how much is left after paying the pickers?
Takes in: 240\times\$9.00=\$2\,160.00
Left: \$2\,160.00-\$840.00=\$1\,320.00
(This pays the farm’s other costs and profit.)
(c) What percent of the selling price goes to the picker? Round to one decimal place.
3.50\div9.00\times100=38.88\ldots
About 38.9% of the price goes to the picker.

Contracts: One Price for the Whole Job

A contract is an agreement to do a whole job for one set price, no matter how long it takes. The person who does the job is a contractor. Before the job, a contractor gives the customer a bid (or quote): the price they will charge.

Out of the contract price, the contractor usually pays for the materials (paint, lumber, parts) and for any helpers. What is left is what the contractor really earns.

Key idea
  • Contractor’s earnings = contract price − materials − helpers’ wages − other costs
  • Effective hourly rate = contractor’s earnings ÷ hours the contractor worked
  • Helpers’ wages = number of helpers × hours × hourly wage
!
Watch out. Subtract all the costs before you divide by the hours. The contract price is not all yours.
Use brackets: ( 2400 − 465.80 ) ÷ 38 ENTER. Or find the earnings first, press ENTER, then ÷ 38 ENTER.
Example 6

Darnell, the house painter

Darnell is a house painter. He agrees to paint the inside of a house in Cochrane for $2 400.00. He buys the paint and supplies for $465.80. He does all the work himself.

(a) The job takes Darnell 38 hours. How much does he earn, and what is his effective hourly rate?
Earnings: \$2\,400.00-\$465.80=\$1\,934.20
Per hour: \$1\,934.20\div38=\$50.90
He earns $1 934.20, or $50.90 per hour.
(b) Suppose the walls need extra repairs, and the job takes 52 hours instead. The price stays the same. What is his effective hourly rate now?
\$1\,934.20\div52=37.196\ldots\approx\$37.20 per hour
He earns the same $1 934.20, but only $37.20 per hour, because the job took longer.

Setting a contract price

To make a bid, the contractor adds up all the costs and the amount they want to earn. Some contractors instead add a percent to their costs for their profit, the same way a store adds a markup (Unit 1).

Key idea   Contract price = materials + helpers’ wages + what the contractor wants to earn
or  contract price = costs × (1 + profit rate)    e.g. costs plus 30%: costs × 1.30
Example 7

Amara’s patio bid

Amara runs a small landscaping business in Okotoks. She is bidding on a job to build a stone patio.

  • Pavers, gravel and sand cost $1 180.00.
  • She will hire 2 helpers for 12 hours each at $19.50 per hour.
  • She wants to earn $600.00 for herself.
(a) What should Amara charge for the patio?
Helpers: 2\times12\times\$19.50=\$468.00
Price: \$1\,180.00+\$468.00+\$600.00=\$2\,248.00
Amara should charge $2 248.00. (Many contractors round a bid, for example to $2 250.00.)
(b) Amara’s friend says, “Just charge your costs plus 30%.” What price would that give? How much would Amara earn for herself?
Costs: \$1\,180.00+\$468.00=\$1\,648.00
Price: \$1\,648.00\times1.30=\$2\,142.40
She would earn \$2\,142.40-\$1\,648.00=\$494.40, which is less than the $600.00 she wants.

Which Is Better? Comparing Ways to Earn

Each way of being paid has good points (advantages) and bad points (disadvantages) for the worker.

MethodAdvantagesDisadvantages
Hourly wagePaid for every hour, even on slow days. Overtime pay for long days and weeks.Working faster does not earn more money.
PieceworkFast, skilled workers earn more. You can see your pay grow as you work.Pay drops on slow days (bad weather, learning a new job). Rushing can lead to mistakes or injuries.
ContractIf you work well and fast, you can earn a lot per hour. You are your own boss and choose your jobs.If the job takes longer or costs more, you earn less. You pay for materials, tools and helpers. No minimum wage, overtime, paid vacation or benefits. You must keep finding new jobs.

To compare a contract with an hourly job, find what each pays for the same number of hours. Ask: “What if the job takes longer?”

Example 8

Jaxon’s two offers

Jaxon installs drywall. A builder offers him two ways to be paid for finishing a basement. The work is spread over two weeks, so there is no overtime.

Option A: $26.50 per hour.
Option B: a contract for $1 400.00. Jaxon must buy the tape and mud, about $95.00.
(a) Jaxon thinks the job will take 40 hours. Which option pays more, and by how much?
A: 40\times\$26.50=\$1\,060.00
B: \$1\,400.00-\$95.00=\$1\,305.00
B pays more, by \$1\,305.00-\$1\,060.00=\$245.00.
(b) What if the job takes 55 hours? Which option pays more now?
A: 55\times\$26.50=\$1\,457.50
B: still $1 305.00
A pays more, by \$1\,457.50-\$1\,305.00=\$152.50.
(c) For about how many hours do the two options pay the same? Round to one decimal place.
hours = pay ÷ rate
\$1\,305.00\div\$26.50=49.24\ldots
About 49.2 hours. If the job takes less time, the contract is better; if it takes more, the hourly wage is better.
Example 9

Kiana’s choice

Kiana is choosing between two jobs at a sewing company. As a beginner, Kiana can sew about 5 bags an hour. After a few months of practice, she expects to sew about 9 bags an hour.

Job 1: $16.50 per hour.
Job 2: piecework, $2.40 for each bag sewn (the company tops up to the $15.00 minimum wage if needed).
(a) Find what Job 2 pays per hour for a beginner and after practice.
Beginner: 5\times\$2.40=\$12.00 per hour, which is topped up to $15.00
After practice: 9\times\$2.40=\$21.60 per hour
(b) Finish the sentences.

As a beginner, the better job is Job 1 because $16.50 is more than $15.00.

After practice, the better job is Job 2 because $21.60 is more than $16.50.

One disadvantage of Job 2 is her pay drops if she is slow, sick or tired, or the work is harder, and rushing to sew faster can cause mistakes or injuries.

Job 1 for a beginner ($16.50 > $15.00); Job 2 after practice ($21.60 > $16.50).

Kiana: when does Job 2 start to win?

Kiana gets faster a little at a time. Check every speed from 5 to 9 bags an hour.

Key ideas

Piecework pay = piece rate × pieces. Different pieces: find each pay, then add. Estimate to check.
Effective hourly rate = pay ÷ hours. If it is under the $15.00 minimum wage, the employer adds the difference. Backwards: rate = pay ÷ pieces; pieces = pay ÷ rate (round up for a goal).
Contracts: earnings = price − materials − helpers − other costs. Subtract before you divide by the hours. A bid = costs + what you want to earn, or costs × (1 + profit rate).
Compare for the same number of hours. A contract wins if the job is fast; an hourly wage wins if it takes longer.
