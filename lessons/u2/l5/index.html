Up 10%, Down 10%

A jacket at a store in Calgary costs $100.

On Monday, the store raises the price by 10%.

On Friday, the jacket goes on sale: 10% off the new price.

What does the jacket cost now? More than $100, less than $100, or exactly $100?

?
Got a guess? Check it: find the Monday price first, then take 10% off that.
It costs $99, less than $100! Monday: 10% of $100 is $10, so the price goes up to $110. Friday: 10% of $110 is $11, so the price drops to \$110-\$11=\$99. The 10% off is taken from a bigger number, so it takes away more than the $10 that was added. A percent is always a percent of something. Today: commission, a percent of what you sell.

1

Straight Commission

Some jobs pay you for what you sell, not for your time. A commission is money you earn as a percent of your sales. The percent is the commission rate. Your sales are the total dollar value of everything you sold.

If commission is your only pay, it is called straight commission. Jobs that often pay this way: real estate agents, car and RV salespeople, and some furniture, appliance and travel sales jobs.

Key idea   Commission = sales × commission rate  (change the rate to a decimal first)
5% commission on $2000 of sales:  \$2000\times0.05=\$100
!
Watch out. Write the rate as a decimal carefully: 6\%=0.06, 2.5\%=0.025, 12\%=0.12. A commission is a part of the sales, so it should be much smaller than the sales.
≈
Estimate. Find 10% first (move the decimal one place left). 5% is half of that. For 6%, the answer is a bit more than the 5% amount.

Percent to decimal: divide by 100

“Percent” means “out of 100”. To change a percent to a decimal, divide by 100: the decimal point hops two places left. Fill any empty place with a zero.

Think Kai’s rate is only 2.5%, but he can earn almost $1000 on one sale. How can a small rate give a big commission?
The sales are big. A travel trailer costs tens of thousands of dollars, so even a small part of it is a lot of money.
Example 1

Maya sells appliances

Maya sells appliances (fridges, stoves, washers) at a store in Calgary. She earns 6% commission. Last week she sold $7385.00 worth of appliances.

(a) Estimate her commission.
10% of $7385 is about $740.
5% is half of that, about $370.
6% is a bit more: about $440.
(b) Calculate her commission.
6\%=0.06
commission =\$7385.00\times0.06=\$443.10
Maya earned $443.10 in commission. (Close to the estimate ✓)
7385 × 0.06 ENTER shows 443.1. Write it as money: $443.10.
Example 2

A trailer and a week of shoes

(a) Kai sells trailers at an RV lot in Airdrie. He earns 2.5% of the selling price. He sells a travel trailer for $38 900.00. What is his commission?
2.5\%=0.025
commission =\$38\,900.00\times0.025=\$972.50
Kai earns $972.50 on the trailer.
(b) Bianca works in a shoe store. She earns 8% commission. Her sales this week were $1249.99, $869.50 and $2380.00. What is her commission for the week?
total sales =\$1249.99+\$869.50+\$2380.00=\$4499.49
commission =\$4499.49\times0.08=\$359.9592
\approx\$359.96
Bianca earns $359.96 in commission.
Add first with brackets: ( 1249.99 + 869.50 + 2380 ) × 0.08 ENTER shows 359.9592.
2

Salary Plus Commission

Straight commission is risky: in a slow week you might earn very little. Many sales jobs pay a base pay that you always get, plus a commission. The base pay can be a salary (a set amount each pay period) or an hourly wage (hours × rate, as in Lesson 2).

Key idea   Salary plus commission
gross pay = base pay + commission  =  base pay + (sales × commission rate)
Base pay is a salary, or hours worked × hourly wage.
!
Watch out. Find the commission first, then add the base pay. Do not take the percent of the base pay.

Percent of what? The sales, not the base pay

The commission is a reward for selling, so it is a percent of the sales. Taking the percent of the base pay gives a tiny number that has nothing to do with what was sold.

Example 3

Phones and mattresses

(a) Jordan sells cell phone plans. He earns $450.00 a week plus 4% of his sales. This week he sold $7325.00. Find his gross pay.
commission =\$7325.00\times0.04=\$293.00
gross pay =\$450.00+\$293.00=\$743.00
Jordan’s gross pay is $743.00.
(b) Priya works at a mattress store in Lethbridge. She earns $15.50/h plus 2.5% commission. Last week she worked 32 h and sold $18 640.00. Find her gross pay.
wage: 32\times\$15.50=\$496.00
commission: \$18\,640.00\times0.025=\$466.00
gross pay =\$496.00+\$466.00=\$962.00
Priya’s gross pay is $962.00.

Graduated Commission and Quotas

Some employers pay a higher rate when you sell more. In a graduated commission (also called a tiered commission), the sales are split into levels, and each level has its own rate. For example: 3% on the first $10 000 of sales, and 5% on the amount over $10 000.

Key idea   Graduated commission
Step 1: Split the sales into the levels.   Step 2: Find the commission for each level.   Step 3: Add.
!
Watch out. The higher rate is only for the part of the sales above the level, not for all the sales.
Example 4

Lucas sells furniture

Lucas sells furniture. He earns 3% on the first $10 000 of his monthly sales and 5% on sales over $10 000. In March he sold $16 400. Complete the table to find his commission.

LevelSales in this levelRateCommission
First $10 000$10 0003%\$10\,000\times0.03=\$300.00
Over $10 000\$16\,400-\$10\,000=\$64005%\$6400\times0.05=\$320.00
Total commission\$300.00+\$320.00=\$620.00
Lucas earned $620.00 in commission in March. (Using 5% on all $16 400 would wrongly give $820.00.)
Do it in one line with brackets: 10000 × 0.03 + ( 16400 − 10000 ) × 0.05 ENTER shows 620.

Quotas

A quota is a sales target. Some jobs pay commission only on the sales over the quota. Sell less than the quota and you get only your base pay.

Think Why might a radio station pay commission only on sales over a quota?
The salary already pays for a normal month of sales. The commission is a reward for selling more than expected, so it pushes the worker to go past the target.
Example 5

Hana sells radio ads

Hana sells advertising for a radio station in Red Deer. She earns $2400.00 a month plus 6% of her sales over a monthly quota of $20 000.

(a) Find her gross pay in a month when she sells $27 850.
sales over quota =\$27\,850-\$20\,000=\$7850
commission =\$7850\times0.06=\$471.00
gross pay =\$2400.00+\$471.00=\$2871.00
Hana earns $2871.00 that month.
(b) Find her gross pay in a month when she sells $18 500.
$18 500 is less than the $20 000 quota, so there are no sales over the quota.
commission = $0
Hana earns only her salary, $2400.00.

Finding the Rate or the Sales

Sometimes you know the commission and want the rate or the sales. Use the same formula, worked backwards. (Lesson 8 does more of this.)

Key idea   Working backwards
commission rate =\dfrac{\text{commission}}{\text{sales}}\times100 (as a percent)    sales =\dfrac{\text{commission}}{\text{rate as a decimal}}
Check: sales × rate should give the commission.
The rate as a percent in one line: 378 ÷ 8400 × 100 ENTER shows 4.5.
Example 6

Work it backwards

(a) Ethan earned $378.00 in commission on sales of $8400.00. What is his commission rate?
rate =\$378.00\div\$8400.00=0.045
0.045\times100=4.5\%
Check: \$8400\times0.045=\$378 ✓
His commission rate is 4.5%.
(b) A band’s booking agent keeps 15% of what the band is paid for each show. Last summer the agent earned $1620.00 from the band’s shows. How much was the band paid in all?
15\%=0.15
sales =\$1620.00\div0.15=\$10\,800.00
Check: \$10\,800\times0.15=\$1620 ✓
The band was paid $10 800.00.
5

Comparing Pay Plans

Which pays more: a steady salary or a plan with commission? It depends on how much you sell. Make a table of the pay at a few sales amounts, then look for where the plans pay the same.

Key idea   Where two plans pay the same (salary only vs. smaller salary + commission)
sales needed = (difference in base pay) ÷ (commission rate as a decimal)
Sell more than this and the commission plan pays more. Sell less and the salary pays more.

Why difference ÷ rate? Job B catches up

Job A starts $1300 ahead. Every $1000 that Ava sells gives Job B 5% of $1000 = $50 more. Count how many $50 steps it takes to close the $1300 gap.

Example 7

Ava’s two job offers

Ava has two job offers selling windows and doors for a home renovation company.

Job A: $2800 a month salary.
Job B: $1500 a month plus 5% of her sales.
(a) Complete the table.
Monthly sales$10 000$20 000$30 000$40 000
Job A pay$2800$2800$2800$2800
Job B pay$2000$2500$3000$3500
Job B at $10 000: \$1500+\$10\,000\times0.05=\$1500+\$500=\$2000. The others the same way: +$1000, +$1500, +$2000.
(b) At what monthly sales do the two jobs pay the same?
Job A pays \$2800-\$1500=\$1300 more in base pay.
Job B catches up when 5% of sales = $1300.
sales =\$1300\div0.05=\$26\,000
Check: \$1500+\$26\,000\times0.05=\$2800 ✓
They pay the same at $26 000 a month.
(c) Ava thinks she can sell about $24 000 a month. Which job should she take? Give a reason that is not only the pay.
$24 000 is less than $26 000, so Job A pays more (B: \$1500+\$1200=\$2700).
Sample reason: Job A is the same every month, so she can plan rent and bills. (If she expects her sales to grow, Job B could pay more later.)

Key ideas

Commission = sales × rate (rate as a decimal: 6% = 0.06, 2.5% = 0.025). Estimate with 10% and 5% first. The commission is much smaller than the sales.
Gross pay = base pay + commission. Base pay is a salary or hours × wage. Take the percent of the sales, never of the base pay.
Levels: split the sales, find each level’s commission, add. The higher rate is only for the part over the level. Quota: commission only on sales over the target.
Backwards: rate = commission ÷ sales × 100; sales = commission ÷ rate. Compare plans: they pay the same at (difference in base pay) ÷ rate.
