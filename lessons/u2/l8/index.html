At the Calgary Stampede, you buy a bag of mini-donuts. On the way to your seat, you meet 3 friends.

Each friend takes half of the donuts in your bag, plus 1 more. When you sit down, 1 donut is left.

How many mini-donuts were in the bag?

Working Backwards: How Many Hours Do I Need?

“I want $400. How many hours is that?” Today we turn a pay formula around to find the hours, the wage or the sales you need. Then we check by putting the answer back in.

I can…
  • rearrange pay = rate × hours to find the hours or the rate, and check my answer
  • round up to whole hours or whole shifts when I need at least an amount
  • find the overtime hours I need, and find a wage from paid hours
  • find the sales needed to earn a commission goal
  • find the hours needed to take home an amount after deductions

Turning the Pay Formula Around

You know how to find gross pay: multiply the hourly wage (the rate) by the hours worked. But often you know the pay you want, and you need something else: “I want $400. How many hours is that?”

This is working backwards. You rearrange the formula. That means you write it a new way, so the thing you want to find is by itself on one side of the equal sign.

Key idea   The pay formula, three ways
To find the pay:pay = rate × hours To find the hours:hours = pay ÷ rate To find the rate (wage):rate = pay ÷ hours
Check: put your answer back into pay = rate × hours. You should get the pay you started with.
!
Watch out. Divide the pay by the rate, not the rate by the pay. Estimate first: $520 at about $16 an hour is about 30 hours. If your calculator says 0.03 hours, you divided the wrong way.

Cover the one you want

The formula triangle holds all three formulas. Cover the thing you want to find; what is left tells you what to do.

Which way do I divide?

Pay ÷ rate gives hours. Rate ÷ pay gives a tiny number that is not the hours. An estimate tells you right away which one makes sense.

Think Jonas gets a raise to $20.00 an hour. Does he need more or fewer hours to earn $520.00? How many?
Fewer. Each hour pays more, so it takes fewer hours: \$520.00\div\$20.00=26 hours.
Example 1

Find the wage, find the hours

(a) Marco earned $446.25 for 25.5 hours of work at a garden centre. What is his hourly wage?
rate = pay ÷ hours
rate =\$446.25\div25.5=\$17.50
Check: \$17.50\times25.5=\$446.25 ✓
Marco earns $17.50 an hour.
(b) Jonas earns $16.25 an hour at a car wash. How many hours must he work to earn $520.00?
hours = pay ÷ rate
hours =\$520.00\div\$16.25=32
Check: \$16.25\times32=\$520.00 ✓
Jonas must work 32 hours.
(a) 446.25 ÷ 25.5 ENTER shows 17.5.   (b) 520 ÷ 16.25 ENTER shows 32.

Round up to be sure

↑
Round up. When you need at least some amount of money, round the hours up to the next whole hour (or whole shift), even if the decimal is small: 41.1 h means you need 42 h. Rounding down would leave you a little short.
Example 2

Priya’s new phone

Priya earns $15.50 an hour at a grocery store. She wants to earn $650.00 (before deductions) to buy a new phone.

(a) How many whole hours must she work?
hours = pay ÷ rate =\$650.00\div\$15.50=41.935\ldots
Round up: she must work 42 hours.
Check: 42\times\$15.50=\$651.00, which is at least $650 ✓
(41\times\$15.50=\$635.50 is not enough.)
(b) Her shifts are 6 hours long. How many shifts is that?
42\div6=7. Priya needs 7 shifts.

Working Backwards With Overtime

Remember the Alberta rule: hours over 8 in a day (or over 44 in a week, whichever gives more) are overtime, paid at 1.5 times the wage. If someone already works 8 hours every day, any extra hour added to a shift is an overtime hour.

Key idea   How many overtime hours do I need?
  1. Step 1 Find the pay for the regular hours.
  2. Step 2 Extra pay needed = target pay − regular pay
  3. Step 3 Overtime rate = 1.5 × wage
  4. Step 4 Overtime hours = extra pay needed ÷ overtime rate
Example 3

Kofi’s overtime

Kofi works 8 hours a day, Monday to Friday, at a warehouse. He earns $20.00 an hour. Any extra hours he adds to a shift are overtime at time-and-a-half. How many overtime hours does he need to earn $950.00 this week?

Step 1: regular pay =40\times\$20.00=\$800.00
Step 2: extra needed =\$950.00-\$800.00=\$150.00
Step 3: overtime rate =1.5\times\$20.00=\$30.00 an hour
Step 4: overtime hours =\$150.00\div\$30.00=5
Check: \$800.00+5\times\$30.00=\$950.00 ✓  (Weekly rule: 45-44=1 h, so the daily rule gives more.)
Kofi needs 5 hours of overtime.

Finding the wage when there is overtime: count the paid hours

To find the wage when a pay stub has overtime in it, count the paid hours. Each overtime hour is paid like 1.5 regular hours (a double-time hour is paid like 2 hours).

Key idea   paid hours = regular hours + 1.5 × overtime hours   ·   wage = gross pay ÷ paid hours
Example 4

Lena’s wage

Lena’s pay stub shows 40 regular hours and 6 overtime hours (time-and-a-half). Her gross pay is $1 029.00. What is her hourly wage?

paid hours =40+1.5\times6=40+9=49
wage =\$1\,029.00\div49=\$21.00
Check: regular 40\times\$21.00=\$840.00; overtime 6\times\$31.50=\$189.00; \$840.00+\$189.00=\$1\,029.00 ✓
Lena earns $21.00 an hour.

How Much Do I Need to Sell?

A salesperson on commission earns a percent of what they sell. To reach a pay goal, they need to know how much to sell. Rearrange the commission formula the same way.

Key idea   Commission: commission = rate × sales   →   sales needed = commission wanted ÷ rate
Salary + commission: first find the commission needed = target pay − salary. Then divide by the rate.
Graduated commission: use up the first level, then find the sales needed at the next rate.
!
Watch out. Change the percent to a decimal: 8\%=0.08. \$1\,200\div8=\$150 is wrong; \$1\,200\div0.08=\$15\,000 is right. Sales needed are always much bigger than the commission.
Think Why is the sales amount always much bigger than the commission?
The salesperson only keeps a small part of each sale (8¢ of every dollar at 8%). To collect $1 200 in 8¢ pieces, you need a lot of dollars of sales: dividing by a small decimal makes the number bigger.
Example 5

Commission goals

(a) Dana sells furniture and earns 8% commission. How much must she sell to earn $1 200.00 in commission this month?
8\%=0.08
sales = commission ÷ rate
sales =\$1\,200.00\div0.08=\$15\,000.00
Check: 0.08\times\$15\,000=\$1\,200 ✓
Dana must sell $15 000.00.
(b) Raj earns a salary of $1 800.00 a month plus 4% commission on his sales of cell phone plans. How much must he sell to earn $3 000.00 this month?
commission needed =\$3\,000-\$1\,800=\$1\,200
sales =\$1\,200\div0.04=\$30\,000
Check: \$1\,800+0.04\times\$30\,000=\$3\,000 ✓
Raj must sell $30 000.00.

Graduated commission: fill the first level, then the next

With a graduated commission, the first part of the sales pays one rate and the sales above it pay a higher rate. Use up the first level, then work backwards for the rest.

Example 6

Jasmine’s hot tubs

Jasmine sells hot tubs. She earns 3% commission on the first $10 000 of her sales in a month and 5% on all sales over $10 000. How much must she sell to earn $1 000.00 in commission?

Step 1: the first level pays 0.03\times\$10\,000=\$300.
Step 2: still needed =\$1\,000-\$300=\$700, earned at 5%.
Step 3: sales over $10 000 =\$700\div0.05=\$14\,000
Step 4: total sales =\$10\,000+\$14\,000=\$24\,000
Check: \$300+0.05\times\$14\,000=\$300+\$700=\$1\,000 ✓
Jasmine must sell $24 000.00.

Hours Needed After Deductions

Your pay cheque shows your net pay, not your gross pay. To buy something, you need enough net pay. So find how much you take home for each hour, then divide.

Key idea   Hours needed to take home an amount
  1. Step 1 Total deduction rate = add the percents
  2. Step 2 Net per hour = wage × (1 − total deduction rate)
  3. Step 3 Hours = net pay needed ÷ net per hour, rounded up
Buying something in Alberta? Net pay needed = price × 1.05 (5% GST)
i
Note. The CPP, EI and income tax rates in this lesson are simple examples. Real deductions come from CRA tables and change with your pay and the time of year.
Don’t round the net per hour too early. Write at least four decimals, or do it all in one line. In Example 8 below: 944.99 ÷ ( 18.50 × 0.7741 ) ENTER
Example 7

Ethan’s trip to Jasper

Ethan earns $16.00 an hour at a movie theatre. His deductions take about 20% of his gross pay. How many hours must he work to take home $600.00 for a trip to Jasper?

net per hour =\$16.00\times(1-0.20)=\$16.00\times0.80=\$12.80
hours =\$600.00\div\$12.80=46.875 → round up to 47 hours
Check: gross =47\times\$16.00=\$752.00; net =\$752.00\times0.80=\$601.60, at least $600 ✓
Ethan must work 47 hours.
Example 8

Sofia’s laptop

Sofia earns $18.50 an hour. Her deductions are CPP 5.95%, EI 1.64% and income tax 15%. She wants a laptop that costs $899.99 in Calgary.

(a) What is the total cost of the laptop with GST?
\$899.99\times1.05=\$944.9895
\approx\$944.99
(b) What percent of her gross pay does Sofia take home? What is her net pay for one hour?
total deductions =5.95\%+1.64\%+15\%=22.59\%
she keeps 100\%-22.59\%=77.41\%
net per hour =\$18.50\times0.7741 =\$14.320\,85\approx\$14.32
(c) How many hours must she work to buy the laptop? Check your answer.
hours =\$944.99\div14.320\,85=65.99\ldots → 66 hours
Check: gross =66\times\$18.50=\$1\,221.00; net =\$1\,221.00\times0.7741=\$945.18, enough ✓
(65 hours gives only \$930.86.)

Think it through

Think Ethan’s deductions go up to 25%. Does he need more or fewer hours to take home $600.00? How many?
More. He keeps less of each hour: \$16.00\times0.75=\$12.00. \$600.00\div\$12.00=50 hours.
Think Why do we multiply by (1-0.20) and not by 0.20?
\$16.00\times0.20=\$3.20 is the part that is taken off. Ethan keeps the rest, 80%: \$16.00\times0.80=\$12.80.
✓

Key ideas

pay = rate × hours, so hours = pay ÷ rate and rate = pay ÷ hours. Estimate first, and check by putting the answer back in.
Need at least an amount? Round the hours (or shifts) up. Overtime: extra pay needed ÷ (1.5 × wage). Wage from a stub: gross ÷ paid hours (regular + 1.5 × overtime).
Sales needed = commission ÷ rate (rate as a decimal). Salary first: commission needed = target − salary. Graduated: use up the first level first.
Take-home goal: net per hour = wage × (1 − deduction rate); hours = net needed ÷ net per hour, rounded up. Add 5% GST to a price first.
