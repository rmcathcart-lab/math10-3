Overtime: Time-and-a-Half and Double Time

A long week at work? The extra hours pay more. Today we find overtime rates, add overtime to a paycheque, and use Alberta’s 8/44 rule to decide which hours count as overtime.

I can…
  • find a time-and-a-half rate (× 1.5) and a double-time rate (× 2)
  • find gross pay = regular pay + overtime pay
  • use Alberta’s 8/44 rule: find daily and weekly overtime, then use the bigger one
  • go from a timesheet (start, end, unpaid break) to a paycheque

Today: wages with overtime.
1

Overtime Rates

Every job has a usual number of hours, called the regular hours. Hours worked past that are overtime. Overtime hours are paid at a higher rate than your regular hourly wage (your base wage).

Time and a half1.5 times your base wage. This is the usual overtime rate.
Double time2 times your base wage. Some employers and union agreements pay this, for example on holidays or after a very long day. Use it only when a question says the job pays it.

A few agreements use other numbers, such as 1.25 times or 2.5 times (“double time and a half”). They work the same way: multiply the base wage by the number.

Key idea   time-and-a-half rate = base wage × 1.5   ·   double-time rate = base wage × 2
overtime pay = overtime hours × overtime rate
!
Watch out. Time and a half means 1.5 times the wage. It does not mean adding $1.50 to the wage, or adding half an hour. At $18.00/h, time and a half is \$18.00\times1.5=\$27.00/h, not $19.50/h.
≈
Estimate. Half of the wage, added to the wage, gives time and a half. Half of $18 is $9, and \$18+\$9=\$27.
18 × 1.5 ENTER shows 27. Double time: 18 × 2 ENTER shows 36.

Times 1.5, not plus $1.50

Adding $1.50 gives a tiny raise. Multiplying by 1.5 adds half the wage. Look at the size of the difference.

Think Is time and a half at $16.00/h more or less than double time at $12.00/h?
\$16.00\times1.5=\$24.00 and \$12.00\times2=\$24.00. They are the same: $24.00/h.

2

Gross Pay With Overtime

When you work overtime, your gross pay (pay before deductions) has two parts: regular pay for the regular hours and overtime pay for the extra hours.

Key idea: gross pay with overtime
  1. Step 1: regular pay = regular hours × base wage
  2. Step 2: overtime pay = overtime hours × overtime rate
  3. Step 3: gross pay = regular pay + overtime pay
Round to the nearest cent at the end.

The job sets when overtime starts. Many employers pay overtime after 40 hours a week. The law in Alberta sets the latest point it can start (Section 3). An employer may always pay overtime sooner, because that is better for the worker. If a question gives the employer’s rule, use it.

Example 2

Jordan, the line cook

Jordan is a line cook. He earns $17.60/h. Last week he worked 40 regular hours and 6 overtime hours at time and a half. Find his gross pay.

Step 1: regular pay =40\times\$17.60=\$704.00
Step 2: overtime rate =\$17.60\times1.5=\$26.40/h;  overtime pay =6\times\$26.40=\$158.40
Step 3: gross pay =\$704.00+\$158.40=\$862.40
Jordan’s gross pay is $862.40.
If the overtime rate has three decimals, do not round it. Do the overtime pay in one line instead: 6.5 × 1.5 × 22.75 ENTER shows 221.8125. Round only the final answer.
The whole gross pay in one line (the calculator multiplies before it adds): 40 × 17.60 + 6 × 1.5 × 17.60 ENTER shows 862.4, Jordan’s $862.40.

Why not round the rate first?

A rate of $34.125/h rounds to $34.13/h. That tiny change is multiplied by every overtime hour, and it can change the cents in the answer.

Example 3

Priya, the drywall labourer

Priya is a drywall labourer. She earns $22.75/h. Her company pays time and a half for every hour over 40 in a week. This week she worked 46 h 30 min. Find her gross pay.

Hours: 30 min = 0.5 h, so she worked 46.5 h.  Overtime hours =46.5-40=6.5 h
Step 1: regular pay =40\times\$22.75=\$910.00
Step 2: overtime rate =\$22.75\times1.5=\$34.125/h (keep all the decimals);  overtime pay =6.5\times\$34.125=\$221.8125
Step 3: gross pay =\$910.00+\$221.8125=\$1\,131.8125\approx\$1\,131.81
Priya’s gross pay is $1 131.81.

Alberta’s Overtime Rule: 8 Hours a Day or 44 Hours a Week

In Alberta, the law says most workers must get overtime at time and a half for every hour over 8 hours in a day (daily overtime), or every hour over 44 hours in a week (weekly overtime), whichever gives more overtime hours. You work out both, then use the bigger number.

(Rules as of 2026. Rules can change, and a few kinds of jobs have different rules.)

Key idea: Alberta 8/44 rule
  1. Step 1: Daily overtime. For each day, hours over 8. Add them up.
  2. Step 2: Weekly overtime. Total hours for the week − 44. (If the total is 44 or less, this is 0.)
  3. Step 3: Overtime hours = the bigger of the two.  Regular hours = total hours − overtime hours.
  4. Step 4: Find the gross pay (regular pay + overtime pay).
!
Watch out. Do not add the daily and weekly overtime together. They count the same extra hours in two ways. Pick the bigger one.

Why not add them?

The weekly total already includes every hour, the long days too. Take a long day’s extra time away and watch the weekly overtime shrink.

Think Sam works 4 days of 11 h. How many overtime hours is that under the 8/44 rule?
Daily: 4\times3=12 h. Weekly: 44 h in all, so 0 h. Use the bigger: 12 h. A short week can still have lots of overtime.

Example 6

Ethan at the hardware store

Ethan stocks shelves at a hardware store for $16.50/h. He worked Monday to Saturday: 8 h, 8 h, 8.5 h, 8 h, 8 h and 7.5 h. Find his overtime hours and his gross pay.

Step 1: daily overtime: only Wednesday is over 8, by 0.5 h. Daily overtime = 0.5 h
Step 2: total =8+8+8.5+8+8+7.5=48 h;  weekly overtime =48-44=4 h
Step 3: overtime = 4 h (4 is bigger than 0.5);  regular =48-4=44 h
Step 4: regular pay =44\times\$16.50=\$726.00
overtime rate =\$16.50\times1.5=\$24.75/h;  4\times\$24.75=\$99.00
gross =\$726.00+\$99.00=\$825.00
Ethan worked 4 h of overtime. His gross pay is $825.00.
?
When does each rule win? Long days (more than 8 hours) make daily overtime bigger. Many days in a row, even short ones, can make weekly overtime bigger. That is why you always check both.
Math History1872 and 1894

The march for a nine-hour day

In 1872, many workers in Canada worked 10 hours a day or more, six days a week. In Toronto, the printers went on strike on 25 March 1872. They wanted a nine-hour day.

On 15 April, about 2 000 workers marched through Toronto. By the time they reached Queen’s Park, the crowd had grown to about 10 000. The strike leaders were jailed, but that June a new law made unions legal.

In 1894, Canada made Labour Day a national holiday. Limits on work hours, like Alberta’s 8/44 rule, grew out of fights like this one.

Imagine a printer who works 10 h a day, 6 days a week. Under today’s 8/44 rule, how many overtime hours is that?
Daily: 6\times2=12 h. Weekly: 60-44=16 h. Use the bigger: 16 h of overtime every week.

From Timesheet to Paycheque

Real timesheets show start and end times, not hours. Put together what you learned in Lesson 1 and in this lesson:

  1. Step 1: For each day, find the time from start to end, and subtract any unpaid break.
  2. Step 2: Write each day’s hours as a decimal (15 min = 0.25 h, 30 min = 0.5 h, 45 min = 0.75 h).
  3. Step 3: Use the 8/44 rule to find the overtime hours, then the gross pay.
Think Why do we take off the unpaid break before we use the 8/44 rule?
Overtime counts the hours you are paid to work. An unpaid break is not work time, so it is not part of the 8 h or the 44 h.

Key ideas

Overtime rates: time and a half = base wage × 1.5; double time = base wage × 2. Not + $1.50! Estimate: the wage plus half of it.
Gross pay = regular pay + overtime pay. Overtime pay = overtime hours × overtime rate. Round to the nearest cent at the end.
Alberta 8/44 rule: daily overtime (hours over 8 each day) and weekly overtime (total − 44). Use the bigger; never add them. Regular = total − overtime.
Timesheets: end − start − unpaid break, as a decimal (45 min = 0.75 h). Then the 8/44 rule, then the gross pay.
