Hours, Wages and Salaries

Example 1

Decimal hours, and Leah’s shift

(a) Change each time.
(i) 2 h 40 min to decimal hours
(ii) 4.2 h to hours and minutes
(i) 40\div60=0.666\ldots\approx0.67, so 2.67 h
(ii) 0.2\times60=12 min, so 4 h 12 min
(b) Leah works at a grocery store from 08:45 to 15:15 (24-hour clock) with a 30 min unpaid lunch. She earns $15.75/h. What is her gross pay for the shift?
15:15 is 3:15 p.m.
8:45 to 12:45 is 4 h; 12:45 to 3:15 is 2 h 30 min. In all: 6 h 30 min.
Minus the unpaid lunch: 6 h.
6\times\$15.75=\$94.50. Her gross pay is $94.50.
Minutes to hours: 40÷60ENTER shows 0.666…, so 40 min ≈ 0.67 h. Hours to minutes: multiply only the decimal part by 60.

Example 2

Ines’s job offer

Ines is offered a job as an office manager with a salary of $48 360 a year. She would work 40 h a week.

(a) How much is each pay if she is paid biweekly? semi-monthly?
Biweekly: 26 pays. 48\,360\div26=\$1\,860.00
Semi-monthly: 24 pays. 48\,360\div24=\$2\,015.00
(b) What is her hourly equivalent?
Weekly: 48\,360\div52=\$930.00
Hourly: 930\div40=\$23.25 per hour
(c) Another office pays $23.75/h for 37.5 h a week. Which job pays more in a year, and by how much?
Hourly job: 23.75\times37.5=\$890.625 a week; 890.625\times52=\$46\,312.50 a year.
The salary job pays more: 48\,360-46\,312.50=\$2\,047.50 more a year. (The hourly job has a higher rate but fewer hours.)

Think: why is a semi-monthly pay bigger than a biweekly pay? (Semi-monthly is 24 pays a year and biweekly is 26. The same salary cut into fewer pieces gives bigger pieces. Biweekly workers get 2 extra cheques a year.)

Overtime

!
Watch out. In Alberta, check the overtime both ways: add up the hours over 8 each day, and find the hours over 44 for the week. Use the bigger number. Regular hours = total hours − overtime hours.
Example 3

Tyler’s week at the warehouse

Tyler works in a warehouse for $18.40/h. Overtime is paid at time and a half.

(a) Complete the table. How many overtime hours does Tyler get?
Daily: 2+1+3=6 h over 8.
Weekly: 46-44=2 h over 44.
Use the bigger one: 6 h of overtime.
Regular hours: 46-6=40 h.
(b) Find his regular pay, his overtime pay and his gross pay.
Regular: 40\times18.40=\$736.00
Overtime rate: 18.40\times1.5=\$27.60/h
Overtime: 6\times27.60=\$165.60
Gross: 736.00+165.60=\$901.60
(c) The next week Tyler works 9 h a day Monday to Friday and 6 h on Saturday. How many overtime hours does he get now? What is his gross pay?
Total: 5\times9+6=51 h. Daily: 1 h over 8 on each of 5 days = 5 h. Weekly: 51-44=7 h. Use 7 h.
Regular: 51-7=44 h; 44\times18.40=\$809.60. Overtime: 7\times27.60=\$193.20.
Gross: 809.60+193.20=\$1\,002.80.

Think: in (c), the daily count gave 5 h and the weekly count gave 7 h. Why does a long Saturday push the weekly count up but not the daily one? (Saturday is only 6 h, so it is not over 8 that day. But it still adds to the week’s total.)

Other Ways to Earn: Piecework, Contracts, Commission and Extras

Example 4

Trees and a fence

(a) Kiri plants trees in northern Alberta for $0.16 a tree. One day she plants 1 850 trees in 9.5 h. What does she earn, and what is that per hour?
Pay: 1\,850\times0.16=\$296.00
Per hour: 296\div9.5=31.157\ldots\approx\$31.16
She earns $296.00, about $31.16 an hour.
(b) Owen bids $1 400 to stain a fence and deck. Stain and supplies cost him $265. He pays a helper $20.00/h for 12 h, and he works 16 h himself. What is his profit, and his effective hourly rate?
Helper: 12\times20=\$240.00
Profit: 1\,400-265-240=\$895.00
Per hour: 895\div16=55.9375\approx\$55.94
Owen earns about $55.94 for each hour he works.
(c) Give one disadvantage of being paid by the job (a contract) instead of by the hour.
If the job takes longer than planned (rain, mistakes, extra stain), the contractor still gets the same price, so he earns less per hour. He also pays for materials himself.

Check Example 4

Example 5

Three ways to earn commission

Three salespeople are paid in different ways. Find each person’s earnings for the month.

(a) Hana sells phones for a straight commission of 6%. Her sales are $14 250.
14\,250\times0.06=\$855.00
Hana earns $855.00.
(b) Diego earns $1 800 a month plus 2.5% on sales over a quota of $20 000. His sales are $34 600.
Over the quota: 34\,600-20\,000=\$14\,600
Commission: 14\,600\times0.025=\$365.00
Total: 1\,800+365=\$2\,165.00
(c) Sofia earns a graduated commission: 3% on the first $10 000 of sales and 5% on the rest. Her sales are $26 400.
First part: 10\,000\times0.03=\$300.00
Rest: 26\,400-10\,000=\$16\,400; 16\,400\times0.05=\$820.00
Total: 300+820=\$1\,120.00
Percent to decimal first: 6% = 0.06 and 2.5% = 0.025. For (b): (34600−20000)×.025+1800ENTER

Think: what would Diego earn in a slow month with $18 000 in sales? (Only his $1 800 salary. His sales did not reach the $20 000 quota, so there is no commission.)

Example 6

The tip jar and the night shift

(a) Three servers pool their tips and share them by hours worked. The tips total $630. Ana worked 18 h, Ben 12 h and Cole 15 h. How much does each get?
Total hours: 18+12+15=45 h
Per hour: 630\div45=\$14.00
Ana: 18\times14=\$252; Ben: 12\times14=\$168; Cole: 15\times14=\$210
Check: 252+168+210=630 ✓
(b) Jas is a care aide who earns $24.80/h plus a $2.25/h night-shift premium. This week he works 36 h, all on nights, and gets a $150 bonus for perfect attendance. What is his gross pay?
Night rate: 24.80+2.25=\$27.05/h
Wages: 36\times27.05=\$973.80
With the bonus: 973.80+150=\$1\,123.80
His gross pay is $1 123.80.

Working Backwards

!
Watch out. Write the rearranged formula first, then put in the numbers. When you need enough hours to reach an amount, round up. If you need 86.2 h, then 86 h is not enough: round up to 87 h.
Example 8

Rate, sales and a new phone

(a) Liam was paid $612.00 for 36 h. What is his hourly rate?
rate = pay ÷ hours
612\div36=\$17.00 per hour
Check: 36\times17=612 ✓
(b) Grace earns a straight 4% commission. What must she sell to earn $2 000 in a month?
sales = commission ÷ rate
2\,000\div0.04=\$50\,000
Check: 50\,000\times0.04=2\,000 ✓
(c) Zara wants a $1 100 phone, plus 5% GST. After deductions she takes home $13.40 for each hour she works. How many hours must she work?
Cost with GST: 1\,100\times1.05=\$1\,155.00
hours = net pay needed ÷ net pay per hour: 1\,155\div13.40=86.19\ldots
Round up: 86 h gives only 86\times13.40=\$1\,152.40, which is not enough. Check 87 h: 87\times13.40=\$1\,165.80 ✓
Zara must work 87 hours.

Think: why do we round hours up here, even though 86.19 is closer to 86? (Rounding to the nearest gives 86 h, and 86 h of pay is $2.60 short. She can’t buy the phone, so she needs the next whole hour.)

Key ideas

Hours and wages. Minutes ÷ 60 = decimal hours (45 min = 0.75 h). Count a shift to noon or the next full hour; take off unpaid breaks. Gross pay = hours × rate. Salary ÷ 52, 26, 24 or 12 pays.
Overtime. Time and a half = rate × 1.5; double time = rate × 2. Alberta: count hours over 8 each day and hours over 44 in the week. Use the bigger one. Regular hours = total − overtime.
Other ways to earn. Piecework: pieces × pay per piece. Contract: profit = price − costs, then ÷ hours. Commission: sales × rate (only above a quota; a different rate on each part if graduated). Tips, premiums and bonuses add on.
Net pay and working backwards. Net = gross − deductions (each = gross × rate, or a set amount). Undo the formula: hours = pay ÷ rate, rate = pay ÷ hours, sales = commission ÷ rate. Round hours up, and check.
