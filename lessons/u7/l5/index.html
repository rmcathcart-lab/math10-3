The World’s Steepest Street

Baldwin Street in Dunedin, New Zealand, is the steepest street in the world (Guinness World Records). Its steepest part has a grade of about 35%.

Stand up. Hold your arm out at the angle you think that street makes with flat ground.

About how many degrees steep is the world’s steepest street?

A about 10°
B about 19°
C about 35°
D about 50°
B: only about 19°! A grade of 35% means the road goes up about 35 m for every 100 m across. That makes an angle of about 19°, much less than 35°. It is very steep for a road (people zig-zag to walk up it), but a percent grade is not the same as degrees. Today we meet a new ratio that links an angle to the sides of a right triangle: the sine.

The Sine Ratio

Lean a ladder on a wall. The steeper the ladder, the higher it reaches. For a given angle, the opposite side ÷ the hypotenuse is always the same number, whatever the size of the triangle. That number is the sine of the angle. Today we use it to find the side across from an angle, or the hypotenuse.

Opposite ÷ Hypotenuse Stays the Same

In Lesson 4 you named the sides of a right triangle from the angle you are using, \theta (“theta”).

Quick refresher   The labelling routine (Lesson 4)
  1. Step 1. Find the right angle. The side across from it is the hyp. It is always the longest side.
  2. Step 2. Find \theta. The side across from \theta is the opp. It does not touch \theta.
  3. Step 3. The last side is the adj. It touches \theta, and it is not the hyp.
If you switch to the other acute angle, opp and adj swap. The hyp never changes.

In Lesson 4 you divided opp by adj and got the tangent. In this lesson you divide opp by hyp.

Think Sam measured Raj’s small triangle and got opp = 3.3 cm. Then opp ÷ hyp = 0.66, not 0.64. Does that mean the ratio is not always the same?
No. Measurements are rounded (to the nearest tenth of a centimetre), so a ratio from a ruler is a little off. The calculator gives the exact value: \sin40^\circ=0.6427\ldots

Same angle, same ratio

The size of the triangle does not matter. Only the angle matters. This ratio has a name: the sine of the angle (say “sign”). We write \sin 40^\circ.

Key idea   The sine ratio. In a right triangle, for an acute angle \theta:
\sin\theta=\dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}=\dfrac{\text{length of the side opposite }\theta}{\text{length of the hypotenuse}}
Memory aid: SOH = Sine is Opposite over Hypotenuse.

Finding the Sine of an Angle

There are two ways to find the sine of an angle.

  • From a triangle: divide the opposite side by the hypotenuse.
  • From the angle: use the SIN key on your calculator.
Your TI-30XIIS must be in DEG (degree) mode: look for DEG on the screen. If it shows RAD or GRAD, press DRG, use the arrow keys to underline DEG, and press ENTER. To find \sin 40^\circ: SIN 40 ) ENTER shows 0.642787… The SIN key opens a bracket, so close it with ). Round sine values to 4 decimal places: \sin 40^\circ\approx0.6428. That matches Raj’s 0.64.

3

Finding the Opposite Side

If you know the hypotenuse and an angle, you can find the side across from the angle. The unknown is on top of the fraction, so you multiply.

Key idea   Finding a side with the sine ratio
  1. Step 1. Label opp and hyp from the angle. Mark the side you know and the side you want (x).
  2. Step 2. Write \sin\theta=\dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}} and substitute the numbers.
  3. Step 3. Solve. Unknown on top (opp): multiply both sides by the hyp. Unknown on the bottom (hyp): divide (Section 4).
  4. Step 4. Round at the end. Check: the hyp must be the longest side. Write the units (or a sentence).
For x=12\times\sin34^\circ, press 12 × SIN 34 ) ENTER. It shows 6.710…

Example 6

Will Kai’s ladder reach?

Ladder labels say to set a ladder at about 75° to the ground. Kai has a 6.0 m ladder. He needs to reach a gutter that is 5.5 m above the ground. If he sets the ladder at 75°, will it reach the gutter?
The ladder is the hyp (across from the right angle at the wall). The height h is opp.
\sin75^\circ=\dfrac{h}{6.0}, so h=6.0\times\sin75^\circ=5.795\ldots\approx5.8 m
5.8\text{ m}>5.5\text{ m}, so yes, the ladder will reach the gutter (with about 0.3 m to spare).

Finding the Hypotenuse

If you know the opposite side and the angle, you can find the hypotenuse. Now the unknown is on the bottom of the fraction. Multiply both sides by the unknown to bring it up, then divide by the sine.

\sin27^\circ=\dfrac{4.5}{h}\quad\longrightarrow\quad h\times\sin27^\circ=4.5\quad\longrightarrow\quad h=\dfrac{4.5}{\sin27^\circ}
Key idea   Unknown on top: opp = hyp \times\sin\theta (multiply).    Unknown on the bottom: hyp =\dfrac{\text{opp}}{\sin\theta} (divide).
For h=4.5\div\sin27^\circ, press 4.5 ÷ SIN 27 ) ENTER. It shows 9.912…

Why multiply on top, and divide on the bottom?

Think How can you tell whether to multiply or divide before you touch the calculator?
Look where the unknown sits in \sin\theta=\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}. On top (the opp): multiply. On the bottom (the hyp): divide. A hyp answer must also come out longer than the opp.

Solving Problems and Checking Your Answer

For a word problem, sketch a right triangle first. Put the angle in, label opp and hyp, and mark the side you want. Some problems need one more step at the end, such as adding a height.

Example 9

A boom lift and a zipline

(a) A boom lift at a construction site has an arm 9.5 m long. The arm turns on a pivot 1.4 m above the ground. The arm is raised to 62° from the horizontal. How high above the ground is the worker’s bucket at the end of the arm?
The arm is hyp. The height of the bucket above the pivot is opp.
\sin62^\circ=\dfrac{x}{9.5},  x=9.5\times\sin62^\circ=8.388\ldots m
Add the pivot height: 8.388\ldots+1.4=9.788\ldots\approx9.8 m. The bucket is about 9.8 m above the ground.
(b) A zipline at a summer camp starts on a tower and ends on a lower platform 18 m below the start. The cable makes a 7° angle with the horizontal. How long is the cable? (Treat it as straight.)
The drop, 18 m, is opp (across from the 7° angle). The cable c is hyp.
\sin7^\circ=\dfrac{18}{c},  c=\dfrac{18}{\sin7^\circ}=147.699\ldots\approx147.7 m
The cable is about 147.7 m long.
!
Watch out. Keep the full calculator value until the last step. In (a), add 1.4 to 8.388…, not to a rounded 8.4, and round only the final answer.
Do (a) in one go, so nothing is rounded early: 9.5 × SIN 62 ) + 1.4 ENTER shows 9.788…

Key ideas

Labels: the hyp is across from the right angle (the longest side). The opp is across from \theta. The adj touches \theta. Switch angles: opp and adj swap.
SOH: \sin\theta=\dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}, the same in every right triangle with that angle. Calculator in DEG: SIN 40 ) ENTER. Round sines to 4 decimal places.
Less than 1: the hyp is on the bottom and it is the longest side, so \sin\theta<1. Bigger angle, bigger sine. \sin30^\circ=0.5: the opp is half the hyp.
