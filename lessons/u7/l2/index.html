The Pythagorean Theorem: Finding the Hypotenuse

The three sides of every right triangle are connected by one famous rule. Today we discover it with squares, write it as a^2+b^2=c^2, use it to find the longest side, see why it only works for right triangles, and solve real problems: TV screens, a shortcut across a park, and a set of deck stairs.

I can…
  • name the legs and the hypotenuse of a right triangle, and find the hypotenuse with any letters
  • explain how the squares on the sides are connected: a^2+b^2=c^2
  • find the hypotenuse with the \sqrt{\ } key, and check that my answer is reasonable
  • use a counterexample to show the rule only works for right triangles
  • find the right triangle in a real problem and solve it (in one unit, even feet and inches)

1

Squares on the Sides of a Right Triangle

Key words   A right triangle has one right angle (90°), shown with a small square in the corner. The two sides that make the right angle are the legs. The side across from the right angle is the hypotenuse. The hypotenuse is always the longest side.

Draw a square on each side of a right triangle. The areas of the squares are connected in a surprising way. Let’s find out how. A tilted square is hard to count, so we put a box around it and take away the corners.

Example 1

Count the squares

Each small square on the grid is 1 square unit. The legs of the right triangle are 3 units and 4 units long.

(a) Count the squares. Area of square A (on the 3-unit leg): 9
Area of square B (on the 4-unit leg): 16
A =3\times3=9,   B =4\times4=16
(b) Square C sits on the hypotenuse. It is tilted, so it is hard to count. Draw a 7\times7 box around it (corner at the right angle). The box has area 49. Inside the box but outside square C are 4 triangles (one is the grey one). Each has legs 3 and 4, so each has area 3\times4\div2= 6.
Area of square C = 49 - 24 = 25
49-4\times6=49-24=25
(c) Compare: A + B = 25. The side of square C is 5 units, so the hypotenuse is 5 units long.
9+16=25, the same as C. A square of area 25 has side 5 (5\times5=25).

2

The Pythagorean Theorem

The pattern you found is called the Pythagorean theorem. A theorem is a math rule that has been proved to be true every time. Watch the tiles: the two small squares fill the big one.

Key idea   The Pythagorean theorem. In a right triangle with legs a and b and hypotenuse c:
a^2+b^2=c^2
In words: (leg)² + (leg)² = (hypotenuse)². The square on the hypotenuse has the same area as the two squares on the legs put together.

Why is it true for every right triangle? A proof with no counting

Take two big squares of the same size, side a+b. Put four copies of the triangle in each one, in two different ways. The space that is left over must be the same.

Think The squares on the legs of a right triangle have areas 20 cm² and 29 cm². What is the area of the square on the hypotenuse? How long is the hypotenuse?
20+29=49 cm², so the hypotenuse is \sqrt{49}=7 cm. (You don’t need the legs themselves.)

Naming the sides

Each corner (vertex) gets a capital letter. Each side gets the small letter of the corner across from it: side a is across from \angle A, and so on. We usually put the right angle at C, so the hypotenuse is c.

!
Other letters? Find the right angle first. The side across from it is the hypotenuse, and its square goes alone on one side of the equals sign.
Example 3

Which side is the hypotenuse?

For each right triangle, name the hypotenuse. Then write the Pythagorean theorem using the triangle’s own letters.
(a) Hypotenuse: q
q is across from the right angle at Q.
p^2+r^2=q^2
(b) Hypotenuse: y
y is across from the right angle.
x^2+z^2=y^2
(c) A ladder leans on a wall. Hypotenuse: \ell
The wall meets the ground at 90°.
h^2+d^2=\ell^2

The rule is older than Pythagoras

The theorem is named after Pythagoras, a Greek thinker who lived about 2500 years ago. But the rule is much older. A clay tablet from ancient Babylon (in today’s Iraq), about 3800 years old, lists sets of numbers that fit a^2+b^2=c^2. It is called Plimpton 322. Ancient writings from India and China also describe the rule.

Today it finds the size of TV and phone screens, the length of ramps, stairs and rafters, distances on a map, and how far apart two players are in a video game. Carpenters use it to square corners (next lesson).

One row of the tablet is a triangle with a width of 45 and another side of 60. Find its diagonal (the hypotenuse). Does it match the tablet’s 75?
45^2+60^2=2\,025+3\,600=5\,625, and \sqrt{5\,625}=75. Yes, it matches!
3

Finding the Hypotenuse

If you know both legs, you can find the hypotenuse. Square each leg, add, then take the square root to undo the squaring.

Key idea   To find the hypotenuse c:
  1. Step 1. Find the right angle. Label the legs a and b and the hypotenuse c.
  2. Step 2. Write a^2+b^2=c^2 and substitute the two legs.
  3. Step 3. Square each leg, then add.
  4. Step 4. Take the square root: c=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}.
  5. Step 5. Round, and check: c must be longer than each leg but shorter than the two legs added together.
To square a number, press the number, then x². The square root \sqrt{\ } is 2ndx². You can do it all at once: 2ndx² 7.5 x² + 4 x² ) ENTER gives 8.5. The \sqrt{\ } opens a bracket, so close it with ) before ENTER.
!
Watch out. Do not just add the legs: 7.5+4=11.5 is too long. And don’t forget the last step: c^2=72.25 is not the side length. You still need \sqrt{72.25}=8.5.

Two traps, and how to catch them

Both wrong answers fail the Step 5 check. See why.

Think A right triangle has legs 6 m and 8 m. Sam says the hypotenuse is 15 m. How can you tell he is wrong without using the \sqrt{\ } key?
The hypotenuse must be shorter than the two legs added: 6+8=14, and 15 is longer than that. (It is really \sqrt{36+64}=\sqrt{100}=10 m.)

Why It Only Works for Right Triangles

The Pythagorean theorem is only true for right triangles. One example where a rule fails is called a counterexample. One counterexample is enough to show a rule does not work for every triangle.

Example 5

The hinge

Picture two sticks, 3 cm and 4 cm long, joined at one end by a hinge. Open the hinge to three different angles and measure the distance c between the free ends.

Angle at hingeThird side cc^23^2+4^2Is 3^2+4^2=c^2?
60°3.6 cm12.9625No, c^2 is smaller
90°5.0 cm2525Yes
120°6.1 cm37.2125No, c^2 is bigger
Complete: When the angle is smaller than 90°, c^2 is less than a^2+b^2. When it is bigger than 90°, c^2 is greater than a^2+b^2. They are equal only when the angle is 90°.
The 60° and 120° triangles are counterexamples: a^2+b^2=c^2 is only true for the right triangle.

Try it: open and close the hinge

Drag the round handle at the end of the 4 cm stick. Watch c^2 and 3^2+4^2: they match only when the angle is exactly 90°. (At home: cut two paper strips, or use a free geometry app, and measure.)

5

Solving Problems with Right Triangles

Right triangles are everywhere: a wall meets flat ground at 90°, the corner of a rectangle (a screen, a field, a door) is 90°, and north meets east at 90°. When a problem asks for a slanted or diagonal distance, look for the right triangle.

Key idea   Solving a problem:
  1. (1) Sketch it and mark the right angle.
  2. (2) Label the two legs and the hypotenuse. Make sure all lengths are in the same unit.
  3. (3) Use a^2+b^2=c^2.
  4. (4) Check that the answer is reasonable.
  5. (5) Answer with a sentence.
Example 6

Is it really a 55-inch TV?

A TV’s size is the length of the diagonal of its screen, in inches. Amira’s new TV screen is 48.0 in wide and 27.0 in high.
(a) Find the length of the diagonal, to the nearest inch.
48^2+27^2=c^2
2\,304+729=3\,033=c^2
c=\sqrt{3\,033}=55.07\ldots\approx55 in
(b) The box says “55-inch TV”. Is that right? Yes, the diagonal is about 55 in.
Yes, the diagonal is about 55 in.
Example 7

The shortcut across the park

A park in Airdrie is a rectangle 140 m long and 90 m wide. Noah usually walks along two edges to get to the opposite corner. Today he cuts straight across the grass.
(a) How far is the walk along the two edges?
140+90=230 m
(b) How far is the shortcut, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
140^2+90^2=c^2
19\,600+8\,100=27\,700=c^2
c=\sqrt{27\,700}=166.43\ldots\approx166.4 m
(c) How much shorter is the shortcut?
230-166.43\ldots=63.56\ldots The shortcut is about 63.6 m shorter.

Feet and inches

Key idea   Before you square, change every length to one unit, usually inches (1\text{ ft}=12\text{ in}). At the end, change back: divide by 12. The whole number is feet, and the remainder is inches.
Example 8

Kenji’s deck stairs

Kenji is building a set of deck stairs. The side board that holds the steps (the stringer) is the hypotenuse of a right triangle. The stairs rise 5 ft 3 in and run 7 ft along the ground.
(a) How long must the stringer be? Give your answer in feet and inches.
Rise: 5 ft 3 in =5\times12+3=63 in. Run: 7 ft =7\times12=84 in.
63^2+84^2=3\,969+7\,056=11\,025
c=\sqrt{11\,025}=105 in. 105\div12=8 remainder 9, so the stringer is 8 ft 9 in.
(b) Boards come in 8 ft, 10 ft and 12 ft lengths. Which should he buy?
8 ft is too short. Kenji should buy a 10 ft board.
Extra example

More slanted distances

(a) A kite is 30 m above the ground, 16 m across from Jaya’s hand (ignore her height). How long is the string, pulled straight?
30^2+16^2=900+256=1\,156
c=\sqrt{1\,156}=34 m
(b) A soccer field is 100 m long and 64 m wide. How far is it from one corner flag to the opposite one, to the nearest tenth?
100^2+64^2=10\,000+4\,096=14\,096
c=\sqrt{14\,096}=118.72\ldots\approx118.7 m
(c) A rafter runs up 4 ft 6 in and across 6 ft. How long is it, in feet and inches?
54 in and 72 in. 54^2+72^2=2\,916+5\,184=8\,100, \sqrt{8\,100}=90 in. 90\div12=7 R 6: 7 ft 6 in.

Key ideas

Parts: the two sides that make the right angle are the legs. The side across from the right angle is the hypotenuse, always the longest side.
The theorem: a^2+b^2=c^2. The squares on the legs fill the square on the hypotenuse. With other letters, the hypotenuse’s square goes alone.
Find c: square, add, then \sqrt{\ } (2nd x²). Check: longer than each leg, shorter than the legs added. Don’t add the legs; don’t stop at c^2.
Only right triangles: one counterexample (a 60° or 120° hinge) shows it. In problems: find the 90°, use one unit (inches), answer with a sentence.
