The Magic Triangle

Six circles sit on a triangle: one at each corner and one in the middle of each side.

Put the numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 in the circles, one number in each circle.

Can you make the three numbers on every side add up to 9?

?
Try it on paper or on the board. Fast finishers: what is the biggest side total you can make?
Yes! Put the small numbers 1, 2, 3 in the corners. Then 1 + 6 + 2, 2 + 4 + 3 and 3 + 5 + 1 all make 9. The trick: each corner number is on two sides, so it gets counted twice. Small corners make small totals. Biggest total: put 4, 5, 6 in the corners. Every side makes 12 (4 + 3 + 5, 5 + 1 + 6, 6 + 2 + 4). Today we also count sides of triangles, very carefully.

The Cosine Ratio

You know two trig ratios: the tangent (opp ÷ adj) and the sine (opp ÷ hyp). Today you meet the third one, the cosine: adj ÷ hyp. You will use it to find how far a ladder sits from a wall and how long a roof rafter is. Then you will learn to pick the right ratio, sin, cos or tan, for any question.

I can…
  • explain why adj ÷ hyp is the same in similar right triangles, and write \cos\theta=\dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}
  • find the cosine of an angle from a triangle, or with the cos key in DEG mode, and tell if it is reasonable
  • find the adjacent side (multiply) or the hypotenuse (divide) with the cosine ratio
  • choose sin, cos or tan from the side I know and the side I want (SOH CAH TOA)

1

Adjacent ÷ Hypotenuse Stays the Same

In Lessons 4 and 5 you saw that similar right triangles (same shape, different size) keep the same ratio of sides. Opp ÷ adj gave the tangent, and opp ÷ hyp gave the sine. In this lesson you divide adj by hyp.

Quick refresher
  • hyp (hypotenuse): across from the right angle. It is always the longest side.
  • opp (opposite): across from \theta. It does not touch \theta.
  • adj (adjacent): next to \theta, but not the hyp.
  • If you change the angle, opp and adj swap. The hyp never changes.
Example 1

Three triangles, one angle

Three right triangles share the 25^\circ angle at A, so they are similar. A student measured the sides.

Triangleadj AC (cm)hyp AB (cm)adj ÷ hyp
(2 decimals)
AB_1C_12.732.7\div3=0.90
AB_2C_24.554.5\div5=0.90
AB_3C_36.376.3\div7=0.90

For a 25^\circ angle, adj ÷ hyp is always about 0.90. The ratio does not depend on the size of the triangle. It depends only on the angle.

All three give 0.90. The triangles are different sizes, but they have the same shape, so adj ÷ hyp is the same: about 0.90. It depends only on the angle.
Key idea   The cosine ratio. For an acute angle \theta in a right triangle:
\cos\theta=\dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}=\dfrac{\text{length of the side adjacent to }\theta}{\text{length of the hypotenuse}}
Say “cos” (short for cosine). Memory aid: CAH = Cos is Adj over Hyp.

Try it: change the angle

Drag the slider. The three triangles always share the angle \theta. Watch the adj ÷ hyp column: the three rows stay equal to each other, and they match cos θ on the calculator.

Finding the Cosine of an Angle

You can find the cosine of an angle from a triangle (divide adj by hyp), or from the angle with the cos key on your calculator.

Your TI-30XIIS must be in DEG (degree) mode: look for DEG on the screen. If it shows RAD or GRAD, press DRG, move to DEG with the arrow keys, and press ENTER. To find \cos25^\circ: cos 25 ) ENTER shows 0.906307787. Round cosine values to 4 decimal places: \cos25^\circ\approx0.9063. That matches the 0.90 in Example 1. Quick mode test: \cos60^\circ is exactly 0.5.

(b) As the angle gets bigger, \cos\theta gets smaller.

(c) Every value is between 0 and 1.
between 0 and 1
(d) Why can \cos\theta never be more than 1?
adj is a leg and hyp is the longest side, so adj is always smaller than hyp. A smaller number divided by a bigger number is less than 1.
(e) Find \cos20^\circ and \sin70^\circ. What do you notice? Use the triangle in Example 2 to explain.
\cos20^\circ=0.9397 and \sin70^\circ=0.9397: they are equal. The two acute angles add to 90^\circ. The side adjacent to one angle is opposite the other one (in Example 2, 40 is adj for \angle A and opp for \angle B).
For (e): cos 20 ) ENTER and sin 70 ) ENTER show the same number: 0.9396926…

Why is \cos20^\circ the same as \sin70^\circ?

The two acute angles of a right triangle share the same three sides. One leg is adjacent to one angle and opposite the other.

Think \cos60^\circ=0.5. Without a calculator, what is \sin30^\circ?
60^\circ+30^\circ=90^\circ, so the two angles are in the same right triangle. The side adjacent to 60° is opposite 30°. So \sin30^\circ=\cos60^\circ=0.5.
Key idea   Is my answer reasonable?
  • \cos\theta is always between 0 and 1.
  • adj is always shorter than hyp.
  • For a small angle, adj is almost as long as hyp. For an angle close to 90^\circ, adj is very short.
Think A classmate’s calculator says \cos50^\circ=0.9649. Is that reasonable?
No. \cos60^\circ=0.5 and \cos40^\circ\approx0.77, so \cos50^\circ must be between them (it is 0.6428). 0.9649 is what \cos 50 gives in RAD mode: press DRG and choose DEG.
3

Finding the Adjacent Side

Use cosine when the question is about the adj and the hyp: you know one of them and you want the other one. If you want the adj, the unknown is on top of the fraction, so you multiply.

Key idea   Finding a side with the cosine ratio
  1. Step 1. Label adj and hyp from the angle. Mark the side you know and the side you want (x).
  2. Step 2. Write \cos\theta=\dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}} and substitute the numbers.
  3. Step 3. Solve. Unknown on top (adj): multiply both sides by the hyp. Unknown on the bottom (hyp): divide (Section 4).
  4. Step 4. Round at the end. Check: the adj must be shorter than the hyp. Write the units (or a sentence).
For 15\times\cos28^\circ press 15 × cos 28 ) ENTER. Keep all the digits until the last step.
Example 4

Find the adjacent side

(a) Find x to the nearest tenth of a centimetre.
x touches the 28^\circ angle, so x is adj. 15 cm is hyp.
\cos28^\circ=\dfrac{x}{15}
15\times\cos28^\circ=x
x=13.24\ldots\approx13.2 cm   Check: 13.2<15 ✓
(b) A 24-foot ladder leans against a wall. It makes a 75^\circ angle with the ground (a safe angle). How far from the wall is the bottom of the ladder? Round to the nearest tenth of a foot.
The ladder is hyp (24 ft); the distance d along the ground is adj.
\cos75^\circ=\dfrac{d}{24}, so d=24\times\cos75^\circ=6.21\ldots
The bottom of the ladder is about 6.2 ft from the wall.
Check: a safety rule says about \tfrac14 of the ladder’s length: 24\div4=6 ft ✓
(b): 24 × cos 75 ) ENTER shows 6.211657082.
4

Finding the Hypotenuse

If the unknown side is the hyp, it is on the bottom of the fraction. Multiply both sides by the unknown to get it off the bottom, then divide by \cos\theta.

\cos41^\circ=\dfrac{8.5}{x}⟶x\times\cos41^\circ=8.5⟶x=\dfrac{8.5}{\cos41^\circ}
Key idea   Unknown on top: adj = hyp \times\cos\theta (multiply).    Unknown on the bottom: hyp =\dfrac{\text{adj}}{\cos\theta} (divide).
For x=8.5\div\cos41^\circ, press 8.5 ÷ cos 41 ) ENTER. It shows 11.262….
!
Watch out. If you multiply by mistake, you get a hyp that is shorter than the adj. That is impossible: the hyp is the longest side. Check every answer this way.

Why multiply for the adj, but divide for the hyp?

Think of \cos\theta as a part of the hypotenuse. For 28^\circ, the adj is 0.88 (88%) of the hyp. A bar model shows which way to go.

Example 5

Find the hypotenuse

(a) Find x to the nearest tenth of a metre.
8.5 m touches the 41^\circ angle (adj); x is across from the right angle (hyp).
\cos41^\circ=\dfrac{8.5}{x}
x\times\cos41^\circ=8.5
x=8.5\div\cos41^\circ=11.26\ldots\approx11.3 m   Check: 11.3>8.5 ✓
(b) A garage is 7.2 m wide. Its roof rises from each wall to the middle at 26^\circ. How long is each rafter r (from the top of the wall to the peak)? Round to the nearest tenth of a metre.
The middle is half of 7.2 m: adj =7.2\div2=3.6 m. The rafter is hyp.
\cos26^\circ=\dfrac{3.6}{r}, so r=3.6\div\cos26^\circ=4.005\ldots
Each rafter is about 4.0 m long.   Check: 4.0>3.6 ✓
(b): 3.6 ÷ cos 26 ) ENTER shows 4.005366986.
Extra example

Multiply or divide?

(a) The hyp is 20 m and the angle is 37^\circ. Find the adj to the nearest tenth.
adj on top: multiply.
20\times\cos37^\circ=15.97\ldots\approx16.0 m
Check: 16.0<20 ✓
(b) The adj is 12 cm and the angle is 58^\circ. Find the hyp to the nearest tenth.
hyp on the bottom: divide.
12\div\cos58^\circ=22.64\ldots\approx22.6 cm
Check: 22.6>12 ✓
(c) A 30 m rope runs from the top of a flagpole to a peg in the ground at 64^\circ to the ground. How far is the peg from the pole?
The rope is hyp; the ground distance is adj.
30\times\cos64^\circ=13.15\ldots\approx13.2 m

Sine, Cosine or Tangent? Choosing the Ratio

You now know three ratios. To choose, look at the two sides in the question: the side you know and the side you want.

Key idea   SOH CAH TOA
SOH\sin\theta=\dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}
CAH\cos\theta=\dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}
TOA\tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}
Label the sides. Circle the side you know and the side you want. Use the ratio that has both of them.

Example 7

The grain auger

A grain auger is 16 m long. It leans against a grain bin and makes a 32^\circ angle with the ground.

(a) How far from the bin is the bottom of the auger? Round to the nearest tenth of a metre.
Know hyp (16), want adj (d): cos.
\cos32^\circ=\dfrac{d}{16}
d=16\times\cos32^\circ=13.56\ldots
About 13.6 m from the bin.
(b) How high up the bin does the auger reach? Round to the nearest tenth of a metre.
Know hyp (16), want opp (h): sin.
\sin32^\circ=\dfrac{h}{16}
h=16\times\sin32^\circ=8.47\ldots
It reaches about 8.5 m up.
(c) Check your answers with the Pythagorean theorem.
\sqrt{13.6^2+8.5^2}
=\sqrt{184.96+72.25}
=\sqrt{257.21}\approx16.04
This is about 16 m ✓ (not exact because of rounding)

Key ideas

The ratio: \cos\theta=\dfrac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}} (CAH). Similar right triangles with the same angle have the same adj ÷ hyp. Label from the angle: opp and adj swap when the angle changes; the hyp never does.
Calculator: DEG mode (\cos60^\circ=0.5). cos 25 ) ENTER. Round to 4 places. \cos\theta is between 0 and 1, and gets smaller as the angle grows.
Finding a side: adj on top: adj = hyp \times\cos\theta. Hyp on the bottom: hyp = adj \div\cos\theta. Check: the hyp is the longest side.
Choosing: SOH CAH TOA. Label the sides, circle the side you know and the side you want, and use the ratio that has both.
