The Triangle Quilt

Maya is sewing a quilt for her grandma. It is one big triangle made of 16 small triangles.

Two triangles that share a side can’t be the same colour. (Touching at just a corner is fine.)

What is the fewest colours of fabric Maya needs?

A2 colours
B3 colours
C4 colours
D16 colours
A: just 2 colours! Make every triangle that points up yellow and every triangle that points down blue. Every side inside the quilt is shared by one “up” triangle and one “down” triangle, so two neighbours never match. Most people guess 3, because a triangle has 3 sides. A checkerboard works the same way: 2 colours.

Square Roots and the Pythagorean Theorem

Example 1

A root, an angle and a hypotenuse

(a) \sqrt{70} is between which two whole numbers? Estimate it, then find it to the nearest tenth.
8^2=64 and 9^2=81, so \sqrt{70} is between 8 and 9 (a bit closer to 8).
2ndx² 70 )ENTER: \sqrt{70}\approx8.4
(b) A triangle has angles of 90° and 34°. Find the third angle. What kind of triangle is it?
180^\circ-90^\circ-34^\circ=56^\circ
It has a 90° angle, so it is a right triangle.
(c) A right triangle has legs of 9 cm and 14 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
c^2=9^2+14^2=81+196=277
c=\sqrt{277}\approx16.6 cm
Check: longer than 14 ✓

Example 2

The hay bale elevator

A hay bale elevator (a long conveyor) is 15 m long. Its lower end sits on the ground, 9.5 m from the wall of a barn. Its top end rests at the bottom of the hayloft door. How high is the hayloft door, to the nearest tenth of a metre?

Find the height of the hayloft door, h.
The elevator is the hypotenuse (across from the right angle at the wall). We want a leg, so subtract.
h^2=15^2-9.5^2=225-90.25=134.75
h=\sqrt{134.75}\approx11.6 m
Check: a leg must be shorter than the hypotenuse: 11.6<15 ✓
The hayloft door is about 11.6 m above the ground.
!
Watch out. Add the squares only when you want the hypotenuse. For a leg, subtract: hypotenuse² minus the other leg². Adding here would give 17.8 m, longer than the elevator itself.
Subtract the squares, then take the root: 15 x²− 9.5 x²ENTER gives 134.75. Then 2ndx² 134.75 )ENTER gives 11.608…
Example 3

A shed floor and a right triangle test

(a) Gita is framing a shed floor 2.4 m by 3.2 m. What should the diagonal be if the corners are square? She measures 4.06 m. Are the corners square?
2.4^2+3.2^2=5.76+10.24=16,  \sqrt{16}=4
The diagonal should be 4.00 m. 4.06 m is too long, so the corners are not square (the angle is a little more than 90°).
(b) Is a triangle with sides 16 cm, 63 cm and 65 cm a right triangle? Explain using the Pythagorean theorem.
Longest side: 65.  16^2+63^2=256+3\,969=4\,225
65^2=4\,225. The two are equal, so yes, it is a right triangle (the right angle is across from the 65 cm side).

Think: in (a), if the diagonal had been shorter than 4.00 m, which way would the corner be off? (Less than 90°: the two sides are pulled closer together.)

The Trig Ratios: Finding Sides

Example 4

Three ratios from one triangle

(a) Label opp, adj and hyp for \theta. Then write \sin\theta, \cos\theta and \tan\theta as fractions and as decimals (to 4 decimal places).
hyp = 37 (across from the right angle), opp = 12 (across from \theta), adj = 35
\sin\theta=\frac{12}{37}\approx0.3243,  \cos\theta=\frac{35}{37}\approx0.9459,  \tan\theta=\frac{12}{35}\approx0.3429
(b) A bigger triangle has the same angle \theta and sides 24, 70 and 74. Find its \tan\theta. What do you notice? Why?
\tan\theta=\frac{24}{70}\approx0.3429, the same. The triangles are similar (every side is doubled), so the ratios do not change. A trig ratio depends only on the angle.

Check Example 4: label from θ, then SOH CAH TOA

Example 6

The solar panel near Taber

A solar panel on a farm near Taber is tilted at 35° to the ground. Seen from the side, the panel covers a horizontal distance of 1.6 m.

(a) How much higher is the top edge of the panel than the bottom edge (the rise)?
For the 35° angle, 1.6 m is adj and the rise is opp: TOA.
rise =1.6\times\tan35^\circ\approx1.12 m, so about 1.1 m
(b) How long is the panel (the sloped side)?
The panel is hyp, and we know adj: CAH.
\cos35^\circ=\dfrac{1.6}{x},  x=1.6\div\cos35^\circ\approx2.0 m
(c) Check (b) with the Pythagorean theorem.
\sqrt{1.6^2+1.1203^2}=\sqrt{3.8151}\approx1.95, so about 2.0 m ✓  (use the unrounded rise, 1.1203…)
Unknown on the bottom: divide. 1.6 ÷cos 35 )ENTER gives 1.953…

Finding Angles, Elevation and Depression

Example 7

A boat launch and a coulee path

(a) A boat launch drops 1.8 m over a horizontal distance of 14 m. Find the angle the launch makes with the horizontal.
We have opp and adj: TOA.
\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{1.8}{14}\right)
2ndtan 1.8 ÷ 14 )ENTER
\theta\approx7^\circ
(b) A straight path up the side of a coulee is 250 m long and climbs 60 m. Find the angle between the path and the horizontal, and the other acute angle of the triangle.
We have opp and hyp: SOH.
\theta=\sin^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{60}{250}\right)\approx14^\circ
Other angle: 90^\circ-14^\circ=76^\circ
Check: the 76° angle faces the longer leg ✓

Example 8

The church steeple and the lost dog

(a) Nolan stands 55 m from the base of a church steeple. With a clinometer held at eye level, 1.5 m above the ground, the angle of elevation to the tip of the steeple is 27°. How tall is the steeple?
From the eye: adj = 55 m, want opp: TOA.
opp =55\times\tan27^\circ\approx28.02 m
Add the eye height: 28.02+1.5\approx29.5 m
The steeple is about 29.5 m tall.
(b) A worker on the walkway of a water tower, 32 m above the ground, sees a lost dog at an angle of depression of 9°. How far is the dog from the base of the tower? (Flat ground.)
The angle of elevation from the dog to the worker is also 9° (alternate interior angles).
In that triangle, 32 m is opp and d is adj: TOA.
\tan9^\circ=\dfrac{32}{d},  d=32\div\tan9^\circ\approx202.0 m

Solving Right Triangles

Example 9

Solve the triangle

In \triangle ABC, \angle C=90^\circ, \angle A=41^\circ and the hypotenuse c=15 cm. Solve the triangle (find \angle B, a and b).

Find \angle B, a and b. Then check your answers two ways.
\angle B=90^\circ-41^\circ=49^\circ
For \angle A: a is opp, b is adj, c is hyp.
a=15\times\sin41^\circ\approx9.8 cm  (SOH)
b=15\times\cos41^\circ\approx11.3 cm  (CAH)
Check with Pythagoras: 9.841^2+11.321^2\approx225=15^2 ✓
Check: the bigger angle (49°) faces the longer leg (b) ✓
Both legs from the hypotenuse: 15 ×sin 41 )ENTER and 15 ×cos 41 )ENTER.

Think: once you have a, could you find b with the Pythagorean theorem instead? (Yes: b=\sqrt{15^2-9.841^2}\approx11.3. Use the unrounded a.)

Example 10

The cell tower and its two guy wires

A cell tower is 52 m tall. Two guy wires run from the top of the tower to anchors on opposite sides of the tower, on flat ground. One wire makes an angle of 61° with the ground and the other makes 48°. How far apart are the two anchors?

Find the distance between the two anchors.
Two right triangles share the 52 m side (the tower). In each, the tower is opp and the ground distance is adj: TOA.
Left: x=52\div\tan61^\circ\approx28.82 m
Right: y=52\div\tan48^\circ\approx46.82 m
Total: 28.82+46.82\approx75.6 m
The anchors are about 75.6 m apart. (Reasonable: the flatter wire, 48°, has its anchor farther from the tower.)
One triangle at a time: 52 ÷tan 61 )ENTER, then 52 ÷tan 48 )ENTER. Add the two full answers, then round.

Think: what if both anchors were on the same side of the tower? (Then the distance between them is 46.82-28.82\approx18.0 m: subtract instead of add.)
