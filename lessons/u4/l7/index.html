Warm-up

One Spoonful Back

Glass A holds 1 cup of orange juice. Glass B holds 1 cup of water.

Move one spoonful of juice from A into B and stir. Then move one spoonful of that mix from B back into A.

Now is there more juice in Glass B, or more water in Glass A?

?
Pick one: more juice in B, more water in A, or the same. Convince the person beside you.
They are exactly the same. After both moves, each glass holds 1 cup again. So whatever juice is missing from Glass A was filled back in by water, the same amount. That juice is sitting in Glass B. Try numbers: 100 mL cups, a 10 mL spoon. B gets 10 mL of juice (110 mL). A spoonful of the mix back is about 0.91 mL juice and 9.09 mL water. Now A has 9.09 mL of water, and B has 10-0.91=9.09 mL of juice.

Volume and Capacity

Example 1

The raised garden bed

A raised garden bed is a box 2.4 m long, 1.2 m wide and 0.35 m deep.

(a) How many cubic metres of soil fill the bed?
V=lwh=2.4\times1.2\times0.35
V=1.008\text{ m}^3
It takes about 1.008 m³ of soil.
(b) Garden soil is sold in 25 L bags. How many bags are needed?
1\text{ m}^3=1\,000 L, so 1.008\times1\,000=1\,008 L
1\,008\div25=40.32 bags
40 bags is not quite enough, so round up: 41 bags.

Think: why can’t you buy 40 bags and stop? (40 bags hold 40\times25=1\,000 L. The bed needs 1008 L, so it would be 8 L short.)

Example 2

The rain barrel

A rain barrel is a cylinder 60 cm across (diameter) and 90 cm tall.

(a) Find the volume of the barrel in cubic centimetres.
r=60\div2=30 cm
V=\pi r^2h=\pi\times30^2\times90\approx254\,469\text{ cm}^3
(b) How many litres does it hold?
1\text{ L}=1\,000\text{ cm}^3: 254\,469\div1\,000\approx254.5 L
(c) An American store lists the barrel in US gallons. About how many US gallons is it? (1 US gal \approx3.785 L)
254.469\div3.785\approx67.2
The barrel holds about 67.2 US gallons.
(d) A British store lists it in British gallons (1 gal \approx4.546 L). Find that number. Why is it smaller than (c)?
254.469\div4.546\approx56.0 British gallons
A British gallon is bigger than a US gallon, so it takes fewer of them to fill the same barrel.
Cylinder on the TI-30XIIS: π×30x²×90ENTER gives 254 469.0049. Keep that number for (b), (c) and (d), and round at the end.

Think: what goes wrong if you put the diameter, 60, into \pi r^2h? (60^2 is 4 times 30^2, so the answer would be 4 times too big: about 1018 L.)

Check Example 2: the rain barrel

Example 3

Dev’s concrete pad

Dev is pouring a concrete pad for a shed. The pad is 10 ft by 12 ft and 4 in thick.

(a) Find the volume of the pad in cubic feet.
Same units first: 4 in =4\div12=\tfrac13 ft
V=10\times12\times\tfrac13=40\text{ ft}^3
(b) Change the volume to cubic yards.
1\text{ yd}^3=27\text{ ft}^3, so divide by 27.
40\div27\approx1.48\text{ yd}^3
(c) The concrete plant sells in half cubic yards (0.5, 1, 1.5, …) at $210.00 per cubic yard. How much should Dev order, and what will it cost before tax?
1.48 yd³ is more than 1, so he orders the next half yard up: 1.5 yd³.
Cost: 1.5\times\$210.00=\$315.00. Dev orders 1.5 yd³ for $315.00.

Check Example 3: the concrete pad

Why 27 and 1728? Build the cube

A cubic yard is a cube 1 yd on each edge. Every edge is 3 ft, so count the little 1 ft cubes: one layer, then the layers.

Example 4

Phoenix, Calgary and a cookie recipe

(a) The forecast in Phoenix, Arizona, is a high of 104 °F. What is that in Celsius?
C=(104-32)\div1.8
=72\div1.8=40
It will be 40 °C in Phoenix.
(b) It is -15 °C in Calgary. What is that in Fahrenheit?
F=1.8\times(-15)+32
=-27+32=5
It is 5 °F in Calgary.
(c) Use the quick estimate to change 22 °C to Fahrenheit. Then find the exact answer. Is the estimate good enough to decide what to wear?
Estimate: 2\times22+30=74 °F
Exact: 1.8\times22+32=71.6 °F
Yes. The estimate is only about 2 degrees off, so it is good enough to choose clothes.
(d) A cookie recipe from a US website says to bake at 375 °F. What should you set a Celsius oven to?
(375-32)\div1.8=343\div1.8\approx190.6 °C
Ovens go up in steps of 5 or 10 degrees, so set the oven to about 190 °C.

Example 5

Fill in the blanks, and Baby Amira

(a) Fill in the blanks.
3.6 kg = 3600 g
750 mg = 0.75 g
4.2 t = 4200 kg
kg to g: \times1\,000: 3.6\times1\,000=3\,600
mg to g: \div1\,000: 750\div1\,000=0.75
t to kg: \times1\,000: 4.2\times1\,000=4\,200

3 lb 6 oz = 54 oz
74 oz = 4 lb 10 oz
3\times16=48; 48+6=54 oz
74\div16=4 remainder 10 (4\times16=64, 74-64=10)
74 oz = 4 lb 10 oz
(c) Baby Amira weighed 7 lb 9 oz at birth and 11 lb 4 oz at two months. How much did she gain?
4 oz − 9 oz will not work, so borrow 1 lb = 16 oz: 11 lb 4 oz = 10 lb 20 oz

Example 6

Suitcases, ground beef, chips and hay

Use 1 lb \approx0.454 kg, 1 kg \approx2.2 lb, 1 oz \approx28.35 g and 1 ton \approx0.907 t.

(a) An airline allows a checked bag of up to 23 kg. Jordan’s suitcase weighs 48 lb on his bathroom scale. Is it under the limit?
48\times0.454\approx21.8 kg
Check: 21.8 is less than 48 ✓
21.8<23, so the suitcase is under the limit.
(b) Ground beef is $5.99/lb at one store and $12.99/kg at another. Which is the better price? (Use 1 kg \approx2.2 lb.)
1 kg is about 2.2 lb, so a kilogram at the first store costs
5.99\times2.2\approx\$13.18 per kg.
$12.99/kg is less than $13.18/kg, so the second store is the better price.
(c) A bag of chips is labelled 12 oz. What is that in grams?
12\times28.35=340.2 g
(d) A load of hay is 1.5 tons. What is that in tonnes?
1.5\times0.907\approx1.36 t

Think: in (a), how could you check with the other number, 1 kg \approx2.2 lb? (48\div2.2\approx21.8 kg. The same answer, because 0.454 and 2.2 are two ways of saying the same thing.)

Bushels and Tonnes

!
Watch out. A tonne holds many bushels, so the number of bushels is always bigger than the number of tonnes. If your tonnes answer is bigger, you multiplied when you should have divided.
Example 7

The canola and barley harvest

A farmer near Lethbridge harvests 8820 bushels of canola. For canola, use 44.1 bu/t.

(a) How many tonnes of canola is that?
bushels ÷ factor = tonnes
8\,820\div44.1=200 t
(b) The elevator pays $680.00 per tonne. How much is the crop worth?
200\times\$680.00=\$136\,000.00
The crop is worth $136 000.00.
(c) What is the price per bushel, to the nearest cent?
One tonne is 44.1 bushels, so one bushel is worth
\$680.00\div44.1\approx\$15.42 per bushel.
(d) The farmer also has 92 t of barley. For barley, use 45.9 bu/t. How many bushels is that?
tonnes × factor = bushels
92\times45.9=4\,222.8, about 4223 bushels
Check the size: 8820÷44.1ENTER gives 200, fewer tonnes than bushels ✓. 92×45.9ENTER gives 4222.8, more bushels than tonnes ✓.

Key ideas

Volume. Same units first. Prism: area of base × height (a box is lwh). Cylinder: \pi r^2h with the radius. 1\text{ yd}^3=27\text{ ft}^3, 1\text{ ft}^3=1\,728\text{ in}^3, 1\text{ m}^3=1\,000\,000\text{ cm}^3.
Capacity. 1\text{ cm}^3=1\text{ mL}, 1\text{ m}^3=1\,000 L. US gallon \approx3.785 L; British gallon \approx4.546 L, about 20% bigger, so you need fewer of them. Round up for bags and loads.
Temperature. F=1.8C+32. C=(F-32)\div1.8: subtract 32 first. Quick estimate: double and add 30. Use (−) for a negative.
Mass. SI: × or ÷ 1000. 1 lb = 16 oz: regroup 16 oz as 1 lb, borrow 1 lb as 16 oz. 1 kg \approx2.2 lb (more pounds than kilograms). Bushels ÷ factor = tonnes; tonnes × factor = bushels.
