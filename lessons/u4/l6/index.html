Two Combines, One Field

A farmer near Vulcan has a field of wheat to harvest.

The new combine can harvest the whole field in 3 hours. The old combine takes 6 hours.

If both combines work on the field together, how long will it take?

A 4.5 hours
B 3 hours
C 2 hours
D 9 hours
C, 2 hours. Think about 6 hours: the new combine could harvest 2 fields and the old one 1 field. That is 3 fields in 6 hours, so 1 field takes 6\div3=2 hours. 4.5 h (the average) can’t be right: the new combine alone only takes 3 h, and help can only make it faster!

From Volume to Mass: Bushels, Tonnes and Conversion Factors

A bin of wheat, a truck of gravel, a jug of milk: we know how much space it takes. But how heavy is it? Today we use a conversion factor to change a volume into a mass, and back, all the way to Prairie grain in bushels and tonnes.

I can…
  • use a conversion factor: mass = volume × factor and volume = mass ÷ factor
  • explain what a bushel is, and why a bushel of wheat is heavier than a bushel of oats
  • change bushels to tonnes (÷ factor) and tonnes to bushels (× factor)
  • change a price per bushel to a price per tonne, and back
  • find what a grain bin or truck box holds: volume → bushels → tonnes

1

Volume and Mass Are Different

Volume is how much space something takes up (L, m³, ft³). Mass is how heavy it is (kg, t, lb). A bucket of sand and the same bucket of popcorn have the same volume, but the sand is much heavier. So to change a volume into a mass, you need to know what the stuff is.

A conversion factor tells you how much mass is in one unit of volume of one material, for example “milk: 1.03 kg per L”. Water is the easy one: the metric system was built so that 1 mL of water has a mass of about 1 g.

Key idea
Water: 1 mL ≈ 1 g  ·  1 L ≈ 1 kg  ·  1 m³ = 1000 L ≈ 1000 kg = 1 t
Any material:   mass = volume × (mass per unit of volume)     volume = mass ÷ (mass per unit of volume)
Think Which is heavier: a 4 L jug of milk or a 4 kg bag of flour?
The milk: 4\times1.03=4.12 kg, a little more than 4 kg. (Water would be a tie: 4 L of water ≈ 4 kg.)
!
Watch out. The units must match. If the factor is “kg per L”, the volume must be in litres (change mL to L first), and the answer is in kg.

Loads and limits: round down or round up?

↕
Round down or round up? If a load must not go over a limit, round the amount down, so you stay under the limit. If you need enough loads, trips or bags to do the whole job, round up.
Example 2

Ravi’s gravel driveway

Ravi is building a gravel driveway in Cochrane. Gravel has a mass of about 1.7 t per m³. The driveway needs 8 m³ of gravel. The landscaper’s dump truck can carry at most 6 t.

(a) What is the mass of the gravel Ravi needs?
mass = volume × factor
8\times1.7=13.6 t
The gravel has a mass of 13.6 t.
(b) How many cubic metres can the truck carry in one load? Round down to the nearest tenth.
volume = mass ÷ factor
6\div1.7=3.529\ldots
Round down: 3.5 m³ per load.
(c) How many loads are needed?
13.6\div6=2.27\ldots loads
Round up: 3 loads.
(2 loads carry only 12 t.)

What Is a Bushel?

A bushel (bu) is an old imperial unit of volume for grain and other dry crops. One bushel is 8 imperial (British) gallons. That is about the space inside a box 33 cm on each side.

Key idea   1 bushel ≈ 36.4 L ≈ 1.28 ft³ ≈ 2220 in³   ·   1 m³ ≈ 27.5 bu

Prairie farmers still talk in bushels (“this bin holds 3000 bushels”). But grain elevators weigh grain and pay by the tonne. So farmers must change bushels to tonnes.

US
US or British? A US bushel is a little smaller (about 35.2 L), just like a US gallon is smaller than a British gallon. In this lesson a bushel is the Canadian (imperial) bushel.

Each crop has its own factor: bushels per tonne

A bushel of heavy grain weighs more than a bushel of light grain. So each crop has its own conversion factor in bushels per tonne (bu/t): the number of bushels it takes to make 1 tonne (1000 kg).

CropWheatCanolaBarleyOats
Bushels per tonne36.744.145.964.8

Factors in this lesson are rounded examples. Grain buyers use exact tables for each crop.

Key idea   A bigger factor means a lighter bushel: you need more bushels to make a tonne.
Mass of one bushel: kg per bushel = 1000 ÷ factor    e.g. wheat: 1000\div36.7\approx27.2 kg
Think Without a calculator: is a bushel of canola (44.1 bu/t) heavier or lighter than a bushel of wheat (36.7 bu/t)?
Lighter. It takes more bushels of canola to make the same tonne, so each canola bushel holds less mass.

Converting Between Bushels and Tonnes

The factor says how many bushels make 1 tonne. So:

Key idea
Tonnes → bushels: multiply by the factor.   12\text{ t}\times\dfrac{36.7\text{ bu}}{1\text{ t}}=440.4\text{ bu}
Bushels → tonnes: divide by the factor.   850\text{ bu}\times\dfrac{1\text{ t}}{44.1\text{ bu}}=850\div44.1\approx19.27\text{ t}

Write the factor as a fraction so the unit you start with cancels (t on the bottom to cancel t; bu on the bottom to cancel bu).

✓
Check. The number of bushels is always bigger than the number of tonnes (about 35 to 65 times bigger). If your tonnes come out bigger than your bushels, you went the wrong way.
≈
Rounding. Unless a question says otherwise, round bushels to the nearest whole bushel and tonnes to two decimals (the nearest 10 kg).

Why divide? Count how many tonnes fit

Every 44.1 bushels of canola make one tonne. So the number of tonnes is the number of groups of 44.1 in the bushels: a division.

On the truck scale

An elevator weighs the whole truck on a truck scale. A legal load has a mass limit. To find the most grain you can haul, change the limit to bushels and round down.

Example 5

Barley for the cattle, wheat on the truck

(a) A hobby farm near Olds buys 150 bushels of barley for its cattle. Find the mass in tonnes, in kilograms (to the nearest kg) and in pounds. Use 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb.
Tonnes: 150\div45.9=3.2679\ldots t
Kilograms: 3.2679\times1000\approx3268 kg
Pounds: 3268\times2.2=7189.6\approx7190 lb
The barley is about 3.27 t, 3268 kg or 7190 lb.
(b) Nadia’s grain truck may legally carry 14.5 t. Can she haul 560 bushels of wheat in one load? What is the most wheat (whole bushels) she can haul?
560\div36.7\approx15.26 t. That is more than 14.5 t, so no.
Most wheat: 14.5\times36.7=532.15 bu
Round down: she can haul 532 bushels.

Price per Bushel and Price per Tonne

Elevators post grain prices in dollars per tonne, but many farmers still think in dollars per bushel. One tonne of wheat is 36.7 bushels, so the price of one tonne is shared by 36.7 bushels.

Key idea   $ per bushel = $ per tonne ÷ factor     $ per tonne = $ per bushel × factor
The price per tonne is always the bigger number, because a tonne is many bushels.
Money. Keep all the decimals in your calculator and round to the nearest cent only at the end. 680 ÷ 44.1 ENTER shows 15.41950…, about $15.42 per bushel.

Why does the same factor work for prices?

One tonne of wheat is 36.7 bushels. If one bushel sells for $7.80, then 36.7 bushels sell for 36.7 times as much: 7.80\times36.7=\$286.26. Going the other way, the price of a tonne is shared by its 36.7 bushels, so you divide.

This is the same idea as meat prices in Lesson 5: the bigger unit (the tonne) always has the bigger price.

Think Without a calculator: canola (44.1 bu/t) at $15.00 a bushel is about how much per tonne?
About 15\times44=660, so about $660 per tonne. (Exactly: 15\times44.1=\$661.50.)

Grain Bins: From Volume to Bushels

A round steel grain bin is a cylinder, so the grain inside has volume V=\pi r^2h (Lesson 1), where h is how deep the grain is. To find what the bin holds, change the volume to bushels, then to tonnes.

Key idea   volume → bushels → tonnes
ft³ → bu: divide by 1.28 (1 bu ≈ 1.28 ft³)      m³ → bu: multiply by 27.5 (1 m³ ≈ 27.5 bu)
Think Why do we divide by 1.28 for cubic feet, but multiply by 27.5 for cubic metres?
A bushel is bigger than a cubic foot (1 bu ≈ 1.28 ft³), so there are fewer bushels than cubic feet: divide. A cubic metre is bigger than a bushel (1 m³ ≈ 27.5 bu), so there are more bushels than cubic metres: multiply.
✓
Check. The number of bushels is a bit less than the number of cubic feet (about \tfrac45 of it), but about 27 times more than the number of cubic metres. Real bins have a cone-shaped pile on top. In this lesson the grain is level.
Example 8

A wheat bin near Lethbridge

A wheat bin on a farm near Lethbridge has a diameter of 18 ft. The wheat is level and 15 ft deep.

(a) Find the volume of the wheat, to the nearest cubic foot.
r=18\div2=9 ft
V=\pi r^2h=\pi\times9^2\times15\approx3817 ft³
(b) How many bushels is that?
3817\div1.28\approx2982 bu
(c) Find the mass of the wheat (36.7 bu/t), to the nearest tenth of a tonne.
2982\div36.7\approx81.3 t
(d) A truck hauls 600 bushels per trip. How many trips will it take to empty the bin?
2982\div600=4.97 trips. Round up: 5 trips.
Example 9

A grain truck box

A grain truck box is 6.1 m long and 2.4 m wide. It is filled level to a depth of 1.5 m. The truck may carry at most 14.5 t.

(a) Find the volume of grain in the box.
V=lwh=6.1\times2.4\times1.5=21.96 m³
(b) How many bushels is that, to the nearest bushel?
21.96\times27.5=603.9\approx604 bu
(c) If the box is full of canola (44.1 bu/t), what is the mass? Is the load legal?
604\div44.1\approx13.70 t
13.70 t is less than 14.5 t, so the load is legal.
(d) If the box is full of wheat (36.7 bu/t), is the load legal? What should the driver do?
604\div36.7\approx16.46 t, more than 14.5 t, so it is not legal.
The driver should not fill the box: load at most 532 bu of wheat (Example 5).

Key ideas

Volume is space, mass is heaviness. mass = volume × factor; volume = mass ÷ factor. Water: 1 L ≈ 1 kg, 1 m³ ≈ 1 t. Round down to stay under a limit, up for enough loads.
A bushel is 8 imperial gallons ≈ 36.4 L ≈ 1.28 ft³; 1 m³ ≈ 27.5 bu. Each crop has a factor in bushels per tonne. Bigger factor → lighter bushel: kg per bu = 1000 ÷ factor.
t → bu: × factor. bu → t: ÷ factor. Bushels are always the bigger number. Round bushels to a whole number and tonnes to two decimals.
Prices: $/bu = $/t ÷ factor; $/t = $/bu × factor. Bins: volume (\pi r^2h or lwh) → bushels (÷ 1.28 for ft³, × 27.5 for m³) → tonnes.
