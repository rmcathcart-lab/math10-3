Mass, Weight and Referents

Mass is how much matter (stuff) is in an object. In everyday speech we say weight: “weigh the apples”, “my weight”. In this lesson, mass and weight mean the same thing. (A scientist would say weight is the pull of gravity. On the Moon you would weigh less, but your mass would not change.)

Canada uses SI (metric) units of mass. You will still see imperial units on meat counters, at the gym, on baby scales and in trucking.

SI units of mass
milligrammgmedicine and vitamins (a pill might have 500 mg)
gramga paper clip is about 1 g; a toonie is about 7 g
kilogramkg1 L of water is about 1 kg; a bag of sugar is often 2 kg
tonnet1000 L of water is about 1 t; a small car is about 1.3 t
Imperial units of mass
ounceoza slice of bread is about 1 oz; a hockey puck is about 6 oz
poundlba block of butter is 1 lb
ton(tn)a big bison bull is about 1 ton
!
Watch out. A tonne (t) is metric: 1 t = 1000 kg. A ton is imperial: 1 ton = 2000 lb. They are different units, even though they sound the same. (The imperial ton is also called a short ton.)
Think Which is heavier: 1 tonne or 1 ton?
The tonne, a little. 1 t = 1000 kg, which is about 2200 lb. That is a bit more than the 2000 lb in a ton. (Next lesson: 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb.)

Converting SI Units of Mass

SI units use the same prefixes as length: kilo means 1000, milli means \tfrac{1}{1000}. Each step on the staircase is 10 times the next one down.

The grey units are rarely used for mass. The ones you need are 1000 times apart, so each jump is three steps:

Key idea   t → kg → g → mg: × 1000 each jump going down; ÷ 1000 each jump going up.
1 t = 1000 kg1 kg = 1000 g1 g = 1000 mg
Bigger unit → smaller unit: multiply.    Smaller unit → bigger unit: divide.
✓
Check. When you change to a smaller unit you need more of them, so the number gets bigger: 2.4 kg = 2400 g. Multiplying or dividing by 1000 moves the decimal point 3 places.
Think Which is more: 0.5 kg of cheese or 450 g of cheese?
Same unit first: 0.5\times1000=500 g. 500 g is more than 450 g, so 0.5 kg is more (by 50 g).

Math History1889 to 2019

The kilogram that lived in a vault

For 130 years, one kilogram was the mass of one small metal cylinder in a vault near Paris, France. Scientists called it Le Grand K (“the big K”). It sat under three glass bell jars. Countries kept copies of it to check their own scales.

But copies drifted apart by about 50 micrograms, about the mass of a grain of salt. So on May 20, 2019, the kilogram got a new definition from a number in physics that never changes.

Le Grand K is exactly 1 kg. What is that in grams? In milligrams?
Going down to smaller units, multiply: 1\times1000=1000 g, and 1000\times1000=1\,000\,000 mg. One kilogram is a million milligrams!
Example 3

Ruby’s bake sale

Ruby is baking bread for a bake sale. Each loaf uses 650 g of flour. She bakes 6 loaves. Flour comes in 2.5 kg bags.

(a) How much flour does she need, in kilograms?
6\times650=3900 g
3900\div1000=3.9 kg
She needs 3.9 kg of flour.
(b) How many bags of flour must she buy?
3.9\div2.5=1.56 bags
You cannot buy part of a bag, so round up.
She must buy 2 bags.
(c) How much flour will be left over? Is it enough for one more loaf?
2 bags: 2\times2.5=5 kg
5-3.9=1.1 kg =1100 g left
Yes: 1100 g > 650 g.

Ruby’s flour, bag by bag

Fill the bags with what Ruby needs. Whatever is left in the second bag is the leftover flour.

Example 4

Can the truck cross the bridge?

A flatbed truck has a mass of 4.8 t when it is empty. It is loaded with 14 pallets of patio stones. Each pallet has a mass of 925 kg. A sign on a bridge says the limit is 18 t.

(a) Find the mass of the pallets, in tonnes.
14\times925=12\,950 kg
12\,950\div1000=12.95 t
(b) Can the loaded truck cross the bridge?
Total: 4.8+12.95=17.75 t
17.75 t < 18 t
Yes, the truck can cross.
(c) Could the driver add one more pallet? Explain.
One more: 925 kg =0.925 t
17.75+0.925=18.675 t
No. That is over the 18 t limit.
!
Watch out. Change to the same unit before you add or compare. Adding 4.8 t and 12 950 kg as “4.8 + 12 950” makes no sense.

Loading the truck, pallet by pallet

Load the pallets one at a time and watch the total grow in tonnes. The bridge sign is the limit.

Example 5

The deli and the vitamins

(a) Sliced turkey costs $2.38 per 100 g at the deli. Find the price per kilogram, and the cost of 350 g.
1 kg = 1000 g = 10 hundreds
Per kg: 10\times\$2.38=\$23.80
350 g = 3.5 hundreds: 3.5\times\$2.38=\$8.33
It costs $23.80 per kg, and 350 g costs $8.33.
(b) Each vitamin C tablet has 500 mg of vitamin C. A health guide says an adult should take no more than 2 g a day. What is the most tablets a day?
Same unit first: 2 g =2\times1000=2000 mg
2000\div500=4
The most is 4 tablets a day.

Converting Imperial Units of Mass

Imperial units are not based on 10. You have to remember the numbers:

Key idea   ton → lb: × 2000  ·  lb → oz: × 16    (going back up: ÷ 2000, ÷ 16)
1 ton = 2000 pounds (lb)1 pound (lb) = 16 ounces (oz)
Bigger unit → smaller unit: multiply.    Smaller unit → bigger unit: divide.

Masses are often written in pounds and ounces, like 3 lb 7 oz. To change it to ounces, change the pounds to ounces and add the extra ounces: 3\times16+7=55 oz.

Ounces to pounds and ounces. Step 1: divide by 16; the whole number is the pounds. Step 2: the ounces left over are the decimal part × 16 (or subtract the ounces in those pounds).

70 oz: 70 ÷ 16 ENTER shows 4.375, so 4 lb. Then − 4 ENTER shows 0.375, and × 16 ENTER shows 6. So 70 oz = 4 lb 6 oz. (Check: 4\times16+6=70.)
!
Watch out. The decimal part of a pound is not ounces. 1.5 lb is not 1 lb 5 oz. Half a pound is 8 oz, so 1.5 lb = 1 lb 8 oz.

1.5 lb is not 1 lb 5 oz

A pound is cut into 16 ounces, not 10. So the digits after the decimal point are not ounces.

Think Without a calculator: what is 1.25 lb in pounds and ounces? (Hint: a quarter of 16.)
A quarter of a pound is 16\div4=4 oz, so 1.25 lb = 1 lb 4 oz.

Working with Pounds and Ounces

To add or subtract masses in pounds and ounces, line up pounds under pounds and ounces under ounces, like adding hours and minutes.

Key idea
Adding: add the ounces, add the pounds. If you have 16 oz or more, trade 16 oz for 1 lb.
Subtracting: if there are not enough ounces on top, borrow 1 lb and turn it into 16 oz.
Multiplying: multiply the pounds and the ounces, then trade every 16 oz for 1 lb.
Example 8

Add, subtract, multiply

(a) 5 lb 12 oz + 3 lb 9 oz
8 lb 21 oz
21 oz = 16 + 5 = 1 lb 5 oz
Answer: 9 lb 5 oz
(b) 7 lb 3 oz − 2 lb 10 oz
3 oz is less than 10 oz, so borrow:
7 lb 3 oz = 6 lb 19 oz
6 lb 19 oz − 2 lb 10 oz
Answer: 4 lb 9 oz
(c) 4\times(1\text{ lb }11\text{ oz})
4\times1=4 lb, 4\times11=44 oz → 4 lb 44 oz
44\div16=2.75, so 2 lb and 44-32=12 oz
Answer: 6 lb 12 oz
Example 9

The backpack and the puppy

(a) Owen is packing for an overnight hike. His tent is 4 lb 9 oz, his sleeping bag is 2 lb 14 oz, his stove and pot are 1 lb 3 oz, and his food is 3 lb 11 oz. He wants the total under 12 lb. Is it? By how much is he over or under?
Pounds: 4+2+1+3=10 lb
Ounces: 9+14+3+11=37 oz
37 oz = 32 + 5 = 2 lb 5 oz
Total: 12 lb 5 oz
No. He is 5 oz over 12 lb.
(b) A puppy named Bear weighed 6 lb 13 oz at 8 weeks old. At 12 weeks he weighed 11 lb 4 oz. How much did he gain?
4 oz is less than 13 oz, so borrow:
11 lb 4 oz = 10 lb 20 oz
10-6=4 lb, 20-13=7 oz
Bear gained 4 lb 7 oz.

Prices by the pound or ounce

To compare sizes, change both to the same unit (usually ounces), then find the unit price, just like in Unit 1. Sometimes part of what you buy is thrown away (peel, bones, fat). The true cost per pound is what you paid ÷ the pounds you can actually use.

Key idea   True cost per pound = total price paid ÷ usable pounds
Change pounds and ounces to decimal pounds first: 9 oz =9\div16=0.5625 lb
Example 10

Coffee and a watermelon

(a) A 12 oz bag of coffee costs $10.99. A 2 lb bag of the same coffee costs $26.99. Which is the better buy?
2 lb =2\times16=32 oz
12 oz: \$10.99\div12\approx\$0.916 per oz
32 oz: \$26.99\div32\approx\$0.843 per oz
The 2 lb bag is the better buy.
(b) Nadia buys a watermelon that weighs 9 lb 4 oz, at $0.80 per lb. After she cuts off the rind, she has 5 lb 9 oz of fruit. What did she pay, and what is her true cost per pound of fruit?
9 lb 4 oz =9+4\div16=9.25 lb
Paid: 9.25\times\$0.80=\$7.40
Fruit: 5 lb 9 oz =5+9\div16=5.5625 lb
True cost: \$7.40\div5.5625\approx\$1.33 per lb
She paid $7.40, a true cost of about $1.33 per lb of fruit.

Reading Scales

A scale measures mass. To read a scale with a dial or a pointer:

Step 1Find two numbers next to each other and their difference.
Step 2Count the spaces (not the lines) between them. One space is worth difference ÷ spaces.
Step 3Start at the last number before the pointer and count on.

A digital scale shows the number for you. Its TARE (or ZERO) button sets the scale to 0 with a bowl on it, so you weigh only what you put in the bowl. Without it, subtract: mass of food = total − mass of the bowl.

Example 11

Read each scale

(a) Kitchen scale.

One small space is worth 20 g

It reads 340 g

From 0 to 100 there are 5 spaces: 100\div5=20 g each. 300+2\times20=340 g.
(b) Bathroom scale (pounds).

One small space: 1 lb   Reading: 143 lb

10 spaces from 120 to 130, so 1 lb each; 3 past 140 is 143 lb.
(c) Produce scale (pounds).

One small space: 2 oz   Reading: 1 lb 10 oz

8 spaces in 1 lb: 16\div8=2 oz each. The pointer is 5 spaces past 1:
5\times2=10 oz, so 1 lb 10 oz.
(d) Jae puts a mixing bowl full of flour on a digital scale. It shows 1.24 kg. The empty bowl has a mass of 315 g. How much flour is in the bowl?
Same unit: 1.24 kg =1240 g  ·  1240-315=925 g
There are 925 g of flour in the bowl. (Next time, he could press TARE with the empty bowl on.)
✓

Key ideas

Referents: paper clip ≈ 1 g, toonie ≈ 7 g, 1 L of water ≈ 1 kg, slice of bread ≈ 1 oz, butter = 1 lb. A tonne (1000 kg) is not a ton (2000 lb).
SI: t → kg → g → mg, × 1000 each jump down, ÷ 1000 each jump up. Same unit before you add or compare.
Imperial: 1 ton = 2000 lb, 1 lb = 16 oz. Ounces → lb oz: ÷ 16, then the leftover ounces. 1.5 lb = 1 lb 8 oz. Trade 16 oz for 1 lb.
True cost = price paid ÷ usable pounds. Read a scale: difference ÷ spaces, then count on. TARE, or subtract the bowl.
