The Two Jugs

You have a 3 L jug, a 5 L jug and a tap. The jugs have no lines on them.

You can fill a jug, empty it, or pour from one jug into the other.

How can you end up with exactly 4 L of water?

?
Found a way? Count your steps. Can anyone do it in fewer?
One way (6 steps): ① Fill the 5 L jug. ② Pour it into the 3 L jug until that is full: 2 L stay in the 5 L jug. ③ Empty the 3 L jug. ④ Pour the 2 L into the 3 L jug. ⑤ Fill the 5 L jug again. ⑥ Top up the 3 L jug: it only takes 1 L. Now the 5 L jug holds 5-1=4 L! Another way: fill the 3 L jug twice into the 5 L jug (1 L is left over), empty the 5 L jug, pour in the 1 L, then add one more full 3 L jug: 1+3=4.

Capacity and Volume: Millilitres, Litres and Cubic Centimetres

Volume is the amount of space an object takes up. Capacity is the amount a container can hold, usually of a liquid like water, milk or gas. A jug’s capacity is the volume of the space inside it.

In the SI (metric) system, capacity is measured in litres (L) and millilitres (mL). “Milli” means one thousandth, so 1000 mL = 1 L. Capacity and volume are linked: a cube 1 cm on each side holds exactly 1 mL, and a cube 10 cm on each side holds exactly 1 L.

Key idea   1 mL = 1 cm³   ·   1 L = 1000 mL = 1000 cm³   ·   1 m³ = 1000 L
L → mL: × 1000    mL → L: ÷ 1000     m³ → L: × 1000    L → m³: ÷ 1000

× 1000 or ÷ 1000: the decimal point moves 3 places

Multiplying by 1000 moves the decimal point 3 places to the right. Dividing by 1000 moves it 3 places to the left.

A stock tank near Brooks

A rancher near Brooks has a round stock tank for her cattle. It is a cylinder with a diameter of 2.4 m and a height of 0.6 m.

Imperial Capacity: Fluid Ounces, Cups, Pints, Quarts and Gallons

Canada uses litres, but you still see imperial capacity units on US recipes, on drinks bottled for the US, at gas stations in the US, and on older tools and machines. The imperial units, from smallest to largest, are the fluid ounce (fl oz), the cup, the pint (pt), the quart (qt) and the gallon (gal).

Key idea   US units:   1 gal = 4 qt  ·  1 qt = 2 pt  ·  1 pt = 2 cups  ·  1 cup = 8 fl oz
So 1 gal = 4 qt = 8 pt = 16 cups = 128 fl oz.   Bigger unit → smaller unit: multiply.   Smaller unit → bigger unit: divide.
!
Watch out. A fluid ounce measures capacity (how much liquid). An ounce (oz) on a scale measures mass. They are different units (mass is Lesson 4).

US Gallons vs British Gallons

Here is the tricky part: the word “gallon” means two different sizes. The US gallon is used in the United States. The British gallon (also called the imperial gallon) was used in Britain and in Canada before Canada changed to metric in the 1970s. You still see it on older Canadian farm equipment, boats and recipes.

Both systems say 1 gallon = 4 quarts = 8 pints. But a British pint holds 20 British fluid ounces, while a US pint holds 16 US fluid ounces. So the British pint, quart and gallon are all about 20% bigger than the US ones.

Converting Between SI and Imperial Capacity

To change a capacity between the two systems, multiply or divide by the matching number. Write the units in each step so you can see which way to go. A gallon is bigger than a litre (1 US gal ≈ 3.785 L), and a fluid ounce is bigger than a millilitre (1 US fl oz ≈ 29.57 mL). So going to L or mL you get more units: multiply. Going back to gallons or fluid ounces you get fewer units: divide.

Key idea
US gal → L: × 3.785L → US gal: ÷ 3.785 US fl oz → mL: × 29.57mL → US fl oz: ÷ 29.57 British gal → L: × 4.546L → British gal: ÷ 4.546
≈
Estimate first. A US gallon is a bit less than 4 L. A British gallon is about 4.5 L. A US fluid ounce is about 30 mL. If your answer is far from the estimate, you probably multiplied when you should have divided.
Think Which is more: 1 US quart or 1 litre?
1 US quart is a quarter of a gallon: 3.785\div4\approx0.946 L. So 1 litre is a little more.

Kitchen measures

Canadian measuring cups and spoons are metric. American recipes use US cups. The two cups are close but not the same.

Key idea   Canadian (metric) kitchen: 1 teaspoon (tsp) = 5 mL  ·  1 tablespoon (tbsp) = 15 mL = 3 tsp  ·  1 cup = 250 mL
US recipes: 1 US cup = 8 US fl oz ≈ 237 mL

Key ideas

Capacity is how much a container holds. 1 mL = 1 cm³, 1 L = 1000 mL = 1000 cm³, 1 m³ = 1000 L. Tank: volume inside, then change to litres.
US units: 1 gal = 4 qt, 1 qt = 2 pt, 1 pt = 2 cups, 1 cup = 8 fl oz (1 gal = 128 fl oz). Big → small: multiply. Small → big: divide.
Two gallons: US ≈ 3.785 L, British ≈ 4.546 L (about 20% bigger). A “gallon” is US unless it says British.
Converting: estimate first (US gal ≈ 4 L, fl oz ≈ 30 mL). Gal → L: × 3.785. L → gal: ÷ 3.785. Fl oz → mL: × 29.57. Kitchen: 1 tsp = 5 mL, 1 tbsp = 15 mL, metric cup 250 mL, US cup ≈ 237 mL.
