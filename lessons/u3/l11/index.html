Roll It Two Ways

Take one sheet of paper, 8½ in by 11 in. Roll it into a tube and tape the edges. You can roll it two ways: a tall, thin tube or a short, fat tube.

Stand each tube on a table and fill it to the top with popcorn.

Which tube holds more popcorn: the tall one, the short one, or are they the same?

?
Pick one: tall, short or the same. Tell the person beside you why.
The short, fat tube holds more, about 30% more! The tall tube holds about 63 in³ and the short tube about 82 in³. Both tubes use the same paper (8.5\times11=93.5 in² around the side), but a wider circle holds a lot more inside. Today we find how much material covers a can, a tent or a cone. Same paper does not mean same space inside.

Surface Area of Cylinders, Pyramids and Cones

How much metal makes a can? How much fabric is in a tent, or steel in a grain bin? Today we unroll a cylinder, unfold a pyramid and flatten a cone. Then we add up the areas of the pieces.

I can…
  • draw the net of a cylinder (2 circles and a rectangle) and find its surface area
  • use only the pieces that are there: a pail has no lid, a pipe has no ends
  • find the surface area of a square pyramid with the slant height
  • find the surface area of a cone: base \pi r^2 plus side \pi rs
  • add the parts of an object made of two solids, and find the cost of the material
M4 Solve problems that involve SI and imperial area measurements of regular, composite and irregular 2-D shapes and 3-D objects, including decimal and fractional measurements, and verify the solutions.
1

The Net of a Cylinder

A cylinder has two circles of the same size (the top and the bottom) and one curved side. Think of a can of soup. Peel off the label and lay it flat: it is a rectangle. So the net of a cylinder is one rectangle and two circles.

  • The rectangle’s width is the height of the cylinder, h.
  • The rectangle’s length wraps once around the can, so it is the circumference of the circle: C=2\pi r (or \pi d).

The surface area (SA) of a solid is the total area of all the pieces of its net. It is in square units.

Key idea
Each circle: A=\pi r^2
Curved side (the label): A=\text{length}\times\text{width}=2\pi r\times h=2\pi rh
Closed cylinder:   \text{SA}=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh   (two circles + the side)
Find each part, write it down, then add. Keep all the decimals until the end. For r=4 and h=11: 2 × π × 4 x² ENTER gives 100.53…, and 2 × π × 4 × 11 ENTER gives 276.46…
!
Watch out. If you are given the diameter, halve it to get the radius. And 2\pi r^2 means 2\times\pi\times r\times r: only the r is squared.
Think A can is 8 cm across and 11 cm tall. Which is longer: the label, or the can’s height?
The label. It goes all the way around: \pi\times8\approx25.1 cm. That is more than 3 times the 8 cm width of the can, and much longer than the 11 cm height.

Why is the label’s length the circumference? Roll the can

Put wet paint on the label and roll the can one full turn. The stripe it prints is the label, laid flat.

Example 1

A can of soup

A can of soup is 8 cm across (its diameter) and 11 cm tall.

(a) Fill in the measurements of the net.

The rectangle is \pi\times8\approx25.1 cm long and 11 cm wide.
Each circle has a radius of 4 cm.

Length =\pi d=\pi\times8\approx25.1 cm; width = the height, 11 cm; radius =8\div2=4 cm.
(b) Find the area of the label (the curved side), to the nearest tenth.
Side =2\pi rh (or \pi d\times h)
=2\times\pi\times4\times11=276.46\ldots
The label is about 276.5 cm².
(c) Find the surface area of the whole can, to the nearest tenth.
Two circles =2\times\pi\times4^2=100.53\ldots
\text{SA}=100.53\ldots+276.46\ldots=376.99\ldots
The can’s surface area is about 377.0 cm².
Example 2

Rhys paints a rain barrel

Rhys is painting the outside of an old steel drum (with both ends) to use as a rain barrel. The drum has a diameter of 22\tfrac12 in and a height of 34\tfrac12 in.

(a) Find the surface area in square inches, to the nearest whole number.
r=22.5\div2=11.25 in; h=34.5 in
Circles: 2\times\pi\times11.25^2=795.21\ldots
Side: 2\times\pi\times11.25\times34.5=2\,438.66\ldots
\text{SA}=795.21\ldots+2\,438.66\ldots=3\,233.87\ldots
The drum is about 3 234 in².
(b) Change the area to square feet, to the nearest tenth. (1 ft² = 144 in²)
Small unit to big unit: divide.
3\,233.87\ldots\div144=22.457\ldots
The drum is about 22.5 ft².
(c) One can of spray paint covers about 20 ft². How many cans does Rhys need for one coat?
22.46\div20=1.12\ldots cans. Round up: Rhys needs 2 cans.
Think 1.12 is closer to 1 than to 2. Why does Rhys still buy 2 cans?
One can covers only 20 ft². With 1 can, about 22.46-20\approx2.5 ft² of the drum would not get painted. You can’t buy part of a can, so round up.

Open Cylinders: Use Only the Parts You Need

Not every cylinder has two ends. A pipe has no ends. A pail has a bottom but no top. Before you calculate, ask: which pieces are really there, or which pieces really get covered?

Key idea
Closed (a can with a lid, a drum)2 circles + side\text{SA}=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh
Open at one end (a pail, a planter, a stock tank)1 circle + side\text{SA}=\pi r^2+2\pi rh
No ends (a pipe, a label, the walls of a grain bin)side only\text{SA}=2\pi rh
Example 3

Which pieces are there?

(a) Which pieces of the net does each object need? Write “2 circles + side”, “1 circle + side” or “side only”.
a tube of tennis balls with a lid 2 circles + side
a metal drainpipe side only
a round cake pan 1 circle + side
the paper label on a jar side only
Tennis ball tube: 2 circles + side · drainpipe: side only · cake pan: 1 circle + side (no top) · jar label: side only
(b) Amara is covering a round storage ottoman with fabric. The fabric covers the top and the side, but not the bottom. The ottoman has a diameter of 18 in and a height of 16\tfrac12 in. How much fabric covers it, in in² (to the nearest whole number) and in ft² (to the nearest tenth)?
Pieces: 1 circle (the top) + the side. r=18\div2=9 in
Top: \pi\times9^2=254.46\ldots   Side: 2\times\pi\times9\times16.5=933.05\ldots
\text{SA}=254.46\ldots+933.05\ldots=1\,187.52\ldots\approx1\,188 in²
1\,187.52\ldots\div144=8.246\ldots\approx8.2 ft². The fabric covers about 1 188 in², or 8.2 ft².
Example 4

Painting parkade columns

A parkade in Calgary has 12 round concrete columns. Each column has a diameter of 0.6 m and a height of 3.2 m. Only the curved sides get painted, with 2 coats. One can of paint covers 37 m² and costs $68.99.

(a) Find the area of the side of one column.
Side only: 2\pi rh (r=0.3 m)
2\times\pi\times0.3\times3.2=6.031\ldots m²
(b) Find the total area to paint (12 columns, 2 coats).
6.031\ldots\times12\times2=144.76\ldots
About 144.8 m² of painting.
(c) How many cans of paint are needed?
144.76\ldots\div37=3.91\ldots cans
Round up: 4 cans.
(d) What do the cans cost with 5% GST?
4\times\$68.99=\$275.96
\$275.96\times1.05=\$289.758\approx\$289.76
The paint costs $289.76 with GST.

Math Historyc. 225 BCE

The ball and the can on Archimedes’ grave

About 2 250 years ago, the Greek thinker Archimedes fit a ball snugly inside a cylinder (a can just as tall and as wide as the ball). He proved something amazing: the ball’s surface has exactly the same area as the can’s curved side, the label!

He was so proud of it that he asked for a ball inside a cylinder to be carved on his tomb. In 75 BCE, the Roman writer Cicero found the tomb in Sicily, hidden in thorn bushes. He knew it by the ball and the cylinder.

A ball with a radius of 3 cm fits snugly in a can 6 cm wide and 6 cm tall. Use the label to find the area of the ball’s surface.
Label =2\pi rh=2\times\pi\times3\times6=113.09\ldots So the ball’s surface is about 113.1 cm².

Square Pyramids

A square pyramid has a square base and four triangles that meet at a point at the top, called the apex. In this booklet the four triangles are always the same size. Unfold it and the net is a square with a triangle on each side.

The slant height s is the height of one triangle face. It goes from the middle of a base edge, up the face, to the apex. The height h of the pyramid goes straight up the inside, from the centre of the base to the apex. Surface area uses the slant height, not the height.

Key idea
Base: b\times b=b^2
One triangle: \tfrac12\times b\times s
Square pyramid:   \text{SA}=b^2+4\times\tfrac12bs   (base + 4 triangles; this is the same as b^2+2bs)
A roof, or a tent with no floor, has no base: use only the 4 triangles.
!
Watch out. Use the slant height s, not the height h. The slant height is on the outside of the face, and it is always longer than the height. If a picture shows both, the height is the dashed line inside.

Why is the slant height always longer than the height?

Cut the solid straight down the middle. The height, half the base and the slant height make a right triangle.

Example 5

A pyramid and Jonah’s tent

(a) Find the surface area of the square pyramid. Which length is not used?
Base: 10\times10=100 cm²
One triangle: \tfrac12\times10\times13=65 cm²
4 triangles: 4\times65=260 cm²
\text{SA}=100+260=360 cm². The height, 12 cm, is not used.
(b) Jonah’s camping tent is a square pyramid with a sewn-in floor. The floor is 7 ft by 7 ft, and the slant height of each wall is 6\tfrac12 ft. How much fabric is in the tent (4 walls and the floor)?
One wall: \tfrac12\times7\times6\tfrac12=22\tfrac34 ft² (22.75)
4 walls: 4\times22.75=91 ft²   Floor: 7\times7=49 ft²
Total: 91+49=140 ft². The tent has 140 ft² of fabric.
4

Cones

A cone has a circle for a base and a curved side that comes to a point, the apex. The slant height s goes from the apex, down the outside, to the edge of the base.

Cut the curved side along a slant line and flatten it: you get a fan shape. The fan’s curved edge goes all the way around the base, so it is 2\pi r long. Its area is \pi rs.

Key idea
Base: \pi r^2
Curved side: \pi rs
Closed cone:   \text{SA}=\pi r^2+\pi rs
An open cone (an ice-cream cone, a funnel, a party hat, a roof) has no base: use only \pi rs.
For r=9 and s=15: π × 9 x² ENTER gives 254.46… (base), and π × 9 × 15 ENTER gives 424.11… (side).
!
Watch out. Just like the pyramid, use the slant height s, not the height h. And use the radius: if you are given the diameter, halve it first.

Why is the fan’s area \pi rs? Cut it into thin slices

Think of the fan as many thin triangles. Each one has a height of s, and all their bases together go around the circle, 2\pi r. Put the slices top to tail and they make a shape very close to a rectangle.

Example 6

A cone and a waffle cone

Round each answer to the nearest tenth.

(a) Find the surface area of the closed cone (radius 9 cm, slant height 15 cm, height 12 cm).
Base: \pi\times9^2=254.46\ldots
Side: \pi\times9\times15=424.11\ldots
\text{SA}=254.46\ldots+424.11\ldots=678.58\ldots
The cone is about 678.6 cm². The 12 cm height is not used.
(b) A waffle cone for ice cream is open at the top. It is 5 cm across the top and has a slant height of 12 cm. How much waffle is in it?
Open: side only. r=5\div2=2.5 cm
Side: \pi rs=\pi\times2.5\times12=94.24\ldots
The cone has about 94.2 cm² of waffle.

5

Two Solids Together

Some objects are made of two solids, like a grain bin with a cone roof. Find the area of each part you need, then add. Leave out the faces that are hidden or not there, like the floor of a bin that sits on concrete.

Example 7

A steel grain bin near Three Hills

A farmer near Three Hills is building a steel grain bin. The walls are a cylinder with a diameter of 6.4 m and a height of 5.5 m. The roof is a cone with a slant height of 3.6 m. The floor is concrete, so it needs no steel.

(a) Find the area of the walls.
Walls: side only. r=6.4\div2=3.2 m
2\times\pi\times3.2\times5.5=110.58\ldots m²
(b) Find the area of the roof.
Roof: open cone, \pi rs
\pi\times3.2\times3.6=36.19\ldots m²
(c) Find the total area of steel, to the nearest tenth.
110.58\ldots+36.19\ldots=146.77\ldots
About 146.8 m² of steel.
(d) The steel costs $24.80 per m². Use your answer to (c) to find the cost before tax.
146.8\times\$24.80=\$3\,640.64
The steel costs $3 640.64 before tax.

Key ideas

Surface area = the total area of all the pieces of the net, in square units. Find each piece, write it down, then add. Round only at the end.
Cylinder: 2 circles \pi r^2 + a rectangle 2\pi r\times h. Closed: 2\pi r^2+2\pi rh. Use only the pieces that are there: pail \pi r^2+2\pi rh, pipe 2\pi rh.
Square pyramid: b^2+4\times\tfrac12bs. Cone: \pi r^2+\pi rs. Use the slant height s, not the height. A roof or an open cone has no base.
On the job: halve the diameter, add only the parts you need, change units (÷ 144 for in² to ft²), round up cans and bundles, then find the cost.
