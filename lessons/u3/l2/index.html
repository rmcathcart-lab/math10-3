The Palindrome Odometer

Maya is driving to Banff. Her odometer shows 15951 km. That number is a palindrome: it reads the same forwards and backwards.

Exactly 2 hours later, the odometer shows the very next palindrome.

How fast was Maya driving?

?
Lots of people guess 15961. Is that really a palindrome?
No: 15961 backwards is 16951. The middle digit is already 9, so it can’t go up. The next palindrome is 16061 km. Maya drove 16061-15951=110 km in 2 h, so she was driving 55 km/h (about the speed limit on a small highway). Today: kilometres, metres, and all the other SI units.

SI Units: What We Measure and With What

The SI (short for Système International, French for “International System”) is the metric system. It is the official measurement system in Canada and in most of the world. Each kind of measurement has one base unit. Bigger and smaller units are made from it by multiplying or dividing by 10, 100, 1000, …, just like place value.

Masshow heavy something isgram (g)milligram (mg), kilogram (kg), tonne (t)
Capacityhow much a container holdslitre (L)millilitre (mL)

How the SI units fit together
  • Water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C. 1 tonne (t) = 1000 kg.
  • A cube 1 cm on each side has a volume of 1 cm³. It holds exactly 1 mL, and 1 mL of water has a mass of about 1 g.
  • So 1 L of water has a mass of about 1 kg. (Area and volume units come later in this unit and in Unit 4.)

The Prefixes

A prefix is a word part at the front of a unit. It tells you how many base units the unit is worth. The same prefixes work for metres, grams and litres.

Prefixkilohectodeca(base unit)decicentimilli
Symbolkhdam, g, Ldcm

Example1 km = 1000 m1 hm = 100 m1 dam = 10 m1 m1 dm = 0.1 m1 cm = 0.01 m1 mm = 0.001 m

Read the bottom row the other way, too: 1 m = 10 dm = 100 cm = 1000 mm. A memory trick for the order: King Henry Died By Drinking Chocolate Milk (kilo, hecto, deca, Base, deci, centi, milli).

The units you will use most
1 km = 1000 m 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm 1 cm = 10 mm
1 kg = 1000 g 1 g = 1000 mg 1 L = 1000 mL 1 t = 1000 kg
!
The letter m does two jobs. At the end of a symbol it means metre; at the front of a two-letter symbol it means milli. So mm is a millimetre, mg a milligram, mL a millilitre. Write litre with a capital L, write km with a small k, never add an “s” to a symbol, and leave a space: 5 km, not 5km or 5 kms.

Math History1791 – 1983

A metre measured from the North Pole

In 1791, French scientists wanted a unit that belonged to everyone. They chose a piece of the Earth: one metre would be one ten-millionth of the distance from the North Pole to the equator, along a line through Paris.

Starting in 1792, the astronomers Delambre and Méchain measured part of that line, from Dunkirk in France to Barcelona in Spain. In 1799 a platinum bar one metre long was made and kept in the French archives. Their measuring was a little off: the bar is about 0.2 mm too short. Since 1983, the metre is defined by the speed of light.

If a metre is \tfrac{1}{10\,000\,000} of the way from the North Pole to the equator, how many kilometres is that trip?
The trip is 10 000 000 m. Metres to kilometres is up 3 steps, so ÷\,1000: 10 000 km. (Because the bar came out a little short, the true distance is about 10 002 km.)

Referents and Choosing a Sensible Unit

A referent is a familiar object that is about the size of a unit. A referent helps you picture a unit and estimate a measurement without a ruler or a scale.

Choosing a sensible unit

To choose a sensible unit, pick the unit that gives an easy number, not a huge one or a tiny one. The height of a door is about 2 m. Saying 2000 mm or 0.002 km is correct but not sensible.

Length: mm for very small things (a screw, the thickness of glass); cm for things you hold (a book, a phone); m for rooms, buildings and fields; km for distances between places.
Mass: mg for medicine and vitamins; g for small things and food packages; kg for people, pets and big bags; t for trucks and heavy loads.
Capacity: mL for small containers and spoonfuls; L for jugs, tanks and pools.

Example 4

Estimating with referents

Use a referent to estimate.

(a) Tae’s smallest fingernail is about 1 cm wide. A pencil is about 17 fingernails long. About how long is the pencil?
17\times1\text{ cm}=17\text{ cm}
The pencil is about 17 cm long.
(b) A door is about 2 m tall. The gym wall is about 4 doors tall. About how tall is the wall?
4\times2\text{ m}=8\text{ m}
The wall is about 8 m tall.
(c) A paper clip has a mass of about 1 g. About what is the mass of 250 paper clips?
250\times1\text{ g}=250\text{ g}
About 250 g (that is a quarter of a kilogram).
(d) A milk carton holds 1 L. A soup pot holds about as much as 6 cartons. About how much does the pot hold?
6\times1\text{ L}=6\text{ L}
The pot holds about 6 L.

Measuring With a Metric Ruler or Tape

On a metric ruler the numbers are centimetres. Each centimetre is split into 10 small spaces. Each small space is 1 millimetre. The middle mark, a bit longer, is at 5 mm (half a centimetre).

  1. Line up one end of the object with the 0 mark (not the end of the ruler).
  2. Read the last full centimetre before the other end.
  3. Count the millimetre marks past it. 6 cm and 4 mm is 6.4 cm or 64 mm.
If the object does not start at 0, subtract: length = end reading − start reading.

A metric tape measure works the same way but is longer. Most tapes number every centimetre, so the mark 126 means 126 cm, which is 1 m 26 cm or 1.26 m.

Changing Units: Moving the Decimal Point

SI units are based on 10, so to change units you multiply or divide by 10, 100 or 1000. That just moves the decimal point. Use the staircase.

  1. Find the unit you have and the unit you want on the staircase.
  2. Count the steps between them, and see if you go down or up.
  3. Down: multiply (move the decimal point right one place per step). Up: divide (move it left). Fill empty places with zeros.
For example, metres to centimetres is 2 steps down, so \times100: 3.45 m → 34.5 → 345 cm.
Smaller unit ⇒ bigger number. Bigger unit ⇒ smaller number.
Check every answer: 3.45 m = 345 cm makes sense, because it takes many small centimetres to make a few metres.
Watch out: count every step, even the ones you do not use much. From m to mm is 3 steps (m, dm, cm, mm), so \times1000, not \times100.
On the TI-30XIIS: 3.45×100ENTER shows 345. 850÷1000ENTER shows 0.85.

Comparing measurements

To compare measurements in different units, change them to the same unit first.

Example 7

Which is more?

(a) Which is longer, 0.8 m or 75 cm?
0.8 m =0.8\times100=80 cm
80 cm is more than 75 cm. 0.8 m is longer.
(b) Which holds more: one 1.5 L bottle of pop, or four 355 mL cans?
1.5 L =1.5\times1000=1500 mL
Cans: 4\times355=1420 mL
The 1.5 L bottle holds more (80 mL more).
(c) Four friends did the long jump. Sana jumped 4.35 m, Theo 428 cm, Uma 4410 mm and Vic 0.0043 km. Put the jumps in order from shortest to longest. Who won?
Change all to cm: Sana 4.35\times100=435 cm, Theo 428 cm, Uma 4410\div10=441 cm, Vic 0.0043\times100\,000=430 cm
(For Vic: km to m is \times1000, so 4.3 m; then m to cm is \times100, so 430 cm.)
Shortest to longest: Theo (428 cm), Vic (430 cm), Sana (435 cm), Uma (441 cm). Uma won.

Problems with units

In a word problem, change everything to one unit before you multiply, divide, add or subtract. Then answer in the unit the question asks for.

Example 8

Lemonade and laps

(a) Marisol is selling lemonade at a fundraiser. Each cup holds 250 mL. A jug holds 4 L.
(i) How many cups can she fill from one jug?
(ii) How many jugs does she need to make to fill 50 cups?
(i) 4 L = 4000 mL; 4000\div250=16 cups
(ii) 50\times250=12\,500 mL = 12.5 L; 12.5\div4=3.125 jugs
3 jugs are not enough, so she needs 4 jugs.
(b) Owen runs 3 laps of a 400 m track. Then he runs 1.8 km on the road. How far does he run in all, in kilometres?
Track: 3\times400=1200 m
1200 m =1200\div1000=1.2 km
Total: 1.2+1.8=3.0 km
Owen runs 3 km.

Key ideas

SI base units: metre (m) for length, gram (g) for mass, litre (L) for capacity, degree Celsius (°C) for temperature. 1 L of water ≈ 1 kg.
Prefixes: kilo 1000, hecto 100, deca 10, base 1, deci 0.1, centi 0.01, milli 0.001. 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm.
Referents help you estimate: dime ≈ 1 mm, fingernail ≈ 1 cm, door ≈ 2 m, paper clip ≈ 1 g, milk carton ≈ 1 L ≈ 1 kg. Choose the unit that gives an easy number.
Staircase: down = × 10 per step (point moves right), up = ÷ 10 per step (point moves left). Smaller unit ⇒ bigger number. Compare in the same unit.
