Arm Span vs Height

Stretch your arms out wide. The distance from the tip of one middle finger to the other is your arm span.

For most people, is the arm span shorter than their height, about the same, or longer?

A shorter than their height
B about the same as their height
C longer than their height
B, about the same! For most people, the arm span is within a few centimetres of their height. Artists have used this for hundreds of years: Leonardo da Vinci’s famous drawing of a man in a circle and a square shows it. Check it with a partner and a tape measure. Old units came from the body too: your thumb is about 1 inch wide.

A TV is 55 inches. A form asks for your height in centimetres. Every road sign in the United States is in miles. Today we change inches, feet, yards and miles to centimetres, metres and kilometres and back: in our heads first, then exactly.

The Conversion Facts and Quick Estimates

Canada uses the SI (metric) system, but imperial units are still all around us: lumber and plywood, people’s heights, TV and tire sizes, and every road sign in the United States. To change a length from one system to the other, you use a conversion fact. The inch fact is exact (and so are the foot and yard facts, which are built from it). The others are rounded, so we write ≈ (“is about”).

Key idea

1 in = 2.54 cm = 25.4 mm (exact)about 2.5 cmthe width of your thumb
1 ft = 30.48 cmabout 30 cma 30 cm ruler is just under 1 ft
1 yd = 0.9144 ma bit less than 1 mone long step
1 m ≈ 3.28 ft ≈ 39.37 ina bit more than 3 fta metre stick is about 3 in longer than a yardstick
1 mi ≈ 1.609 kmabout 1.6 kmabout 20 minutes of walking
1 km ≈ 0.621 miabout 0.6 mia bit more than half a mile
Try it: drag the red line along the ruler. The top edge is inches, the bottom edge is centimetres.
0 in = 0 cm

Where do the other facts come from?

Since 1959, the inch has been defined as exactly 2.54 cm. Every other fact is built from it.

1 ft = 12 in = 12\times2.54=30.48 cm (exact)
1 yd = 36 in = 36\times2.54=91.44 cm = 0.9144 m (exact)
1 m = 100 cm = 100\div2.54\approx39.37 in, and 39.37\div12\approx3.28 ft
1 mi = 5280 ft = 5280\times30.48=160\,934.4 cm ≈ 1.609 km
1 km = 1\div1.609\approx0.621 mi
Think Why do we write 1 ft = 30.48 cm, but 1 mi ≈ 1.609 km?
30.48 is the whole answer: it ends there. A mile is 1.609344 km, and we rounded it to 1.609. Rounded facts get ≈.

Which is longer? Change one length to the other’s unit

To compare a length in one system with a length in the other, change one of them so both are in the same unit. Then the bigger number is the longer length.

Think A metre is about 39.37 in. How many feet and inches is that?
3\times12=36, and 39.37-36=3.37. A metre is about 3 ft 3 in: a yardstick (3 ft) plus about 3 more inches.

Quick estimates in your head

Use the rounded facts.

Key idea

mi → km: × 1.6 (add six tenths of the number)50 mi: 50+30=80 km
km → mi: × 0.6 (six tenths of the number)100 km: about 60 mi

Converting with a Unit Factor or a Proportion

For an exact answer, multiply by a unit factor. A unit factor is a fraction made from a conversion fact, like \dfrac{2.54\text{ cm}}{1\text{ in}}. Its top and bottom are the same length, so it is equal to 1. Multiplying by it changes the unit but not the length.

Key idea
  1. Step 1. Write the length you have, with its unit.
  2. Step 2. Multiply by a unit factor. Put the unit you have on the bottom and the unit you want on the top.
  3. Step 3. Cross out the unit that is on the top and the bottom. Calculate, then round.
  4. Step 4. Check with a quick estimate, or convert your answer back.
9 in to centimetres: 9\ \cancel{\text{in}}\times\dfrac{2.54\text{ cm}}{1\ \cancel{\text{in}}}=9\times2.54=22.86\text{ cm}
Check: 9\times2.5=22.5, close.
60 cm to inches: 60\ \cancel{\text{cm}}\times\dfrac{1\text{ in}}{2.54\ \cancel{\text{cm}}}=60\div2.54\approx23.6\text{ in}
The 2.54 is on the bottom, so you divide.

You can also use a proportion, as in Unit 1. Keep the same units in the same places: \dfrac{1\text{ in}}{2.54\text{ cm}}=\dfrac{9\text{ in}}{x} gives x=9\times2.54=22.86 cm.

!
Watch out. Ask: should the number get bigger or smaller? A centimetre is shorter than an inch, so it takes more centimetres: 9 in is about 23 cm, not about 3.5 cm. In the same way, a kilometre is shorter than a mile, so a distance in km is a bigger number than the same distance in miles.
9 × 2.54 ENTER shows 22.86. 60 ÷ 2.54 ENTER shows 23.62204…, about 23.6.

If a fact gives centimetres but you want metres, convert inside SI at the end: 182.88 cm = 182.88\div100=1.8288 m.

Why does a unit factor work?

One inch and 2.54 cm are the same length, written two ways. So \dfrac{2.54\text{ cm}}{1\text{ in}} is like \dfrac{6}{6}: the top and the bottom are the same amount, and the fraction equals 1.

Multiplying by 1 never changes an amount. It only changes how we write it. And units cancel just like numbers: \dfrac{\cancel{\text{in}}\cdot\text{cm}}{\cancel{\text{in}}}=\text{cm}.

Think Lee wrote 9\text{ in}\times\dfrac{1\text{ in}}{2.54\text{ cm}}. What goes wrong?
The inches don’t cancel: they end up as in × in ÷ cm, which is not a length. The unit you have (in) must be on the bottom.

Bigger or smaller? Count the blocks

Line up inch blocks and centimetre blocks along the same length. Each inch is 2.54 cm, so it takes more centimetre blocks.

Example 3

The “2 by 4” that isn’t

A “2 by 4” from the lumber store is not really 2 in by 4 in. After it is dried and planed smooth, it is 1\tfrac12 in by 3\tfrac12 in.

(a) Use a proportion to change 3\tfrac12 in to centimetres.
3\tfrac12=3.5
\dfrac{1\text{ in}}{2.54\text{ cm}}=\dfrac{3.5\text{ in}}{x}
x=3.5\times2.54=8.89 cm
(b) Change 1\tfrac12 in to millimetres.
1\tfrac12=1.5;  1 in = 25.4 mm
1.5\times25.4=38.1 mm
(c) Check your answer to (a) by converting it back to inches.
8.89\div2.54=3.5 in = 3\tfrac12 in ✓
(d) Ravi’s metric plan needs a board at least 9 cm wide. Is the wide side of a 2 by 4 wide enough? Explain.
No. The board is 8.89 cm wide, which is 9-8.89=0.11 cm (about 1 mm) too narrow.

Heights and Lengths in Feet and Inches

A height like 5′ 10″ (5 ft 10 in) uses two units. Change it to one unit (inches) before you convert.

Key idea
Feet and inches → cm:
Step 1: inches = feet × 12 + inches.
Step 2: × 2.54.
cm → feet and inches:
Step 1: ÷ 2.54 to get inches (round).
Step 2: take out as many 12s as you can. They are the feet. What is left over is the inches.
Think Who is taller: Sam at 6 ft, or Lee at 1.8 m?
6 ft = 6\times30.48=182.88 cm, and 1.8 m = 180 cm. Sam is taller, by about 2.9 cm.
!
Watch out. 5′ 10″ is not 5.10 ft. There are 12 inches in a foot, not 10. On a calculator, 70\div12=5.833\ldots, and the 0.833 is part of a foot, not 83 inches. Instead: 5\times12=60,  70-60=10, so 70 in = 5′ 10″.

Will it fit? A clearance sign in feet and inches

Parkades, drive-throughs and bridges post a clearance: the greatest height that fits under. Signs often use feet and inches. Put both heights in the same unit, then compare.

Metric lengths on an inch tape

An inch tape measure shows sixteenths of an inch. To find a metric length on it, change to inches, then change the decimal part to sixteenths.

Key idea   Decimal inches → nearest \tfrac{1}{16} in: multiply the decimal part by 16, round to a whole number, write it over 16 and simplify.
e.g. 2.3 in:  0.3\times16=4.8\approx5,  so 2.3 in \approx2\tfrac{5}{16} in.
Think Why do we multiply the decimal part by 16?
The tape cuts each inch into 16 equal pieces. 0.75 of an inch is 0.75 of those 16 pieces: 0.75\times16=12 sixteenths, which is \tfrac{12}{16}=\tfrac34 in.

Distance, Speed and Price per Length

Road signs in the United States use miles, and speeds in miles per hour (mph). Canadian signs use km and km/h. To convert a speed, use the same fact as for distance; the “per hour” does not change: 60 mph = 60\times1.609\approx96.5 km/h.

Think Which is faster: 100 km/h or 60 mph?
60 mph = 60\times1.609\approx96.5 km/h. So 100 km/h is a little faster (by about 3.5 km/h).
Example 7

The Tran family drives to Montana

The Tran family drives from Calgary to Montana. Their car’s speedometer and odometer show only km/h and km. Round to the nearest whole number.

(a) The speed limit on the interstate highway is 80 mph. What is that in km/h?
80\times1.609=128.72
The limit is about 129 km/h.
(b) A sign says “Great Falls 87 mi”. How far is that in kilometres?
87\times1.609=139.983
Great Falls is about 140 km away.
(c) Near a town, the limit drops to 55 mph. Mr. Tran is driving at 95 km/h. Is he speeding? By how much?
55\times1.609=88.495\approx88.5 km/h
Yes. He is going about 95-88.5=6.5 km/h over the limit.
(d) At the sign in (b), the odometer read 48 312 km. What should it read at Great Falls?
48\,312+140=48\,452
It should read about 48 452 km.

Price per length

Fabric, rope, trim and chain are often sold by the foot or by the yard. To compare with a price per metre, find the price of one metre (a unit price, as in Unit 1).

Key idea   Price per metre = price ÷ length in metres
e.g. $12.49 per yd: 1 yd = 0.9144 m, so the price per metre is \$12.49\div0.9144\approx\$13.66
(For a price per foot, divide by 0.3048, because 1 ft = 30.48 cm = 0.3048 m.)
↑
Round up when you need enough. If you need at least a certain length, and the store sells it in steps (whole yards, quarter yards), round up to the next step. 2.73 yd means you must buy 2\tfrac34 yd: 2\tfrac12 yd would be too short.
Think A hardware store sells chain for $0.99 per foot. What is the price per metre?
1 ft = 0.3048 m, so \$0.99\div0.3048=3.248\ldots\approx\$3.25 per metre.
Example 8

Mei’s curtains

Key ideas

The facts: 1 in = 2.54 cm (exact), 1 ft = 30.48 cm, 1 yd = 0.9144 m, 1 mi ≈ 1.609 km, 1 km ≈ 0.621 mi. Estimate: in → cm double and add half; mi → km add six tenths; km → mi take six tenths.
Unit factor: the unit you have on the bottom, the unit you want on top, cross out, calculate. Check: more centimetres than inches; more kilometres than miles. Or convert back.
Feet and inches: change to inches first (feet × 12 + inches). 5′ 10″ is 70 in, not 5.10 ft. Back: ÷ 2.54, then take out the 12s. Inch tape: decimal part × 16.
Speeds and prices: mph → km/h is × 1.609 (the “per hour” stays). Price per metre = price ÷ length in metres. Round up when you must buy enough.
