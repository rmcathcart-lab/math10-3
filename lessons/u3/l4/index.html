Which Line Is Longer?

Here are two straight black lines.

The top line has arrowheads on its ends. The bottom line has tails on its ends.

Which black line is longer: the top one or the bottom one?

A the top line
B the bottom line
C they are the same length
C: they are exactly the same length! Look at the red guide lines. The tails make the bottom line look longer, and the arrowheads make the top line look shorter. This trick is called the Müller-Lyer illusion. Your eyes can fool you. That is why carpenters measure instead of guessing, and today we make sure the units do not fool us either.

Converting Length Within SI and Within Imperial

A plan says 4.2 m, but your tape is in centimetres. A board is 8 ft, but the pieces are 30 in. Today we change units: down and up the SI staircase, and with 12, 3, 1760 and 5280 in imperial. Then we add and subtract feet and inches and plan cuts from lumber.

Converting Within SI: The Staircase

Every SI unit of length is the metre with a prefix in front. On the staircase, each step is 10 times as big as the step below it. To convert (change) a length to another unit, count the steps.

Key idea   Going down to a smaller unit: multiply by 10 for each step.   Going up to a bigger unit: divide by 10 for each step.
1 km = 1000 m   ·   1 m = 100 cm   ·   1 m = 1000 mm   ·   1 cm = 10 mm
!
Watch out. A smaller unit means a bigger number: 4.2 m is 420 cm, not 0.042 cm. Before you finish, ask: “Should my answer be bigger or smaller than what I started with?”
i
Good to know. People mostly use km, m, cm and mm. The hm, dam and dm steps are rarely used, but they still count when you count steps (m to mm is 3 steps).

Why multiply when we go down?

Smaller units are shorter, so it takes more of them to cover the same length. Watch one metre stick fill up with centimetres.

Mixed units

A length like 3 m 45 cm uses two units. You can only add, subtract or compare lengths when they are in the same unit, so convert first.

Example 2

Same unit first

(a) Write 3 m 45 cm in centimetres, and in metres.
3 m = 300 cm, so 3 m 45 cm =300+45=345 cm.
345\div100=3.45 m
(b) A plumber cuts 65 cm off a 2.4 m length of pipe. How long is the piece that is left, in cm and in m?
2.4 m =2.4\times100=240 cm
240-65=175 cm = 1.75 m
The piece left is 175 cm (1.75 m) long.
(c) Put in order from shortest to longest:
1.2 km,   950 m,   125 000 cm,   1 300 000 mm
Change all to metres: 1.2 km = 1200 m; 950 m; 125\,000\div100=1250 m; 1\,300\,000\div1000=1300 m.
Order: 950 m, 1.2 km, 125 000 cm, 1 300 000 mm
(d) Maya runs 4 laps of a 400 m track, then 1.8 km home on the road. How far does she run in all, in km?
Track: 4\times400=1600 m = 1.6 km
1.6+1.8=3.4 km
Maya runs 3.4 km in all.

Converting Within Imperial: Multiply or Divide?

The imperial units of length are the inch (in or ″), the foot (ft or ′), the yard (yd) and the mile (mi). They do not go up by tens, so you need to know these numbers.

Key idea
12 in = 1 ft3 ft = 1 yd36 in = 1 yd5280 ft = 1 mi1760 yd = 1 mi
Bigger unit to smaller unit: multiply.   Smaller unit to bigger unit: divide.
mi× 1760⟶⟵÷ 1760yd× 3⟶⟵÷ 3ft× 12⟶⟵÷ 12in
to a smaller unit: multiply →← to a bigger unit: divide

Unit factors. A unit factor is a fraction that equals 1, such as \dfrac{12\text{ in}}{1\text{ ft}}. Multiplying by it does not change the length, only the unit. Put the unit you want on top and the unit you have on the bottom, so the old unit cancels:

7\,\cancel{\text{ft}}\times\dfrac{12\text{ in}}{1\,\cancel{\text{ft}}}=7\times12=84\text{ in}54\,\cancel{\text{in}}\times\dfrac{1\text{ ft}}{12\,\cancel{\text{in}}}=54\div12=4.5\text{ ft}

Unit factors are handy when you need two steps (miles to feet to inches): just multiply by one factor after another.

Unit factors, step by step

Write the unit factor so the unit you have is on the bottom. Cross out the units that cancel. What is left is the new unit.

Think Mia wrote 7\text{ ft}\times\dfrac{1\text{ ft}}{12\text{ in}}. Why can’t that be right?
Nothing cancels: she would get “ft × ft ÷ in”, which is not a length unit. The unit she has (ft) must be on the bottom: \dfrac{12\text{ in}}{1\text{ ft}}.

Feet and Inches

Heights and building measurements are often written in feet and inches, like 5'\,7'' (say “5 feet 7 inches”). The same length can be written three ways:

feet and inches5'\,6''
inches only66 in
decimal feet5.5 ft
Key idea
ft and in → in: feet × 12, then add the inches.5'\,7''=5\times12+7=67 in
in → ft and in: ÷ 12; the whole number is the feet; inches left = inches − feet × 12.100 in: 100\div12=8.33\ldots, so 8 ft; 100-8\times12=4, so 8'\,4''
ft and in → decimal ft: inches ÷ 12, add to the feet.6'\,9''=6+9\div12=6.75 ft
decimal ft → ft and in: decimal part × 12.4.25 ft: 0.25\times12=3, so 4'\,3''
!
Watch out. 5.6 ft is not 5'\,6''. The 0.6 is a part of a foot, not inches: 0.6\times12=7.2, so 5.6 ft \approx5'\,7''. (And 5'\,6''=5.5 ft.)
i
Handy facts. 3 in = 0.25 ft · 6 in = 0.5 ft · 9 in = 0.75 ft · 4 in \approx 0.33 ft · 8 in \approx 0.67 ft
For 100 in: 100 ÷ 12 ENTER shows 8.333333333, so 8 ft. Then 100 − 8 × 12 ENTER shows 4, so 4 in left.

5.6 ft is not 5′ 6″: tenths of a foot vs inches

A decimal cuts a foot into 10 equal parts. Inches cut it into 12. So 6 tenths and 6 inches are different lengths.

Adding and Subtracting Feet and Inches

Add feet to feet and inches to inches. If you get 12 or more inches, trade 12 in for 1 ft (regroup), like trading 10 ones for 1 ten. When you subtract and the top has too few inches, borrow 1 ft and turn it into 12 in. To multiply, multiply the feet and the inches, then regroup.

Key idea
Add: 4'\,7''+3'\,10''=7'\,17''
17''=12''+5''=1'\,5'', so 7'\,17''=8'\,5''
Subtract: 9'\,3''-5'\,8''
borrow 1 ft: 9'\,3''=8'\,15''; 8'\,15''-5'\,8''=3'\,7''
✓
Check. Change everything to inches. For the addition: 55+46=101 in, and 101-96=5, so 8'\,5'' ✓

Example 8

Will the SUV fit?

Owen’s garage door opening is 7'\,0'' high. His SUV is 5'\,11'' tall.

(a) He puts a cargo box on the roof that adds 1'\,2''. Will the SUV fit through the door?
5'\,11''+1'\,2''=6'\,13''=7'\,1''
7'\,1'' is more than 7'\,0'', so it will not fit. It is 1 inch too tall.
(b) A slimmer cargo box adds only 11\tfrac12''. How much room is left above it?
5'\,11''+11\tfrac12''=5'\,22\tfrac12''=6'\,10\tfrac12''
7'\,0''-6'\,10\tfrac12''=6'\,12''-6'\,10\tfrac12''=1\tfrac12''
There is 1\tfrac12 inches of room.

Cutting and Fitting

Lumber is sold in lengths such as 8 ft, 10 ft and 12 ft. Trim and pipe are often sold in 8 ft or 2.4 m pieces. To plan the cuts:

  • Step 1. Change everything to the same, smaller unit (inches, or cm).
  • Step 2. Divide the board length by the piece length.
  • Step 3. Round in the way that makes sense.
Key idea   Pieces you can cut from one board: round down (a short leftover is not a piece).
Boards, rolls or packs you must buy: round up (you cannot buy part of a board).
i
On the job. Each saw cut turns about \tfrac18'' of wood into sawdust (the kerf). For tight jobs, leave room for it.
Example 9

Noah’s garden planters

Noah is building garden planters. He needs pieces of board 30 in long. The boards are 8 ft long and cost $5.48 each.

(a) How many 30 in pieces can he cut from one board? How much is left over?
8 ft =8\times12=96 in
96\div30=3.2, so 3 pieces (round down)
Leftover: 96-3\times30=96-90=6 in
(b) He needs 14 pieces. How many boards must he buy, and what do they cost?
14\div3=4.67, so 5 boards (round up)
5\times\$5.48=\$27.40
He must buy 5 boards for $27.40.
(c) For the planter trim, he buys trim in 2.4 m lengths and cuts it into 55 cm pieces. How many pieces can he cut from one length, and how much is left over?
2.4 m = 240 cm; 240\div55=4.36\ldots, so 4 pieces
Leftover: 240-4\times55=240-220=20 cm
✓

Key ideas

SI staircase: km, hm, dam, m, dm, cm, mm. Down a step: × 10. Up a step: ÷ 10. Smaller unit, bigger number.
Imperial: 12 in = 1 ft, 3 ft = 1 yd, 5280 ft = 1 mi, 1760 yd = 1 mi. To a smaller unit, multiply; to a bigger unit, divide. Unit factors: the unit you want on top.
Feet and inches: 5'\,7''=5\times12+7=67 in. 100 in =8'\,4''. Decimal feet: inches ÷ 12. 5.6 ft is not 5'\,6''.
Add and subtract: feet with feet, inches with inches; trade 12 in for 1 ft, or borrow 1 ft = 12 in. Cuts: round down for pieces, round up for boards to buy.
