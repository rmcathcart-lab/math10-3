Fold It to the Moon

A sheet of paper is about 0.1 mm thick. Fold it in half: now it is 2 sheets thick. Fold it again: 4 sheets thick.

Pretend you could keep folding it in half forever. The Moon is about 384 000 km away.

About how many folds would it take for the stack to reach the Moon?

A about 42 folds
B about 4 200 folds
C about 420 000 folds
D about 42 million folds
A: only 42 folds! Each fold doubles the thickness. After 10 folds the stack is about 10 cm thick. After 20 folds, about 105 m (taller than a 30-storey building). After 30 folds, about 107 km. After 42 folds, about 440 000 km: past the Moon! Try it: most people can’t fold a real sheet in half more than 7 or 8 times. Today: mm, cm, m and km, and every other rule from Unit 3.

  • add, subtract and multiply mixed numbers, and read a ruler to \tfrac{1}{16} in

Measuring and Converting Length

Example 1

A ruler and Sofia’s baseboard

The ruler is real size (on paper).

(a) Read the length at each arrow.
A 1\tfrac{3}{16} in    B 4\tfrac58 in
A: 3 small spaces past 1, so 1\tfrac{3}{16} in.
B: 10 spaces past 4: \tfrac{10}{16}=\tfrac58, so 4\tfrac58 in.
(b) How far apart are the two arrows?
4\tfrac58-1\tfrac{3}{16}=4\tfrac{10}{16}-1\tfrac{3}{16}=3\tfrac{7}{16} in
(c) Sofia needs 3 pieces of baseboard, each 2\tfrac34 ft long. What total length is that? Is one 8 ft piece enough?
3\times2\tfrac34=3\times\tfrac{11}{4}=\tfrac{33}{4}=8\tfrac14 ft
8\tfrac14 ft is more than 8 ft, so one 8 ft piece is not enough.
(d) She buys a 10 ft piece instead. How much is left after she cuts the 3 pieces? (Ignore the saw cuts.)
10-8\tfrac14=9\tfrac44-8\tfrac14=1\tfrac34 ft
1\tfrac34 ft is left over.

Check Example 1: read the ruler, cut the boards

Convert the lengths

65 mm = 6.5 cm
0.48 m = 48 cm

Perimeter, Circumference and Rearranging

Example 3

Edging a garden bed

A garden bed is a rectangle with a half circle on one end.
(a) Find the perimeter of the bed, to the nearest hundredth of a metre.
Straight sides: 6+6+3=15 m
Curved edge: \pi d\div2=\pi\times3\div2\approx4.712 m
P\approx15+4.712=19.712\approx19.71 m
(b) Edging is sold in 2.4 m pieces for $7.98 each. How many pieces are needed, and what do they cost?
19.712\div2.4\approx8.21, so round up: 9 pieces
9\times\$7.98=\$71.82
(c) Find the total with 5% GST.
\$71.82\times1.05=\$75.411\approx\$75.41
The edging costs $75.41 with GST.

Check Example 3: walk the edge, then buy the edging

Example 4

Find the missing length

(a) A rectangular dog run uses 32 m of fencing. It is 9.5 m long. How wide is it? Check your answer.
P=2l+2w, so w=(P-2l)\div2
w=(32-2\times9.5)\div2=(32-19)\div2=6.5 m
Check: 2(9.5)+2(6.5)=19+13=32 ✓
(b) The edge of a round table top is 4.4 m around. Find its diameter, to the nearest hundredth of a metre.
C=\pi d, so d=C\div\pi
d=4.4\div\pi\approx1.4006\approx1.40 m
Check: \pi\times1.40\approx4.40 ✓
(c) A rectangular patio has an area of 22.5 m². It is 3 m wide. How long is it?
A=lw, so l=A\div w=22.5\div3=7.5 m. Check: 7.5\times3=22.5 ✓

Area

Example 5

A sail, a flower bed, a rug and area units

(a) A triangle-shaped sail has a base of 2.4 m and a height of 3.5 m. Find its area.
A=\tfrac12bh=\tfrac12\times2.4\times3.5
A=4.2 m²
(b) A flower bed is a trapezoid. Its parallel sides are 3\tfrac12 ft and 5\tfrac12 ft, and they are 2 ft apart. Find its area.
A=\tfrac12(a+b)h=\tfrac12\times(3.5+5.5)\times2
=\tfrac12\times9\times2=9 ft²
(c) A round rug is 1.8 m across. Find its area, to the nearest hundredth.
r=1.8\div2=0.9 m
A=\pi r^2=\pi\times0.9^2\approx2.544\,69\approx2.54 m²
(d) Fill in the blanks.
9 ft² = 1 296 in²    45 ft² = 5 yd²
4.2 m² = 42 000 cm²
9\times144=1\,296; 45\div9=5; 4.2\times10\,000=42\,000

Check Example 5: where the area formulas come from

Why 144, 9 and 10 000? Cut the big square into small ones

An area unit is a square. A square foot is 12 in long and 12 in wide, so it holds 12\times12 square inches, not 12.

Example 6

Carpet for Jaspreet’s L-shaped room

Jaspreet is putting carpet in an L-shaped family room. All the corners are square.
(a) Find the area as the whole rectangle minus the cut-out corner.
Whole: 18\times15=270 ft²
Cut-out: (18-12)\times(15-11)=6\times4=24 ft²
Area: 270-24=246 ft²
(b) Check by splitting the room into two rectangles.
Split with an up-and-down line:
12\times15=180 ft² and 6\times11=66 ft²
180+66=246 ft² ✓
(c) Carpet costs $38.50 per square yard and is sold in whole square yards. What will the carpet cost before tax?
246\div9\approx27.33 yd², so buy 28 yd² (round up)
28\times\$38.50=\$1\,078.00
The carpet costs $1 078.00 before tax.

Surface Area

The surface area (SA) of a 3-D object is the total area of all its faces. Unfold the object into a flat net, find the area of each part, and add.

Example 7

The chocolate box

This is the net of a chocolate box shaped like a triangular prism. The triangle ends have sides of 5 cm, 5 cm and 8 cm and a height of 3 cm. The box is 25 cm long.

(a) Complete the face table.
FacesArea
2 triangles2\times\tfrac12\times8\times3=24 cm²
bottom (8 cm by 25 cm)8\times25=200 cm²
2 sides (5 cm by 25 cm)2\times5\times25=250 cm²
Surface area24+200+250=474 cm²
2 triangles 24 cm² · bottom 200 cm² · 2 sides 250 cm² · SA = 474 cm²
(b) The factory has a sheet of cardboard with an area of 1 m² (10 000 cm²). If no cardboard is wasted, how many whole boxes can it make?
10\,000\div474\approx21.1. A part of a box is not a box, so round down: 21 boxes.

Check Example 7: fill the face table from the net

Example 8

A soup can, a tent and a funnel

(a) A soup can has a radius of 3.8 cm and a height of 11 cm. Find the area of the paper label that wraps around the side.
The label unrolls into a rectangle: 2\pi r\times h
2\pi rh=2\times\pi\times3.8\times11\approx262.6 cm²
(b) Find the surface area of the whole can (side, top and bottom).
Two circles: 2\pi r^2=2\times\pi\times3.8^2\approx90.7 cm²
\text{SA}\approx262.64+90.73\approx353.4 cm²
(c) A tent is a square pyramid with a 2.2 m by 2.2 m floor. The slant height of each side is 1.8 m. How much fabric is in the 4 sides and the floor?
Floor: 2.2\times2.2=4.84 m²
4 triangles: 4\times\tfrac12\times2.2\times1.8=7.92 m²
\text{SA}=4.84+7.92=12.76 m² of fabric
(d) A kitchen funnel is a cone with a radius of 6 cm and a slant height of 15 cm (ignore the spout). It has no base. Find the area of plastic in the cone.
No base, so only the curved side: \pi rs
\pi\times6\times15\approx282.7 cm²

Key ideas

Length. SI: × or ÷ 10 for each step. Imperial: 12 in = 1 ft, 3 ft = 1 yd, 5 280 ft = 1 mi; regroup 12 in as 1 ft. Between: 1 in = 2.54 cm, 1 km \approx0.621 mi. Ruler: whole inches, then sixteenths, then simplify.
Perimeter. Add the sides; circle C=\pi d; half circle \pi d\div2. Round up for pieces. Rearranging: undo + or − first, then × or ÷, and check.
Area. lw, bh, \tfrac12bh, \tfrac12(a+b)h, \pi r^2 (radius!). Composite: split and add, or whole − cut-out. 1 ft² = 144 in², 1 yd² = 9 ft², 1 m² = 10 000 cm².
Surface area. Net, area of each piece, add. Cylinder 2\pi r^2+2\pi rh; pyramid base + 4\times\tfrac12bs; cone \pi r^2+\pi rs. Use only the pieces that are there.
