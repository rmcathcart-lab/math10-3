Four pieces make a triangle 13 squares long and 5 squares tall.

Move the same four pieces around, and you get the same triangle… with a hole in it!

Where did the extra square come from?

Area of 2-D Shapes

Carpet, sod, paint, tile and pizza are all about the surface inside a shape. Today we measure it in square units, with a formula for each shape, and use it to work out the cost of a job.

I can…
  • explain what area is, and count it in square units (cm², m², in², ft², yd²)
  • find the area of a rectangle, parallelogram, triangle and trapezoid, using the height at a right angle
  • find the area of a circle with A=\pi r^2, and estimate it with 3\times r\times r
  • change between area units, and find the cost of a job, rounding the boxes up
M4 Solve problems that involve SI and imperial area measurements of regular, composite and irregular 2-D shapes and 3-D objects, including decimal and fractional measurements, and verify the solutions.
1

What Is Area?

The area of a flat shape is the amount of surface inside it. We measure area by counting square units. A square that is 1 cm long and 1 cm wide has an area of 1 square centimetre, written 1 cm².

Other square units: square metres (m²), square inches (in²), square feet (ft²) and square yards (yd²). Some referents:

about 1 cm²your fingernail
1 ft²a 1 ft by 1 ft floor tile
1 m²a big square of cardboard, 1 m by 1 m
↔
Area vs perimeter. Perimeter is the distance around a shape (fencing, trim). It is a length, in cm, m, in or ft. Area is the surface inside (carpet, paint, sod). It is in square units.
Example 1

Count the squares

Each small square stands for 1 cm². Find the area of each shape by counting squares.

(a) the rectangle   24 cm²
4 rows of 6 squares: 24 squares, so 24 cm².
(b) the triangle   8 cm²
6 full squares and 4 half squares. The 4 halves make 2 full squares. 6+2=8 cm². The triangle is half of a 4 by 4 square: 16\div2=8 ✓
(c) How could you find the area of (a) without counting every square?
4 rows of 6 squares: 4\times6=24.

Counting works for small shapes on a grid. For real rooms, yards and pieces of fabric, we use a formula instead.

2

Rectangles and Parallelograms

A rectangle is rows of squares, so its area is length times width. A parallelogram is a “pushed over” rectangle with two pairs of parallel sides. Cut a triangle off one end and slide it to the other end: you get a rectangle with the same base and height.

Key idea
RectangleA=lwlength × width
SquareA=s\times s=s^2side × side
ParallelogramA=bhbase × height
The height is measured straight across, at a right angle (90°) to the base. It is not the slanted side.
!
Watch out. Both lengths must be in the same unit before you multiply. Metres times metres gives square metres: \text{m}\times\text{m}=\text{m}^2.

Why the same unit?

A shelf is 2 m long and 50 cm deep. Multiplying the numbers as they are gives nonsense. Change one length first.

Example 2

A bedroom floor and a parallelogram

(a) A bedroom floor is a rectangle 4.2 m long and 3.6 m wide. Estimate its area, then find it.
Estimate: 4\times4=16 m²
A=lw
A=4.2\times3.6=15.12
The floor is 15.12 m² (close to 16 ✓).
(b) Find the area of the parallelogram. Which length do you not use?
A=bh=9.5\times5.2=49.4 cm²
Do not use the slanted side, 6 cm.

3

Triangles and Trapezoids

Two copies of the same triangle fit together to make a parallelogram. So a triangle’s area is half of base times height. The height goes from the top corner straight down to the base. For some triangles, the height falls outside the triangle, so you may need to extend the base with a dotted line.

A trapezoid has exactly one pair of parallel sides, a and b. Two copies of a trapezoid also make a parallelogram, with base a+b. So its area is half of (a+b) times the height.

Key idea
TriangleA=\dfrac12bh(or A=b\times h\div2)
TrapezoidA=\dfrac12(a+b)h(or A=(a+b)\times h\div2)
a and b are the two parallel sides. The height h is the straight distance between them.
!
Watch out. Don’t forget the \tfrac12. Base × height alone gives the area of the whole parallelogram, which is double the triangle.
Example 4

Three triangles

Find the area of each triangle.

(a) A triangle with base 8 cm and height 5 cm.
A=\tfrac12bh
A=8\times5\div2=20
The area is 20 cm².
(b) The triangle at the top of a shed wall has base 8 ft and height 3\tfrac12 ft.
A=8\times3\tfrac12\div2
=28\div2=14
The area is 14 ft².
(c)
The base is 6.4 m (not the dotted part).
A=6.4\times3.5\div2=11.2
The area is 11.2 m².
Example 5

Nadia’s flower bed

Nadia is building a flower bed along her back fence in Okotoks. It is a trapezoid. The side along the fence is 3.8 m, the front side is 2.4 m, and the two parallel sides are 1.5 m apart.

(a) Find the area of the flower bed.
A=\tfrac12(a+b)h
A=(2.4+3.8)\times1.5\div2=6.2\times1.5\div2=4.65
The flower bed is 4.65 m².
(b) Garden soil is sold by the bag. One bag covers 0.5 m². How many bags should Nadia buy?
4.65\div0.5=9.3 bags. She can’t buy part of a bag, so round up.
Nadia should buy 10 bags. (9 bags would only cover 4.5 m².)
Use brackets for a trapezoid so the adding is done first: ( 2.4 + 3.8 ) × 1.5 ÷ 2 ENTER gives 4.65.

Think it through

Think A triangle and a parallelogram have the same base, 10 cm, and the same height, 4 cm. Compare their areas.
Parallelogram: 10\times4=40 cm². Triangle: 40\div2=20 cm². The triangle is half the parallelogram.
Think A trapezoid has parallel sides 6 m and 6 m. What shape is it really? Does the formula still work?
Both pairs of sides are parallel, so it is a parallelogram. The formula gives (6+6)\times h\div2=6h, which is base × height ✓

Circles

The radius r of a circle goes from the centre to the edge. The diameter d goes all the way across through the centre, so d=2r and r=d\div2.

The shaded square is r by r, so its area is r^2. The circle’s area is a bit more than 3 of these squares. The exact number is \pi (pi), about 3.14.

Key idea   Circle: A=\pi r^2 (that is, \pi\times r\times r)     Estimate: A\approx3\times r\times r
Use the π key and the x² key. For a radius of 45 cm: π × 45 x² ENTER gives 6361.725…
!
Watch out. If you are given the diameter, halve it first to get the radius. And r^2 means r\times r, not r\times2.

Why is it π × r × r? Cut the pizza

Cut a circle into thin slices and lay them out top-to-tail. They make a shape that is almost a parallelogram: its base is half the way around the circle, and its height is the radius.

Example 6

A table top and a rug

Round each area to the nearest tenth.

(a) A round table top has a radius of 45 cm. Find its area.
A=\pi r^2=\pi\times45^2
=\pi\times2025=6361.72\ldots
The table top is about 6361.7 cm².
(b) A round rug has a diameter of 8 ft. Estimate its area, then find it.
r=8\div2=4 ft
Estimate: 3\times4\times4=48 ft²
A=\pi\times4^2=50.265\ldots
The rug is about 50.3 ft².
Example 7

Pizza Palace

A pizza’s size is its diameter. Pizza Palace sells one 16 in pizza for $21.99, or two 10 in pizzas for $11.99 each.

(a) Which choice gives more pizza?
16 in: r=8; A=\pi\times8^2\approx201.06 in²
10 in: r=5; A=\pi\times5^2\approx78.54 in²
Two 10 in: 2\times78.54=157.08 in²
One 16 in pizza gives more pizza.
(b) Find the price per square inch of each choice, to a tenth of a cent. Which is the better buy?
16 in: \$21.99\div201.06\approx\$0.109 per in²
Two 10 in: 2\times\$11.99=\$23.98; \$23.98\div157.08\approx\$0.153 per in²
The 16 in pizza is the better buy: more pizza for less money.
Math Historyabout 1650 BCE

A round field, nearly 3700 years ago

The Rhind papyrus is an Egyptian math scroll written by a scribe named Ahmes, copied from an even older text. Today it is in the British Museum. One of its problems asks for the area of a round field 9 khet across (a khet is an old Egyptian length).

Ahmes had no \pi key. His rule: take away \tfrac19 of the diameter, then make a square with what is left. 9-1=8, and 8\times8=64. So the field is about 64 square khet.

Use A=\pi r^2 on the same field. How close was Ahmes?
r=9\div2=4.5; A=\pi\times4.5^2=63.6 square khet. Ahmes’s 64 is only 0.4 too big, less than 1% off. His rule acts like \pi\approx3.16.

Changing Area Units, and the Cost of a Job

A square foot is 12 in by 12 in, so it holds 12\times12=144 square inches. A square yard is 3 ft by 3 ft, so it holds 3\times3=9 square feet. In the same way, 1 m² is 100 cm by 100 cm =10\,000 cm².

Key idea   Area units
1 ft² = 144 in²1 yd² = 9 ft²1 m² = 10 000 cm²1 m² ≈ 10.76 ft²
Big unit → small unit: multiply.Small unit → big unit: divide.
Cost of a job = area × price per square unit
!
Watch out. 1 ft² is not 12 in². The 12 is used twice (12\times12), once for each side. If the lengths are in different units (like ft and in), you can also change the lengths first, then multiply.

Buying materials: round up, and add 10%

Materials like flooring, sod and tile are sold in whole boxes, rolls or pieces. Always round the number you buy up. Installers also buy about 10% extra for cutting and mistakes (multiply the area by 1.10).

Example 9

Owen’s new floor

Owen is putting vinyl plank flooring in a bedroom that is 12 ft 6 in long and 10 ft wide. One box covers 23.4 ft² and costs $54.99. He adds 10% for waste.

(a) Find the area of the floor in ft².
6 in =6\div12=0.5 ft, so 12 ft 6 in = 12.5 ft
A=12.5\times10=125 ft²
(b) How much flooring does Owen need with the 10% extra?
125\times1.10=137.5 ft²
(c) How many boxes must he buy?
137.5\div23.4=5.87\ldots boxes
Round up: Owen must buy 6 boxes.
(d) What is the total cost with 5% GST?
6\times\$54.99=\$329.94
\$329.94\times1.05=\$346.437\approx\$346.44
The flooring costs $346.44 with GST.
(c) 125 × 1.1 ÷ 23.4 ENTER shows 5.876…   (d) 6 × 54.99 × 1.05 ENTER shows 346.437

Two more area jobs

(a) A round pool cover has a diameter of 14 ft. The material costs $2.25 per ft². Estimate the area, then find the cost of the material to the nearest cent.
r=14\div2=7 ft. Estimate: 3\times7\times7=147 ft²
A=\pi\times7^2=153.93\ldots ft²
Cost: 153.93\ldots\times\$2.25=\$346.36
(b) A triangular garden in a Red Deer park has a base of 9 m and a height of 4.4 m. Topsoil covers 2 m² per bag. How many bags are needed?
A=9\times4.4\div2=19.8 m²
19.8\div2=9.9 bags → round up: 10 bags

Think it through

Think A round rug has twice the radius of another. Does it have twice the area?
No, 4 times. The radius is used twice: \pi\times(2r)\times(2r)=4\pi r^2. The 8 ft rug (50.3 ft²) has 4 times the area of a 4 ft rug (12.6 ft²).
Think Without the 10% extra, how many boxes would Owen need? Why do installers add it?
125\div23.4=5.34\ldots, still 6 boxes. Planks get cut to fit the walls, and the cut-off bits often can’t be used, so you need more than the exact area.
✓

Key ideas

Area is the surface inside, in square units. Perimeter is the distance around, in plain units. Both lengths in the same unit before you multiply.
Rectangle A=lw, parallelogram A=bh, triangle A=b\times h\div2, trapezoid A=(a+b)\times h\div2. The height is at a right angle.
Circle A=\pi r^2; estimate with 3\times r\times r. Halve a diameter first. r^2=r\times r.
1 ft² = 144 in², 1 yd² = 9 ft², 1 m² = 10 000 cm². Big → small: ×; small → big: ÷. Cost = area × price; round boxes up; add 10% for waste.
