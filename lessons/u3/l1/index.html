The 17 Horses

A rancher near Cochrane retires and gives her 17 horses to her three kids.

The oldest gets \tfrac12 of the horses, the middle kid \tfrac13, the youngest \tfrac19. No cutting a horse!

How can they share the horses and follow her rule exactly?

?
17 doesn’t split into halves, thirds or ninths. Is it impossible?
A neighbour rides over and lends them her horse. Now there are 18. \tfrac12 of 18 is 9, \tfrac13 of 18 is 6 and \tfrac19 of 18 is 2. That is 9+6+2=17 horses, so one horse is left: the neighbour rides home on it! The twist: \tfrac12+\tfrac13+\tfrac19=\tfrac{17}{18}, not 1 whole, so the rule never gave away all the horses. Fractions Refresher: Mixed Numbers

Tape measures, building plans and recipes are full of fractions like 2\tfrac34 in. Before we measure anything, we review mixed numbers and improper fractions, how to add, subtract and multiply them, and how to check an answer with an estimate and the calculator.

I can…
  • change a mixed number to an improper fraction and back (2\tfrac34=\tfrac{11}{4})
  • add mixed numbers, and regroup when the fraction part is 1 or more
  • subtract mixed numbers, and regroup (borrow) a whole when I need to
  • multiply with mixed numbers by changing them to improper fractions first
  • estimate first, then check my answer with the calculator

Mixed Numbers and Improper Fractions

Measurements in inches, feet, yards and cups often use fractions bigger than 1, like 2\tfrac34 in. There are two ways to write them.

Proper fractionless than 1. The top (numerator) is smaller than the bottom (denominator).\tfrac34,\ \ \tfrac58
Improper fraction1 or more. The top is the same as or bigger than the bottom.\tfrac{11}{4},\ \ \tfrac88
Mixed numbera whole number and a proper fraction together.2\tfrac34 means 2+\tfrac34

Each whole bar is 4 quarters. 2 wholes and 3 more quarters make 11 quarters:
2\tfrac34=\tfrac{11}{4}

Improper fraction → mixed number: divide the top by the bottom. The answer is the whole number. The remainder goes over the same bottom.\tfrac{17}{5}: 17\div5=3 remainder 2, so \tfrac{17}{5}=3\tfrac25
Mixed number → improper fraction: multiply the whole number by the bottom, add the top, and keep the bottom.4\tfrac23=\tfrac{4\times3+2}{3}=\tfrac{14}{3}

Simplest form, and the calculator

A mixed number is in simplest form when its fraction part is in simplest form (Lesson 1.2): 3\tfrac68=3\tfrac34.

!
When you simplify a mixed number, only the fraction part changes. The whole number stays the same: 3\tfrac68=3\tfrac34, not \tfrac34 and not 1\tfrac12.
On the TI-30XIIS: enter 2\tfrac34 as 2A b/c3A b/c4ENTER. The screen shows 2u3⌟4 (the u sits between the whole number and the fraction). Press 2ndd/e (the second job of the A b/c key) to switch to 11⌟4. Press it again to switch back. The calculator also puts fractions in simplest form for you.

Adding Mixed Numbers

You can only add pieces that are the same size. So fractions must have the same denominator before you add them. Then add the tops and keep the bottom: \tfrac38+\tfrac18=\tfrac48=\tfrac12.

If the bottoms are different, find a common denominator: a number that both bottoms divide into evenly. The smallest one, the lowest common denominator, keeps the numbers small.

i
Quick refresher: to find a common denominator, count by the bigger bottom until the smaller one divides in. For 4 and 6: 6, 12. 12 works, so use twelfths. Then make equivalent fractions (Lesson 1.2): \tfrac34=\tfrac9{12} and \tfrac16=\tfrac2{12}.
To add mixed numbers
  1. Give the fraction parts a common denominator.
  2. Add the whole numbers. Add the fractions.
  3. If the fraction part is improper, regroup: change it to a mixed number and add its whole to the whole number. 5\tfrac{11}{8}=5+1\tfrac38=6\tfrac38
  4. Write the answer in simplest form.
!
Never add the denominators. \tfrac14+\tfrac14=\tfrac24 (two quarters), not \tfrac28.

End to end: lengths on a tape measure

When pipes, boards or pieces of fabric are joined end to end, the total length is the sum of the pieces. When a piece is cut off, what is left is a difference. Watch it happen on a tape measure. Estimate first by rounding each piece to the nearest whole unit.

Example 5

Copper pipe

A plumber joins three pieces of copper pipe end to end. They are 14\tfrac38 in, 9\tfrac34 in and 6\tfrac12 in long.

(a) Estimate the total length. Round each piece to the nearest inch.
14\tfrac38\approx14, 9\tfrac34\approx10, 6\tfrac12\approx7
14+10+7=31 in
About 31 in.
(b) Find the exact total length. Is it close to your estimate?
Eighths: 14\tfrac38+9\tfrac68+6\tfrac48
Wholes: 14+9+6=29; fractions: \tfrac{13}{8}=1\tfrac58
29+1\tfrac58=30\tfrac58 in
The pipe is 30\tfrac58 in long, close to 31 in.

Subtracting Mixed Numbers

Subtract the same way: common denominator first, then subtract the whole numbers and the fractions. Sometimes the top fraction is too small to take the other fraction away, as in 5\tfrac14-2\tfrac34. Then regroup (borrow): take 1 whole from the whole number and change it into fraction pieces.

5\tfrac14=4+\tfrac44+\tfrac14=4\tfrac54. Now take away 2\tfrac34: 4\tfrac54-2\tfrac34=2\tfrac24=2\tfrac12.

To subtract mixed numbers
  1. Give the fraction parts a common denominator.
  2. If the first fraction is smaller than the one you subtract, regroup: take 1 from the whole number and add it to the fraction as \tfrac88, \tfrac44, \tfrac{12}{12}, …
  3. Subtract the whole numbers. Subtract the fractions.
  4. Write the answer in simplest form.
!
Do not flip the fractions to make the subtraction easy. In 5\tfrac14-2\tfrac34, doing \tfrac34-\tfrac14 gives the wrong answer 3\tfrac24. Regroup instead. To subtract from a whole number, regroup too: 9=8\tfrac{16}{16}.
Example 6

Subtracting

Fabric for the school play

Multiplying with Mixed Numbers

To multiply two fractions, multiply the tops and multiply the bottoms: \tfrac23\times\tfrac45=\tfrac8{15}. Remember that “of” means multiply: \tfrac12 of 3\tfrac13 is \tfrac12\times3\tfrac13.

Mixed numbers must be changed to improper fractions first. A whole number is a fraction over 1: 4=\tfrac41.

To multiply with mixed numbers
  1. Change each mixed number (and whole number) to an improper fraction.
  2. Multiply the tops. Multiply the bottoms.
  3. Change the answer back to a mixed number in simplest form.
4\times2\tfrac38=\tfrac41\times\tfrac{19}8=\tfrac{76}8=9\tfrac48=9\tfrac12
!
Do not just multiply the whole numbers and the fractions separately. 2\tfrac12\times2\tfrac12 is not 4\tfrac14. Change to improper fractions: \tfrac52\times\tfrac52=\tfrac{25}4=6\tfrac14.
i
Shortcut: you may simplify before you multiply: divide a top and a bottom by the same number. \tfrac74\times\tfrac{12}5: 12\div4=3 and 4\div4=1, so it becomes \tfrac71\times\tfrac35=\tfrac{21}5. Smaller numbers, same answer.

Estimating and Checking with the Calculator

Before you trust an answer, estimate. Round each mixed number to the nearest whole number: if the fraction part is \tfrac12 or more, round up; if it is less than \tfrac12, round down. Then do the easy calculation in your head.

The calculator can do the exact work. It can also change a mixed number to a decimal, and a decimal back to a fraction. Tape measures and plans use fractions of an inch, so this helps when a calculation gives a decimal.

Estimate (round to whole numbers) \;\rightarrow\; calculate (by hand or calculator) \;\rightarrow\; check (is the answer close to the estimate?)
A mixed number as a decimal: divide the fraction part, then add the whole: 3\tfrac34=3+0.75=3.75
On the TI-30XIIS: 7\tfrac78-2\tfrac13: 7A b/c7A b/c8−2A b/c1A b/c3ENTER. If the answer shows as an improper fraction, press 2ndd/e to see the mixed number.
Fraction ↔ decimal: press 2ndF◂▸D. A decimal such as 4.625 ENTER then 2ndF◂▸D shows the fraction (4\tfrac58).

Example 11

Jesse’s bench

Jesse is building a bench seat from 3 boards laid end to end. Each board is 23\tfrac58 in long. He says the seat will be 59\tfrac78 in long.

(a) Use an estimate to show that Jesse’s answer is not reasonable.
23\tfrac58\approx24, and 3\times24=72 in.
59\tfrac78 in is far from 72 in, so Jesse made a mistake.
(b) Find the correct length.
3\times23\tfrac58=\tfrac31\times\tfrac{189}8=\tfrac{567}8
567\div8=70 R 7, so 70\tfrac78 in.
The seat will be 70\tfrac78 in long.
(c) A drawing for the bench says the legs are 16.375 in long. Write this as a mixed number of inches, so Jesse can find it on his tape measure.
0.375=\tfrac{375}{1000}=\tfrac38 (or 16.375 ENTER 2nd F◂▸D), so the legs are 16\tfrac38 in long.
✓

Key ideas

Improper → mixed: divide; the remainder goes over the same bottom (\tfrac{17}5=3\tfrac25). Mixed → improper: whole × bottom + top, over the bottom (4\tfrac23=\tfrac{14}3). Simplify only the fraction part.
Add: common denominator, add wholes, add fractions, then regroup (5\tfrac{11}8=6\tfrac38). Never add the bottoms.
Subtract: common denominator; if the top fraction is too small, borrow 1 whole (5\tfrac14=4\tfrac54). Never flip the fractions.
Multiply: change to improper fractions, multiply tops and bottoms, change back. Estimate (round to whole numbers) and check with the calculator (A b/c, d/e, F◂▸D).
