Hidden Dots

Three dice are stacked in a tower on a table. On every die, opposite faces add to 7: 1 and 6, 2 and 5, 3 and 4.

The top of the tower shows 5.

How many dots are on the faces nobody can see: the faces pressed together and the face on the table?

A 14 dots
B 15 dots
C 16 dots
D 21 dots
C, 16 dots. You don’t need to know any of the hidden numbers! The middle die and the bottom die each hide a top and bottom, a pair of opposite faces: 7+7=14. The top die hides only its bottom, opposite the 5: 7-5=2. So 14+2=16. Bonus: a tower of 4 dice with a 3 on top hides 7\times4-3=25 dots.

Nets and Surface Area of Prisms

How much cardboard makes a shipping box? How much nylon for a tent, or paint for a bedroom? Today we unfold boxes and other prisms into flat nets, and add up the areas of all the faces: the surface area.

I can…
  • count the faces, edges and vertices of a prism, and name it by the shape of its bases
  • tell whether a pattern is a net of a cube
  • use a face table or SA=2lw+2lh+2wh to find the surface area of a box, and 6s^2 for a cube
  • find the surface area of a triangular prism: 2 triangles + 3 rectangles
  • cover a real object: an open box, a tent, the walls of a room (coats, cans, cost)

Prisms, Faces and Nets

A 3-D object (three-dimensional object) has length, width and height, like a box. Each flat surface of a 3-D object is a face. Two faces meet along an edge. Edges meet at a corner called a vertex (plural: vertices).

A prism is a 3-D object with two matching ends that face each other. The ends are called the bases. All the other faces are rectangles. A prism is named for the shape of its bases. In the pictures, dashed lines are edges hidden at the back.

Think A prism has bases with 8 sides (an octagonal prism, like a stop sign made thick). How many faces does it have?
10 faces: the 2 octagon bases, plus one rectangle for each of the 8 sides of the base.

Nets: unfold it flat

A net is a flat pattern that folds up to make a 3-D object. Each face of the object appears in the net once, with no gaps and no overlaps. Think of a cereal box cut open along some edges and pressed flat.

Example 2

Each pattern is made of 6 squares. Which patterns are nets of a cube? Imagine folding each one. Write yes or no.

(a)
Yes. The column of 4 wraps around; the two side squares close the ends.
(b)
No. 5 squares in a row wrap all the way around, so the first and last overlap, and one side is left open.
(c)

(d)

Math History1525

An artist who unfolded shapes

Albrecht Dürer was a German artist, famous for his woodcuts and engravings. In 1525 he wrote a book about measuring and drawing for painters and craftspeople.

In it he drew 3-D shapes in a new way: unfolded flat, so a reader could copy the pattern, cut it out and fold it up. His book has flat patterns for the cube and many fancier solids. It is one of the earliest books to show them. In 1543 another German artist, Augustin Hirschvogel, first called these patterns nets.

Dürer’s pattern for a cube has 6 squares. If each square is 6 cm by 6 cm, how much paper does the net cover?
One square: 6\times6=36 cm². Six squares: 6\times36=216 cm². That is the cube’s surface area (next section).
2

Surface Area of a Rectangular Prism

The surface area (SA) of a 3-D object is the total area of all of its faces. It is the same as the area of its net. Surface area tells you how much material covers the outside: cardboard for a box, wrapping paper, paint or fabric. It is measured in square units, such as cm², m², in² or ft².

A rectangular prism has 6 faces in 3 matching pairs: top and bottom, front and back, and the two ends. A face table keeps track of them so that no face is missed or counted twice.

Key idea   Surface area = the sum of the areas of all the faces = the area of the net.
Rectangular prism (length l, width w, height h)
SA=2lw+2lh+2wh
top & bottomfront & backtwo ends
Cube (edge s)
SA=6s^2
6 equal squares, each s\times s
!
Watch out. Surface area is not l\times w\times h. Multiplying all three lengths gives the volume (how much fits inside, in Unit 4). Surface area adds the areas of the faces.

Fill or wrap? Volume and surface area are different

Volume counts the cubes inside. Surface area counts the squares on the outside.

A shipping box

A shipping box is 40 cm long, 30 cm wide and 25 cm high.

(a) Write the size of each face on the net.
Front and back: 40\times25   Top and bottom: 40\times30   Ends: 30\times25

Example 4

Two cubes

For a cube, use the x² key: an edge of 2.5 in gives 6 × 2.5 x² ENTER =37.5.
(a) A wooden toy block is a cube with edges 2\tfrac12 in long. Find its surface area.
2\tfrac12 in =2.5 in
SA=6s^2=6\times2.5^2=6\times6.25=37.5
The block’s surface area is 37.5 in² (37\tfrac12 in²).
(b) A storage ottoman is a cube with edges 1 ft 3 in long. Fabric covers all 6 faces. How much fabric is needed, in in² and in ft²?
1 ft 3 in =12+3=15 in
SA=6\times15^2=6\times225=1\,350 in²
In ft²: 1\,350\div144=9.375
The ottoman needs 1 350 in², or about 9.4 ft², of fabric.
Think You make every edge of a cube twice as long. What happens to its surface area?
It is 4 times as big. Each face is twice as long and twice as wide: 2\times2=4 times the area. For example 6\times1^2=6 becomes 6\times2^2=24.
3

Surface Area of a Triangular Prism

A triangular prism has 5 faces: 2 matching triangles (the bases) and 3 rectangles. Each rectangle is as long as the prism. Its width is one side of the triangle. So the net is three rectangles in a row with a triangle on each side.

Key idea   Triangular prism: SA = 2 triangles + 3 rectangles
Each triangle: A=\tfrac12bh, using the height of the triangle (straight up from the base).
Each rectangle: A= (side of the triangle) × (length of the prism).
Shortcut: the 3 rectangles together = (perimeter of the triangle) × (length of the prism).
!
Watch out. The height of the triangle is not one of its slanted sides (unless the triangle has a right angle). Use the height for the two triangles, and the three sides for the three rectangles.
Example 5

A rubber door wedge

A rubber door wedge is a triangular prism. Its ends are right triangles with sides 5 cm, 12 cm and 13 cm. The wedge is 4 cm wide.

(a) On the net, write how to find the area of each face.
Triangles: \tfrac12(12)(5) each   Rectangles: 5\times4, 12\times4, 13\times4
(b) Find the surface area of the wedge.
Triangles: \tfrac12\times12\times5=30 cm² each; 2\times30=60 cm²
Rectangles: 5\times4=20, 12\times4=48, 13\times4=52; 20+48+52=120 cm²
(Shortcut: (5+12+13)\times4=30\times4=120 ✓)
SA=60+120=180 cm². The wedge has a surface area of 180 cm².

Why the shortcut works: three rectangles make one

All three rectangles are as long as the prism. Push them together and they make one long rectangle: as wide as the perimeter of the triangle.

Example 6

Jordan’s camping tent

Jordan is sewing a small camping tent shaped like a triangular prism. The front is a triangle 2.4 m wide at the bottom and 1.6 m high. Each sloping side of the tent is 2 m. The tent is 3 m long.

(a) How much fabric is needed for the whole tent, including the floor?
Two ends: 2\times\tfrac12\times2.4\times1.6=3.84 m²
Two sloping sides: 2\times(2\times3)=12 m²
Floor: 2.4\times3=7.2 m²
SA=3.84+12+7.2=23.04 m²
(b) The floor is made of a heavier fabric. How much of the lighter nylon is needed for the rest?
Everything except the floor:
23.04-7.2=15.84 m²  (or 3.84+12=15.84 m²)
Jordan needs 15.84 m² of nylon.
(c) The nylon costs $8.95 per m². What does the nylon cost with 5% GST?
15.84\times\$8.95=\$141.768;  \$141.768\times1.05=\$148.856\ldots\approx\$148.86
The nylon costs $148.86 with GST.

Open Boxes and Real Jobs

Not every job covers every face. An open box has no top, so it has only 5 faces. When you paint a room you paint the walls (and maybe the ceiling), but not the floor, the doors or the windows. Always ask first: which faces get covered?

Key idea   Covering a real object
1Sketch the object. List only the faces that get covered. 2Find the area of each of those faces and add. 3Subtract any openings that are not covered (doors, windows). 4Multiply by the number of coats (for paint or stain). 5Divide by the area one can, roll or sheet covers. Round up. 6Find the cost.
Example 7

Amira’s planter box

Amira is building a planter box with no top out of plywood. The box is 4 ft long, 1\tfrac12 ft wide and 2 ft high.

(a) Which faces does she need to build? Find the area of plywood needed.
5 faces: the bottom, front, back and two ends (no top).
Bottom: 4\times1.5=6 ft²   Front and back: 2\times(4\times2)=16 ft²
Two ends: 2\times(1.5\times2)=6 ft²   Total: 6+16+6=28 ft²
(b) A sheet of plywood is 4 ft by 8 ft. Is one sheet enough? Explain, using area.
One sheet is 4\times8=32 ft². She needs 28 ft², which is less than 32 ft². Yes, one sheet has enough area (if she plans her cuts carefully).
Example 8

Lucas paints his bedroom

Lucas is painting the four walls of his bedroom. The room is 4 m long, 3.5 m wide and 2.5 m high. He does not paint the ceiling, the door (0.9 m by 2 m) or the window (1.5 m by 1.2 m). He wants 2 coats. A can of paint covers about 35 m² and costs $54.98.

(a) Find the area of the four walls.
Long walls: 2\times(4\times2.5)=20 m²
Short walls: 2\times(3.5\times2.5)=17.5 m²
Walls: 20+17.5=37.5 m²
(b) Subtract the door and the window.
Door: 0.9\times2=1.8; window: 1.5\times1.2=1.8
37.5-1.8-1.8=33.9 m²
(c) How many cans does he need for 2 coats?
2 coats: 33.9\times2=67.8 m²
67.8\div35=1.93\ldots Round up: 2 cans.
(d) Find the cost of the paint with 5% GST.
2\times\$54.98=\$109.96
\$109.96\times1.05=\$115.458\approx\$115.46
The paint costs $115.46 with GST.

Key ideas

Prisms have 2 matching bases (named for their shape) and rectangle sides. A net folds up into the object: every face once, no gaps, no overlaps.
Surface area = the total area of all the faces = the area of the net. Box: SA=2lw+2lh+2wh (a face table helps). Cube: SA=6s^2. Not l\times w\times h!
Triangular prism: 2 triangles (\tfrac12bh) + 3 rectangles (each side × length). Shortcut: perimeter × length.
Real jobs: only the faces that get covered, minus openings, × coats, ÷ what one can or sheet covers, round up, then the cost.
