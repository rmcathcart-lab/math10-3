The Birthday Bet

There are 23 people in a room. There are 365 days in a year.

Two people “match” if they have the same birthday (same month and day, any year).

What is the chance that at least two of the 23 people match?

Aabout 6%
Babout 25%
Cabout 50%
Dabout 99%
C: about 50%. It is a coin flip! It feels too high because we think “does someone match me?” But any two people can match. 23 people make 23\times22\div2=253 pairs, and that is a lot of chances. With 30 people the chance is about 70%; with 57 people it is 99%. Let’s test it: call out birthday months, then days. Do we have a match?

Similar polygons and scale factor6.1Similar figures have the same shape: matching (corresponding) angles are equal and matching sides are in the same ratio. Name them in matching order: ABCD\sim PQRS means A\leftrightarrow P, B\leftrightarrow Q, … Scale factor k=\text{new length}\div\text{original length}. New =k\times original; original = new \div\,k. k>1: enlargement; k<1: reduction; k=1: same size (congruent).A 4 cm side becomes 10 cm:
k=10\div4=2.5 (enlargement)
A 6 cm side becomes
2.5\times6=15 cm
Are they similar?6.2Check both: (1) every pair of corresponding angles is equal, and (2) every side ratio (new ÷ original) is the same number. One different ratio or one different angle means not similar. Regular polygons with the same number of sides are always similar.3 × 5 and 6 × 9 rectangles:
6\div3=2, 9\div5=1.8
Ratios differ: not similar
Drawing enlargements and reductions6.3Multiply every length by k and keep every angle the same. Grid method: multiply each side (in squares) by k. Projection method: from a point O, OA'=k\times OA along each ray.k=1.5: a 4-square side becomes 1.5\times4=6 squares; a 90° corner stays 90°
Scale diagrams: maps and floor plans6.4A scale can be written in words (1 cm represents 5 m), as a ratio with the same units on both sides (1:500), or as a bar scale. Real = diagram × scale number; diagram = real ÷ scale number. Change to the same unit first (1 m = 100 cm, 1 km = 100 000 cm).Plan at 1:500, a wall 3.2 cm long:
3.2\times500=1\,600 cm = 16 m
Similar triangles6.5Two triangles are similar if two pairs of angles are equal (AA): the third angles match because each triangle adds to 180°. Right triangles that share an acute angle are similar. A small triangle cut off by a line parallel to one side is similar to the big one. Then (side in big) ÷ (matching side in small) is the same number k for every pair.Small: 3 cm and 5 cm; big: 4.5 cm and x
k=4.5\div3=1.5
x=1.5\times5=7.5 cm
Indirect measurement6.6Shadows: at the same time of day, height ÷ shadow is the same for every object. Mirror: the angle in equals the angle out, so eye height ÷ (you to mirror) = object height ÷ (mirror to object).1.6 m person, 2 m shadow; a tree’s shadow is 15 m:
h=1.6\div2\times15=12 m
Remember. Match the sides in the same order as the names (AB\leftrightarrow PQ). Put the same figure on top in every ratio (big over small, or small over big). Lengths change by k; angles never change. Change to the same unit before you use a scale. Check: an enlargement must give bigger lengths, a reduction smaller ones.
★
Bonus fact. When the lengths are multiplied by k, the area is multiplied by k\times k. Double every side of a 3 m by 4 m patio and you get 6 m by 8 m: 48\div12=4 times the area, not 2 times.

Why is the area k\times k times as big? Count the copies

Similar Polygons and Scale Factor

Example 1

ABCD ∼ WXYZ

ABCD\sim WXYZ. (The diagrams are not to scale.)

(a) Write the side of WXYZ that matches each side of ABCD, and the angle that matches \angle B.
AB\leftrightarrow WXBC\leftrightarrow XYCD\leftrightarrow YZDA\leftrightarrow ZW\angle B\leftrightarrow \angle X
Match the letters in order: the 2nd letters are B and X, so \angle B\leftrightarrow\angle X.
(b) Find the scale factor from ABCD to WXYZ. Is it an enlargement or a reduction?
Use a pair of sides where both lengths are known: AB=6 and WX=9.
k=\text{new}\div\text{original}=9\div6=1.5
k>1, so it is an enlargement.
(c) Find XY, ZW and \angle X.
New =k\times original:
XY=1.5\times4=6 cm, ZW=1.5\times8=12 cm
Angles do not change: \angle X=\angle B=81^\circ.
(d) Find CD.
CD is on the original, so divide:
CD=YZ\div k=7.5\div1.5=5 cm
Check: 1.5\times5=7.5 ✓
New ÷ original: 9÷6ENTER gives 1.5. Going back to the original: 7.5÷1.5ENTER gives 5.

Think: what is the scale factor from WXYZ back to ABCD? (6\div9\approx0.67: a reduction. Going the other way flips the division.)

Are They Similar?

Example 2

Flower beds, a photo print, a square and a rhombus

(a) Two flower beds are shaped like trapezoids. Are they similar? Show both tests.
Sides (Bed 2 ÷ Bed 1)Ratio
bottom: 7.5\div51.5
top: 4.5\div31.5
left: 3\div21.5
right: 3\div21.5
Angles: both beds have 60°, 60°, 120°, 120° in the same places, so the angles match.
Sides: every ratio is 1.5. Both tests pass, so the beds are similar (k=1.5).
(b) Bree orders a 10 in by 12 in print of a 6 in by 9 in photo. Are the two rectangles similar? What will happen to her picture?
All the angles are 90°. Check the sides:
short: 10\div6\approx1.67; long: 12\div9\approx1.33
The ratios are different, so they are not similar. The print has a different shape, so part of the photo will be cut off (cropped).
(c) A square and a rhombus both have 5 cm sides. The rhombus has angles of 60° and 120°. Are they similar? Explain.
The sides pass: every ratio is 5\div5=1.
The angles fail: the square’s angles are 90°, the rhombus’s are 60° and 120°.
So they are not similar: both tests are needed.
Compare the ratios: 10÷6ENTER gives 1.666666667 and 12÷9ENTER gives 1.333333333. Different numbers: not similar.

Drawing Enlargements and Reductions

Example 3

A sign on a grid, and a triangle by projection

(a) Enlarge the sign on the left with a scale factor of 2. Draw it on the grid on the right. (Tap the grid points to draw it, then press Check.)
Count each side in squares and multiply by 2: bottom 2\to4, left side 2\to4, top 2\to4. The point of the arrow is 1 right and 1 up from a corner, so it becomes 2 right and 2 up.
Check: every angle is the same as before (90° at the two left corners and at the tip, 135° at the other two corners). Only the lengths changed.
(b) Use the projection method from point O with k=2.5. Find OA', OB' and OC', then draw \triangle A'B'C'. Here OA=2 cm, OB=3 cm and OC=2.4 cm.
Multiply each distance from O by k:
OA'=2.5\times2=5 cm
OB'=2.5\times3=7.5 cm
OC'=2.5\times2.4=6 cm
Mark each point on its ray and join them. Check: each side of \triangle A'B'C' should measure 2.5 times the matching side of \triangle ABC.

Scale Diagrams

Example 4

A trail map in Kananaskis

The route goes from the trailhead P to Q, then R, then the lake shore at S. In the booklet the map is printed at real size, so you can measure it. (Here, drag the ends of the yellow tape to measure.)

(a) Write the scale in words and as a ratio.
From the bar: 1 cm represents 0.2 km.
Same units: 0.2\text{ km}=0.2\times100\,000=20\,000 cm
Ratio: 1:20\,000
(b) Measure the route. PQ=2.4 cm, QR=3.1 cm and RS=1.5 cm. How far is the hike from P to S?
Map total: 2.4+3.1+1.5=7 cm
Real: 7\times0.2=1.4 km
The hike is 1.4 km.
(c) A sign says the lake is 0.56 km long. How long should the lake be on the map? Measure to check.
Diagram = real ÷ scale: 0.56\div0.2=2.8 cm. The lake on the map measures 2.8 cm ✓

Example 5

A bedroom and a garage on a floor plan

(a) A bedroom is 3.8 m by 4.4 m. Find its size on a floor plan with a scale of 1:50.
Same units: 3.8 m = 380 cm, 4.4 m = 440 cm
Diagram = real ÷ 50:
380\div50=7.6 cm, 440\div50=8.8 cm
The bedroom is 7.6 cm by 8.8 cm on the plan.
(b) A garage is 12 m by 9 m. The space for the drawing is 20 cm by 15 cm. Which scale should you use: 1:50, 1:100 or 1:200? Explain.
12 m = 1 200 cm, 9 m = 900 cm
1:50: 24 cm by 18 cm (too big)
1:100: 12 cm by 9 cm (fits)
1:200: 6 cm by 4.5 cm (fits, but small)
Use 1:100: it fits and is as large as possible.
!
Watch out. A ratio scale like 1:50 has the same units on both sides. Change metres to centimetres before you divide by 50.

Similar Triangles

Example 6

Matching angles, and a shed roof

(a) In \triangle KLM, \angle K=47^\circ and \angle L=68^\circ. In \triangle PQR, \angle P=65^\circ and \angle R=47^\circ. Are the triangles similar? If they are, name them in matching order.
Find the third angles: \angle M=180^\circ-47^\circ-68^\circ=65^\circ; \angle Q=180^\circ-65^\circ-47^\circ=68^\circ.
The angles match: K\leftrightarrow R (47°), L\leftrightarrow Q (68°), M\leftrightarrow P (65°). Similar: \triangle KLM\sim\triangle RQP.
(b) The side of a shed roof is a right triangle. A post DE stands straight up, so it is parallel to the end wall BC. Explain why \triangle ADE\sim\triangle ACB.
Both have a right angle (\angle D=\angle C=90^\circ), and they share \angle A. Two pairs of equal angles, so they are similar (AA). (Also: DE\parallel BC, so \angle AED=\angle ABC as corresponding angles.)
(c) Find the height of the wall, BC.
k=AC\div AD=6\div2.4=2.5
BC=2.5\times1.5=3.75 m. The wall is 3.75 m tall.
(d) Another post will stand 4.2 m from A. How tall must it be, to the nearest tenth of a metre?
This post makes another similar triangle: \dfrac{\text{height}}{4.2}=\dfrac{1.5}{2.4}
height =1.5\div2.4\times4.2=2.625\approx2.6 m. Check: it is between 1.5 m and 3.75 m ✓
One line on the calculator: 1.5÷2.4×4.2ENTER gives 2.625. Round only at the end: 2.6 m.

Indirect Measurement: Shadows and Mirrors

Calculator: to find 1.6\div2.4\times33, press 1.6÷2.4×33ENTER. Keep the full value until the last step.
Example 7

The grain elevator’s shadow

At 3:00 in the afternoon, Priya is 1.6 m tall and casts a shadow 2.4 m long. At the same time, a grain elevator casts a shadow 33 m long. How tall is the elevator?

The sun’s rays are parallel and both objects stand straight up, so the triangles are similar.
\dfrac{h}{33}=\dfrac{1.6}{2.4}
h=1.6\div2.4\times33=22 m
Check: the elevator’s shadow is longer than its height, just like Priya’s ✓ The elevator is 22 m tall.
Example 8

The light pole and the mirror

Jordan wants the height of a light pole at the football field. He puts a mirror on the ground 12.6 m from the pole and steps back until he sees the top of the pole in the mirror. He is then 1.8 m from the mirror, and his eyes are 1.4 m above the ground.

(a) Why are the two triangles similar?
Jordan and the pole both make right angles with the ground, and the angle in equals the angle out at the mirror. Two pairs of equal angles (AA).
(b) Find the height of the light pole.
\dfrac{h}{12.6}=\dfrac{1.4}{1.8}
h=1.4\div1.8\times12.6=9.8 m. The light pole is 9.8 m tall.
Same pattern: 1.4÷1.8×12.6ENTER gives 9.8.

Think: Jordan steps a little farther back. Can he still see the top of the pole in the same spot of the mirror? (No. The angle out gets smaller, so he would see a point lower down. He must stand where the angles match.)

Key ideas

Scale factor. k=\text{new}\div\text{original}. New = k\,\times original; original = new ÷ k. k>1 enlarges, k<1 reduces. Match the parts in name order. Angles never change.
Similar or not? Both tests: equal angles and equal side ratios. Draw an image by multiplying every side (grid) or every distance from O (projection) by k.
Scales. Same units first. Real = diagram × scale number; diagram = real ÷ scale number. Choose the biggest scale that fits the paper.
Similar triangles. Two equal angles (AA) is enough. Shadows at the same time and a mirror on the ground both make similar triangles: write the proportion, then solve.
