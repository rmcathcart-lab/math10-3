Pick a Table

You show up late to a pizza party.

Table A: 3 friends share 2 pizzas.
Table B: 5 friends share 3 pizzas.
Each table shares its pizza equally.

Which table should you sit at to get the most pizza?

?
Which table did the person beside you pick? Why?
It doesn’t matter! Right now, Table A gets more each: 2\div3\approx0.67 of a pizza, and Table B gets 3\div5=0.6. But once you sit down, Table A is 4 people for 2 pizzas and Table B is 6 people for 3 pizzas. That’s half a pizza each at either table.

Ratios

A ratio compares two amounts, like 1 can of juice concentrate to 4 cans of water. Today we write ratios, make them as simple as possible, scale a recipe up or down, and share money fairly.

I can…
  • write a ratio three ways, and tell a part-to-part ratio from a part-to-whole ratio
  • write a ratio in simplest form, even with decimals or different units
  • use a ratio table to make a mix or a recipe bigger or smaller
  • share an amount in a given ratio
N1 Solve problems that involve unit pricing and currency exchange, using proportional reasoning.
1

What Is a Ratio?

A ratio compares two amounts measured in the same units. The numbers in a ratio are its terms.
Three ways to write a ratio
with a colon3:2
in words3 to 2
as a fraction\dfrac{3}{2}
All three are read “3 to 2”. Order matters: the first amount named goes first (or on top).
Part-to-part compares one part of a group with another part.
goldfish : guppies
Part-to-whole compares one part with the whole group. To find the whole, add all the parts.
goldfish : all the fish
!
3:2 and 2:3 are different ratios. Read the question: “guppies to goldfish” means guppies first. Also, never change a ratio like \tfrac{5}{3} into a mixed number (1\tfrac{2}{3}). A ratio compares two numbers, so keep both.
Example 1

The fish tank

A fish tank at a pet store has 5 goldfish, 3 guppies and 4 tetras. Complete the table.

ComparisonColonWordsFractionPart-to-part or part-to-whole?
goldfish to guppies5:35 to 3\dfrac{5}{3}part-to-part
guppies to goldfish3:53 to 5\dfrac{3}{5}part-to-part
tetras to all fish4:124 to 12\dfrac{4}{12}part-to-whole
fish that are not tetras to tetras8:48 to 4\dfrac{8}{4}part-to-part
All fish: 5+3+4=12. Not tetras: 5+3=8 (or 12-4=8). Tap any cell to check just that one.
Think The store adds 2 more tetras. Which ratios in the table change? Which one stays the same?
Now there are 6 tetras and 14 fish. Tetras to all fish becomes 6:14 and not-tetras to tetras becomes 8:6. Goldfish to guppies (5:3) and guppies to goldfish (3:5) don’t change.
Extra

A bag of apples

A bag from the farmers’ market has 7 red apples and 5 green apples.

(a) green to red, three ways
5:7, 5 to 7, \dfrac{5}{7} (part-to-part)
(b) red to all the apples
All: 7+5=12
\boldsymbol{7:12} (part-to-whole)
(c) all the apples to green
\boldsymbol{12:5}. The whole comes first this time; it is still part-to-whole.

Simplifying Ratios

Equivalent ratios compare the same way, like 2:4 and 1:2.
A ratio is in simplest form when its terms are whole numbers with no common factor except 1.
To simplify a ratio: divide both terms by the greatest common factor (GCF), the biggest number that divides into both.
12:18\;\to\;12\div6:18\div6\;\to\;2:3
If you can’t spot the GCF, divide by any common factor and repeat.
The TI-30XIIS simplifies fractions for you. For 12:18, type 12A b/c18=. The screen shows 2┘3, so the ratio is 2:3.
If it shows a mixed number like 2┘2┘3, press 2ndd/e (above A b/c) to see 8┘3, which means 8:3.

Can’t spot the GCF? Divide, then divide again

Any common factor is a good start. Keep dividing both terms until the only common factor left is 1. You end up in the same place as dividing by the GCF once.

Decimals and different units

The terms of a simplified ratio must be whole numbers, and both amounts must be in the same unit.

Decimals: multiply both terms by 10 or 100 to make whole numbers, then simplify.
0.5:2\;\to\;5:20\;\to\;1:4
Different units: change both amounts to the same unit (usually the smaller one), drop the units, then simplify.
1 h = 60 min1 min = 60 s1 kg = 1 000 g1 L = 1 000 mL1 m = 100 cm
Think Why not just write 45 min : 2 h as 45:2?
45:2 makes the 45 min look more than 20 times as long as 2 h. Really it is less than half as long: 45 min out of 120 min.

A drink is a mix of parts

Mix concentrate with water and you get two ratios: concentrate to water (part-to-part) and concentrate to the whole drink (part-to-whole).

Example 4

Iced tea

A 2 L jug of iced tea is made with 400 mL of iced tea concentrate. The rest is water.

(a) Write the ratio of concentrate to the whole drink in simplest form.
2 L = 2 000 mL
400:2\,000, GCF 400: \boldsymbol{1:5}
(b) How much water is in the jug?
2\,000-400=1\,600 mL of water (1.6 L)
(c) Write the ratio of concentrate to water in simplest form.
400:1\,600, GCF 400: \boldsymbol{1:4}
(d) Which ratio, (a) or (c), is part-to-whole?
(a) is part-to-whole: it compares the concentrate with the whole drink.

Equivalent Ratios and Mixing Recipes

Multiply (or divide) both terms of a ratio by the same number to get an equivalent ratio. A ratio table lists equivalent ratios side by side.

Equivalent ratios: a:b=(a\times n):(b\times n)
1:4=2:8=3:12
Two ratios are equivalent if they simplify to the same simplest form.
3:12=1:4 and 5:20=1:4, so they are equivalent.

Mixing instructions often say “1 part to 4 parts”. A part can be any size (a cup, a can, a bucket) as long as every part in the mix uses the same size.

When two ratios are equal, you can write them as an equation called a proportion. You will solve these in Lesson 1.4.

Example 5

Frozen lemonade

A can of frozen lemonade concentrate says: mix 1 can of concentrate with 4 cans of water.

(a) Complete the ratio table.
Cans of concentrate12358
Cans of water48122032
Water is always 4\times the concentrate: 2\times4=8, 3\times4=12, 8\times4=32. Concentrate is water \div4: 20\div4=5.
(b) For a party, Lucas mixes 6 cans of concentrate with 20 cans of water. Is this the same as the instructions? Will his lemonade taste stronger or weaker?
6:20 simplifies to 3:10, not 1:4, so it is not the same.
With 6 cans of concentrate, the instructions need 6\times4=24 cans of water. Lucas used only 20, so his lemonade is stronger.
Extra

Equivalent or not?

Simplify both ratios. Are they equivalent?

(a) 6:15 and 4:10
6:15=2:5 and 4:10=2:5
Yes, equivalent.
(b) 9:12 and 12:15
9:12=3:4 but 12:15=4:5
No, not equivalent. (Adding 3 to both terms does not keep a ratio the same.)

Stronger or weaker? Compare with the same amount of concentrate

Scale the instructions up to the same number of cans of concentrate. Less water than that: stronger. More water: weaker.

Think Lucas’s lemonade is too strong. What could he add to fix it?
4 more cans of water. Then he has 6:24=1:4, just like the instructions.

Ratios with three terms

A ratio can compare three amounts, like 1:2:3. Multiply or divide all three terms by the same number. The whole is all the parts added: 1+2+3=6 parts.

Example 6

A patio of concrete

A concrete mix for a backyard patio uses cement, sand and gravel in the ratio 1:2:3 (by buckets).

(a) Marco uses 4 buckets of cement. How much sand and gravel does he need?
Multiply every term by 4:
1:2:3=4:8:12
8 buckets of sand and 12 buckets of gravel.
(b) Marco has only 15 buckets of gravel. How much cement and sand go with it?
Gravel: 3\times5=15, so multiply by 5:
1:2:3=5:10:15
5 buckets of cement and 10 buckets of sand.
(c) What is the ratio of cement to the whole mix?
Whole: 1+2+3=6 parts
cement : whole =\boldsymbol{1:6}
(d) How many buckets of mix does (b) make?
5+10+15=\boldsymbol{30} buckets
Math Historyabout 25 BCE

A 2000-year-old mixing ratio

The Roman builder Vitruvius wrote a famous book on how to build. His recipe for mortar, the paste that holds stones and bricks together, is a ratio: 3 parts sand to 1 part lime for sand dug from a pit, and 2 parts sand to 1 part lime for river or sea sand.

Roman concrete was built from mortar like this. The concrete dome of the Pantheon in Rome, finished about 1900 years ago, is still the biggest dome in the world made of concrete with no steel inside: about 43 m across.

A Roman builder has 12 baskets of pit sand. How many baskets of lime should he use?
sand : lime =3:1=12:4 (multiply by 4), so 4 baskets of lime.

Sharing in a Ratio

Sometimes a total (money, a mixture) must be split in a given ratio. Split it into equal parts first.

Sharing an amount in the ratio a:b
1Add the terms to get the total number of parts: a+b.
2Find one part: total amount \div total number of parts.
3Multiply one part by each term: a\times (one part) and b\times (one part).
4Check: the shares add up to the total.
!
For 3:5, do not divide by 3 or by 5. Divide by 3+5=8, the total number of parts.
Estimate first, then use the calculator. Owen gets 5 of the 8 parts, a bit more than half of $120, so a bit more than $60. Then find one part and multiply: 120÷8= gives 15, and 5×15= gives 75.

Why not divide by 3 or by 5?

Try it and check: the shares must add up to the total. Dividing by one term gives shares that add up to far too much.

Example 7

Sharing money

(a) Jaya and Owen earn $120 shovelling driveways. Owen shovelled more, so they share the money in the ratio 3:5 (Jaya : Owen). How much does each get?
Step 1: 3+5=8 parts
Step 2: one part =\$120\div8=\$15
Step 3: Jaya 3\times\$15=\$45; Owen 5\times\$15=\$75
Step 4: \$45+\$75=\$120 ✓
Jaya gets $45 and Owen gets $75.
(b) Ellie worked 6 h and Ravi worked 9 h at a car wash. They share $187.50 in tips in the ratio of the hours they worked. How much does each get?
Ratio 6:9=2:3 (or use 6+9=15 parts)
Step 1: 2+3=5 parts
Step 2: one part =\$187.50\div5=\$37.50
Step 3: Ellie 2\times\$37.50=\$75.00; Ravi 3\times\$37.50=\$112.50
Step 4: \$75.00+\$112.50=\$187.50 ✓
Ellie gets $75.00 and Ravi gets $112.50.
Think Jaya says, “The ratio is 3:5, so I get \tfrac35 of the money.” Is she right?
No. 3:5 is part-to-part (Jaya to Owen). Jaya gets 3 of the 8 parts, so \tfrac38 of the money: \tfrac38 of $120 is $45.
Example 8

Washer fluid

Windshield washer fluid is mixed 1 part concentrate to 5 parts water. Nadia wants to fill a 4.5 L jug. How much concentrate and how much water does she need?
Step 1: 1+5=6 parts
Step 2: one part =4.5\div6=0.75 L
Step 3: concentrate 1\times0.75=0.75 L; water 5\times0.75=3.75 L
Step 4: 0.75+3.75=4.5 L ✓
Nadia needs 0.75 L (750 mL) of concentrate and 3.75 L of water.
Extra

Sharing three ways

Kai, Liv and Sam work a booth at the Calgary Stampede. They share $90 in tips in the ratio 1:2:3. How much does each get?
Step 1: 1+2+3=6 parts
Step 2: one part =\$90\div6=\$15
Step 3: Kai 1\times\$15=\$15; Liv 2\times\$15=\$30; Sam 3\times\$15=\$45
Step 4: \$15+\$30+\$45=\$90 ✓
✓

Key ideas

A ratio compares two amounts in the same units: 3:2, 3 to 2, or \tfrac32. Order matters. Part-to-whole: add all the parts to get the whole.
Simplest form: divide both terms by the GCF. Decimals: \times10 or \times100 first. Different units: change to the same unit first.
Equivalent ratios: multiply or divide every term by the same number. A ratio table shows them side by side.
Sharing in a:b: total parts a+b, one part = total ÷ parts, multiply by each term, check the shares add up.
