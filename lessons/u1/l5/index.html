The Snail in the Well

A snail is at the bottom of a well that is 10 m deep.

Every day it climbs up 3 m. Every night it slides back down 2 m.

How many days does it take the snail to get out of the well?

?
Guess first. Then convince the person beside you.
8 days! Most people say 10. Each full day and night, the snail gains only 1 m. After 7 days and 7 nights it is 7 m up. On day 8 it climbs 3 m, reaches the top (10 m), and crawls out. It never slides back that night. The snail’s “rate” is not the same all the time. Today: rates that stay the same.

Rates and Unit Rates

Your pay is dollars per hour. A speed limit is kilometres per hour. Gas is dollars per litre. These are all rates. Today we find the rate for one unit, then use it to predict, compare and plan a road trip.

I can…
  • tell a rate from a ratio, and write a rate with its units
  • find a unit rate, like $16.65/h or 65 words/min
  • use a unit rate to predict an amount, or how many hours or cans I need
  • find and use fuel consumption in L/100 km
  • compare rates, and convert a rate to new units
N1 Solve problems that involve unit pricing and currency exchange, using proportional reasoning.
1

What Is a Rate?

A rate compares two quantities that have different units. “90 km in 1 h”, “$45 for 3 h of work” and “120 heartbeats in 2 min” are all rates.

A ratio (Lesson 3) compares quantities with the same unit, like 2 cups of flour to 3 cups of milk. In a rate the units are different, so you must always write them.

in words$45 for 3 h
as a fraction\dfrac{\$45}{3\text{ h}}
with a slash$45/3 h

The slash “/” is read as per. “Per” means “for each” or “for every”.

Key idea   Rate: a comparison of two quantities with different units.   Ratio: a comparison of quantities with the same unit.
A rate always keeps its units:   72 pages/3 min  ·  $6.60/2 kg  ·  15 L/2 min

Unit Rates

A unit rate is a rate for one unit of the second quantity. “75 km/h” is a unit rate: it means 75 km in 1 hour.

To find a unit rate, divide the first quantity by the second quantity. This is the same as dividing the top and bottom of the rate by the bottom number:

\dfrac{\$45}{3\text{ h}}=\dfrac{\$45\div3}{3\text{ h}\div3}=\dfrac{\$15}{1\text{ h}}=\$15\text{/h}
Key idea   Unit rate = first quantity ÷ second quantity
The answer is the amount for 1 unit.
Speedkm/hkilometres travelled in 1 hour
Wage (pay)$/hdollars earned in 1 hour
Typing speedwords/minwords typed in 1 minute
Heart ratebeats/minheartbeats in 1 minute
!
Watch out. Divide in the right order. The unit after “per” goes on the bottom: dollars per hour means dollars ÷ hours. If Jordan earns $133.20 in 8 h, then 8\div133.20=0.06 makes no sense, but 133.20\div8=16.65 gives a sensible wage of $16.65/h.
Example 2

Find the unit rate

Find each unit rate. Include the units.

(a) A cyclist rides 54 km in 3 h.
54\div3=18. The unit rate is 18 km/h.
(b) Jordan earns $133.20 for an 8-hour shift at a hardware store.
133.20\div8=16.65. The unit rate is $16.65/h.
(c) Mei types 390 words in 6 min.
390\div6=65. The unit rate is 65 words/min.
(d) A tap fills a 24 L bucket in 1.5 min.
24\div1.5=16. The unit rate is 16 L/min.

Example 3

Taking your pulse

To find your heart rate, press two fingers on the side of your neck and count the beats. Counting for a whole minute is slow, so people often count for 15 s and multiply by 4 (because 15\text{ s}\times4=60\text{ s}), or count for 20 s and multiply by 3.

(a) At rest, Aiden counts 18 beats in 15 s. What is his heart rate in beats per minute?
60 s is 4\times15 s, so multiply both by 4: 18\times4=72. His heart rate is 72 beats/min.
(b) After running up the stairs, he counts 37 beats in 20 s. What is his heart rate now?
60 s is 3\times20 s, so multiply both by 3: 37\times3=111. His heart rate is 111 beats/min.

Using a Unit Rate to Predict

Once you know the amount for 1 unit, you can find the amount for any number of units. You can also work backwards to find how many units (how many hours, minutes or cans) you need.

Amount = unit rate × number of units
Number of units = amount ÷ unit rate

You can also set up a proportion (Lesson 4). Put the same units in the same places, for example dollars on top and hours on the bottom of both fractions.

Example 4

Kiran’s pay

Kiran works at a garden centre. He earned $247.50 for 15 h of work. At this rate, how much will he earn for 22 h? Estimate first, then solve two ways.

Estimate
About \$16\text{/h}\times22\text{ h}\approx\$350
Method 1: unit rate
Step 1: unit rate =247.50\div15=\$16.50\text{/h}
Step 2: 16.50\times22=363
Kiran will earn $363.00 for 22 h.
Method 2: proportion
\dfrac{\$247.50}{15\text{ h}}=\dfrac{\$x}{22\text{ h}}
15x=247.50\times22=5445
x=5445\div15=363   ($363.00, close to the estimate)
Method 1 on the calculator: the unit rate first, then times 22.
Example 5

Typing and the deli

(a) Talia types 48 words/min. How long will it take her to type a 1200-word essay?
This asks for the number of minutes, so divide: 1200\div48=25. It will take her 25 min. (Check: 48\times25=1200.)
(b) At the deli, smoked turkey costs $2.38 per 100 g. How much will 450 g cost?
The rate is for 100 g. 450\div100=4.5, so 450 g is 4.5 groups of 100 g. 4.5\times2.38=10.71. 450 g will cost $10.71.
→
Coming up. A rate that gives a price for an amount, like $2.38 per 100 g, is called a unit price. In Lesson 6 you will use unit prices to find the best buy.

Units must match, and sometimes you round up

!
Watch out. The units must match before you multiply or divide. If a rate is per minute and the time is in hours, change the hours to minutes first (1 h = 60 min). And when you must buy whole items (cans, bags, boxes), round up: 4.2 cans means you need 5 cans, because 4 cans are not enough.
Example 6

A bottling machine and a paint job

(a) A machine at a juice plant fills 270 bottles in 45 min. How many bottles can it fill in a 7.5-hour shift?
Step 1: unit rate =270\div45=6 bottles/min
Step 2: change hours to minutes: 7.5\times60=450 min
Step 3: 6\times450=2700
It can fill 2700 bottles in one shift.
(b) One can of paint covers 35 m². Ty needs to paint 160 m² of walls. How many cans should he buy?
160\div35=4.57\ldots cans
4 cans cover only 4\times35=140 m², which is not enough.
Round up: Ty should buy 5 cans.
Think In (b), the answer 4.57 is closer to 5 than to 4. What if it were 4.1 cans? Would Ty still buy 5?
Yes. Even 4.1 cans means 4 cans are not enough. With whole items you always round up, not to the nearest.

Fuel Economy: Litres per 100 km

In Canada, the fuel a vehicle uses is given as a rate in litres per 100 km (L/100 km). It tells you how many litres of gas the vehicle burns to drive 100 km.

Why per 100 km and not per 1 km? A car burns only a tiny amount of gas in 1 km, such as 0.078 L. Numbers like that are hard to read and compare. For 100 km the same car uses 7.8 L, a friendlier number. A lower number means the vehicle uses less fuel, which is better.

Fuel consumption (L/100 km) = litres used ÷ km driven × 100
Fuel needed (L) = fuel consumption × km to drive ÷ 100
Calculator. For 55.2 L used in 480 km, press 55.2 ÷ 480 × 100 ENTER to get 11.5.
Example 7

Dana’s pickup truck

On a road trip, Dana’s pickup truck used 55.2 L of gas to travel 480 km. What is its fuel consumption in L/100 km?
Estimate: 480 km is about 5\times100 km, and 55\div5=11, so about 11 L/100 km.
55.2\div480\times100=11.5. Dana’s truck uses 11.5 L/100 km.
Example 8

A trip to Banff

Calgary to Banff is about 130 km each way. Sam’s car uses 7.4 L/100 km, and gas costs $1.42/L.

(a) How many litres of gas will Sam need for the round trip (there and back)?
Round trip =2\times130=260 km
7.4\times260\div100=19.24
Sam needs 19.24 L of gas.
(b) How much will the gas cost?
19.24\times1.42=27.3208
Round to the cent at the end.
The gas will cost $27.32.
Keep every digit until the end, then round to the cent.
Think Sam’s friend has a truck that uses 11.5 L/100 km. Would the Banff trip cost more or less in the truck? Explain without calculating.
More. 11.5 is higher than 7.4, so the truck burns more litres for the same 260 km, and every litre costs $1.42.

(a) How many litres of gas does Kim need for the round trip?
Round trip =2\times210=420 km
6.5\times420\div100=27.3. Kim needs 27.3 L.

Comparing and Converting Rates

To compare two rates, write both as unit rates with the same units. Then compare the numbers.

Think about what “better” means. For pay or typing speed, higher is better.
For fuel consumption, lower is better: the vehicle burns less gas.
Example 9

Who is faster? Which uses less?

(a) Noah types 504 words in 7 min. Priya types 620 words in 8 min. Who types faster?
Noah: 504\div7=72 words/min
Priya: 620\div8=77.5 words/min
Priya types faster, by 5.5 words/min.
(b) An SUV used 46.8 L of gas for 520 km. A hatchback used 31.5 L for 450 km. Which uses less fuel?
SUV: 46.8\div520\times100=9.0 L/100 km
Hatchback: 31.5\div450\times100=7.0 L/100 km
The hatchback uses less fuel, because 7.0 is lower than 9.0.
Think Priya typed more words (620) than Noah (504). Is that enough to say she is faster?
No. She also typed for longer (8 min, not 7). To be fair, compare the words in 1 minute: the unit rates.

Converting a rate to new units

To convert a rate to new units, change one unit at a time: first the top unit, then the bottom unit.

Key idea   1 km = 1000 m  ·  1 h = 60 min  ·  1 min = 60 s  ·  $1 = 100 cents
km/h to m/min: × 1000 (km to m), then ÷ 60 (per hour to per minute)
m/min to km/h: × 60 (per minute to per hour), then ÷ 1000 (m to km)
Example 10

Converting rates

(a) Lena walks at 4.8 km/h. Write her speed in m/min.
4.8 km =4.8\times1000=4800 m, so 4800 m in 60 min.
4800\div60=80
Lena walks 80 m/min.
(b) An escalator moves at 30 m/min. Write its speed in km/h.
In 1 h (60 min): 30\times60=1800 m
1800 m =1800\div1000=1.8 km
The escalator moves at 1.8 km/h.
(c) Ethan earns $18.60/h. How much is that per minute?
1 h = 60 min
18.60\div60=0.31
He earns $0.31/min (31 cents).
Think How can you check (a)? Lena walks 80 m every minute. How far is that in an hour?
80\times60=4800 m =4.8 km in an hour. That matches 4.8 km/h ✓
Extra

Pay by the shift or by the hour?

A gas station pays $142.00 for an 8-hour shift. A car wash pays $17.95/h.

(a) Which job pays more per hour?
Gas station: 142.00\div8=\$17.75\text{/h}. The car wash pays more: $17.95/h is more than $17.75/h.
(b) How much more would you earn at the car wash in an 8-hour shift?
17.95\times8=143.60, and 143.60-142.00=\$1.60 more.
✓

Key ideas

Rate: two quantities with different units (72 pages/3 min). Ratio: the same unit. A rate always keeps its units. “/” means per.
Unit rate = first quantity ÷ second quantity: the amount for 1 unit. The unit after “per” goes on the bottom.
Predict: amount = unit rate × units; units = amount ÷ unit rate. Make the units match first; round up for whole items.
L/100 km = litres ÷ km × 100 (lower is better). Compare unit rates in the same units. Convert one unit at a time.
