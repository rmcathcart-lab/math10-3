Warm-up

Loonies or Toonies?

Bag A is full of loonies ($1 coins). Bag B is full of toonies ($2 coins).

Each bag weighs exactly 1 kg. A toonie is a bit heavier than a loonie.

Which bag is worth more money? Guess how much each bag is worth.

?
Got a guess? A loonie is about 6.3 g and a toonie is about 6.9 g. How many coins fit in 1 kg?
The toonies win, by a lot. 1 kg is 1 000 g. Loonies: 1\,000\div6.3\approx159 coins, so about $159. Toonies: 1\,000\div6.9\approx145 coins, so about $290. There are fewer toonies, but each one is worth twice as much. Coins per gram, dollars per coin: that is unit rates, from Unit 1.

Ratios, Proportions and Rates

Example 1

Simplest form, and sharing the snow money

(a) Write each ratio in simplest form.
(i) 36:48 (ii) 90 cm : 1.5 m
(i) Divide both by 12: 36:48=\mathbf{3:4}
(ii) 1.5\text{ m}=150 cm, so 90:150. Divide both by 30: \mathbf{3:5}
(b) Priya and Jordan shovelled snow for $250. Priya worked 6 h and Jordan worked 4 h, so they split the money in the ratio 6:4. How much does each person get?
6+4=10 parts. One part: \$250\div10=\$25
Priya: 6\times\$25=\$150; Jordan: 4\times\$25=\$100
Check: 150+100=250. Priya gets $150 and Jordan gets $100.

Example 2

Iced tea and a road map

(a) A café’s iced tea uses 3 cups of tea concentrate for every 8 cups of water. How much concentrate goes with 20 cups of water?
\dfrac{\text{concentrate}}{\text{water}}:\ \dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{x}{20}
Cross-multiply: 8x=3\times20=60
x=60\div8=7.5. Use 7.5 cups of concentrate.
(b) A road map of southern Alberta has a scale of 1 cm : 12 km. Calgary and Drumheller are 11.5 cm apart on the map. About how far apart are they really?
\dfrac{\text{map (cm)}}{\text{real (km)}}:\ \dfrac{1}{12}=\dfrac{11.5}{x}
Scale up: \times11.5 on top, so \times11.5 on the bottom.
x=12\times11.5=138. They are about 138 km apart.

Think: in (a), what is the ratio of concentrate to the whole drink? (3:11, because 3+8=11 cups in all.)

Check Example 2

Example 3

Mateo’s truck

Mateo’s truck used 54 L of gas to drive 600 km.

(a) What is the truck’s fuel economy in L/100 km?
54\div600\times100=9
The truck uses 9 L/100 km.
(b) Mateo drives 970 km from Calgary to Vancouver. Gas costs $1.42/L. Estimate the cost of gas, then calculate it.
Fuel: 970 km is 9.7 hundreds of km. 9.7\times9=87.3 L
Estimate: 90\text{ L}\times\$1.40\approx\$126
Cost: 87.3\times1.42=123.966\approx\$123.97
Gas for the trip costs about $123.97.
One line on the TI-30XIIS: 9.7×9×1.42ENTER gives 123.966. The calculator keeps every digit; round to the cent only at the end.

Finding the Best Buy

Example 4

Three bags of coffee

A Calgary grocery store sells ground coffee in three sizes.

Size340 g925 g1.36 kg
Price$8.99$19.49$27.99
Unit price (per 100 g)$2.644$2.107$2.058
(a) Complete the table. Which size is the best buy?
8.99\div340\times100=2.644, 19.49\div925\times100=2.107
1.36 kg =1\,360 g: 27.99\div1\,360\times100=2.058
The 1.36 kg bag is the best buy: it has the lowest price per 100 g.
(b) Give one reason that is not about price for buying the 340 g bag.
Coffee goes stale once it is opened. Someone who drinks a little coffee would finish a small bag while it is fresh. (Also: less money to pay today; trying a new flavour.)
(c) Four 340 g bags hold 1.36 kg. How much do you save by buying one 1.36 kg bag instead of four 340 g bags?
Four small bags: 4\times\$8.99=\$35.96. Saving: \$35.96-\$27.99=\$7.97. You save $7.97.
Per 100 g on the calculator: 8.99÷340×100ENTER. Change 1.36 kg to 1\,360 g first, so every size uses the same unit.

Markup, Tax and Sales

Example 5

The bike shop

A bike shop pays a wholesale price of $420.00 for a bike and marks it up 45%.

(a) What is the retail price?
Markup: 420\times0.45=\$189.00
Retail: 420+189=\$609.00 (or 420\times1.45)
(b) What does the bike cost with tax in Alberta (5% GST)?
609\times1.05=\$639.45
The bike costs $639.45 in Alberta.
(c) What would it cost in Ontario (13% HST)?
609\times1.13=688.17
The bike costs $688.17 in Ontario.
(d) The shop buys helmets for $32.00 and sells them for $56.00. What is the markup percent?
Markup: 56-32=\$24.00
24\div32\times100=75\%. The markup is 75%.
Example 6

The winter coat sale

A winter coat that regularly sells for $149.00 is on sale for 35% off.

(a) Find the discount and the sale price.
Discount: 149\times0.35=\$52.15
Sale price: 149-52.15=\$96.85 (or 149\times0.65)
(b) Find the GST and the total you pay in Calgary.
Tax goes on the sale price.
GST: 96.85\times0.05=4.8425\approx\$4.84
Total: 96.85\times1.05=101.6925\approx\$101.69
(c) Another store sells the same coat for $104.30 (before tax). What percent decrease is that from $149.00?
Decrease: 149.00-104.30=\$44.70. 44.70\div149\times100=30\%. It is a 30% decrease.
!
Tax goes on the sale price, not on the regular price. Take the discount off first, then add the GST.

Example 7

Two stores, two deals

Runners cost $90.00 at two stores. Store A: 25% off, then an extra 10% off at the till. Store B: $30 off.

(a) Find the price at each store before tax. Which is cheaper?
Store A: 90\times0.75=\$67.50, then 67.50\times0.90=\$60.75
Store B: 90-30=\$60.00
Store B is cheaper by $0.75.
(b) Is “25% off, then 10% off” the same as 35% off? Explain.
No. 35% off would be 90\times0.65=\$58.50.
The extra 10% is taken off $67.50, not off $90, so it saves only $6.75 instead of $9.00.
Two discounts in a row give less than their sum.

Think: would 10% off first, then 25% off, give a different price? (No: 90\times0.90\times0.75=\$60.75, the same. The order of the two discounts does not matter.)

Check Example 7

Why two discounts are not added

Think of the price as 100 squares. The second discount is a percent of the squares that are left, not of all 100.

Currency Exchange

!
Watch out. On a bank’s board, “We sell” is the rate when you buy foreign cash (higher), and “We buy” is the rate when you sell it back (lower). Rates in this booklet are examples.
Example 8

The bank board

A bank in Calgary shows these rates, in Canadian dollars for 1 unit of each currency.

CurrencyWe buyWe sell
US dollar (USD)1.341.42
Euro (EUR)1.461.56
Mexican peso (MXN)0.0720.081
(a) Kayla buys $300 USD in cash for a trip to Montana. How much does it cost her in Canadian dollars?
The bank sells USD at 1.42. 300\times1.42=\$426.00
It costs her $426.00 CAD.
(b) She comes home with $40 USD and sells it back to the bank. How much does she get? How much did she lose on that $40 USD?
The bank buys USD at 1.34: 40\times1.34=\$53.60
She paid 40\times1.42=\$56.80 for it.
She lost 56.80-53.60=\$3.20.
(c) In Montana a hoodie costs $45.00 USD. Estimate the cost in Canadian dollars in your head, then find it exactly using 1\text{ USD}=1.38\text{ CAD}. Why does estimating help when you shop?
Estimate: 45\times1.4\approx45+18=\$63
Exact: 45\times1.38=\$62.10
A quick estimate tells you what you are really paying in your own money, so you can compare with prices at home and stay on budget.
(d) Her friend buys 3 000 Mexican pesos at this bank for a trip to Cancun. How much does that cost in Canadian dollars?
The bank sells MXN at 0.081.
3\,000\times0.081=\$243.00
It costs $243.00 CAD.

Think: in (b), Kayla never spent that $40 USD. Why did she still lose money? (She bought it at the bank’s higher rate and sold it back at the lower rate. The bank keeps the difference: \$0.08 on every US dollar.)

Key ideas

Ratios and proportions. Same units first, then divide both terms by the same number. Sharing: add the parts, find one part, multiply. Proportion: matching units in matching places, then scale or cross-multiply.
Rates and unit price. A unit rate is “per 1”. Fuel economy: litres ÷ km × 100. Unit price: price ÷ quantity, with the same unit for every size. The best buy is about price and what you need.
Percents. Percent → decimal, then multiply. Markup: × (1 + rate). Discount: × (1 − rate). Tax goes on the final price: × 1.05 in Alberta. Percent change: change ÷ original × 100. Discounts in a row are not added.
Currency exchange. The rate starts with the currency you have: multiply; with the one you want: divide. You buy foreign cash at the bank’s higher “We sell” rate and sell it back at the lower “We buy” rate. Estimate with × 1.4.
