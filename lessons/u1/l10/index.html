Currency Exchange: Converting, and Buy vs Sell Rates

A road trip to Montana, a holiday in Mexico, a hoodie from a US website. Today we change Canadian dollars into other money and back, estimate foreign prices in our heads, and see why the bank’s two rates cost you money.

I can…
  • read an exchange rate and tell which currency is worth more
  • convert money both ways: decide to multiply or divide, and check if the answer should be bigger or smaller
  • use a rate table
  • estimate a US price in Canadian dollars in my head
  • use a bank’s buying and selling rates, and find the money lost changing it back
  • compare total costs with fees, and shopping across the border
N1 Solve problems that involve unit pricing and currency exchange, using proportional reasoning.
1

What an Exchange Rate Means

Each country has its own money, called its currency. Banks write each currency with a three-letter code.

An exchange rate tells you how much of one currency you get for 1 unit of another currency.

CurrencyCodeUsed in
Canadian dollarCADCanada
US dollarUSDUnited States
euroEURFrance, Germany, Italy, Spain and many other European countries
British poundGBPUnited Kingdom (England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland)
Mexican pesoMXNMexico
Japanese yenJPYJapan
Key idea   A rate can be written two ways:
1\text{ USD}=1.38\text{ CAD}  means  one US dollar costs $1.38 in Canadian money.
1\text{ CAD}=0.725\text{ USD}  means  one Canadian dollar is worth about 72 US cents.
If the number after the equals sign is bigger than 1, then 1 unit of the first currency is worth more than 1 unit of the second.
i
Note. The exchange rates in this lesson are examples. Real rates change every day; banks post them on their websites.
Think 1\text{ EUR}=1.55\text{ CAD} and 1\text{ USD}=1.38\text{ CAD}. Which is worth more: one euro or one US dollar?
One euro. It costs $1.55 in Canadian money, and a US dollar costs only $1.38.
Example 1

One US dollar, one yen

Use 1\text{ USD}=1.38\text{ CAD}.

(a) Which is worth more, one US dollar or one Canadian dollar? one US dollar
One US dollar. 1.38 is bigger than 1, so one US dollar buys more than one Canadian dollar.
(b) An exchange rate is a unit rate, so you can scale it up. Complete the table.
USD$1.00$10.00$50.00$200.00
CAD$1.38$13.80$69.00$276.00
Multiply 1.38 by each number of US dollars: 10\times1.38=13.80, 50\times1.38=69.00, 200\times1.38=276.00.
(c) In Japan, 1\text{ CAD}=108\text{ JPY}. Is one yen worth more or less than one Canadian cent? Explain.
$1.00 CAD is 100 cents, and it buys 108 yen. You get more yen than cents, so one yen is worth a little less than one cent.
2

Converting Between Currencies

Converting money is a proportion problem: the rate is a unit rate (for 1 unit), and you scale it up. Look at which currency the rate starts with.

Key idea
Step 1. Write the rate as  1\text{ [one currency]}=\text{rate [other currency]}.
Step 2. If the rate starts with the currency of the amount you have, multiply: amount × rate.
If it starts with the other currency, divide: amount ÷ rate.
Step 3. Round money to the cent at the end (yen to the whole yen). Write the currency code.
!
Watch out. Before you press ENTER, ask: should my number get bigger or smaller? A US dollar is worth more than a Canadian dollar, so the same price is a bigger number in CAD than in USD. If your CAD answer is smaller than the US price, you multiplied when you should have divided (or the other way around).
To change $350.00 CAD at 1\text{ CAD}=0.725\text{ USD}: 350 × 0.725 ENTER shows 253.75.
Think Change $100.00 CAD into US dollars at 1\text{ CAD}=0.725\text{ USD}. Then change it straight back at 1\text{ USD}=1.38\text{ CAD}. About how much do you get back?
100\times0.725=72.50 USD, then 72.50\times1.38=100.05. About $100: the two ways of writing one rate undo each other (the 5¢ is rounding in the rates). A bank is different: see Section 4.
Example 2

Hamza’s cash and a hotel in Montana

(a) Hamza changes $350.00 CAD into US dollars. The rate is 1\text{ CAD}=0.725\text{ USD}. How many US dollars does he get?
The rate starts with CAD, the currency he has, so multiply.
350.00\times0.725=253.75
Hamza gets $253.75 USD.
(Check: fewer US dollars than Canadian dollars. Makes sense.)
(b) A hotel in Great Falls, Montana, costs $164.00 USD a night. The rate is 1\text{ USD}=1.38\text{ CAD}. What is the cost in Canadian dollars?
The rate starts with USD, the currency of the price, so multiply.
164.00\times1.38=226.32
The hotel costs $226.32 CAD a night.
(Check: the CAD number is bigger. Makes sense.)

Only one rate posted? Divide

Sometimes only one rate is posted, and it starts with the currency you want. Then divide, or set up a proportion and cross-multiply.

$450.00 CAD at 1\text{ EUR}=1.55\text{ CAD}: 450 ÷ 1.55 ENTER shows 290.3225806, so about 290.32 EUR.

Why divide? Set up a proportion

The rate is a ratio, so the same ratio holds for any amount. Cross-multiplying turns the proportion into one division.

Example 3

Euros for Italy, a bento in Tokyo

A travel website shows only one rate: 1\text{ EUR}=1.55\text{ CAD}.

(a) Sofia has $450.00 CAD. How many euros can she get?
She has CAD and wants EUR. The rate starts with EUR, so divide.
450.00\div1.55=290.322\ldots
Or as a proportion: \dfrac{1\text{ EUR}}{1.55\text{ CAD}}=\dfrac{x}{450}, so 1.55x=450 and x=290.32.
Sofia gets 290.32 EUR.
(b) A train ticket in Italy costs 38.40 EUR. What is that in CAD?
She has EUR. The rate starts with EUR, so multiply.
38.40\times1.55=59.52
The ticket costs $59.52 CAD.
(c) A shop in Tokyo posts only 1\text{ CAD}=108\text{ JPY}. A bento lunch box costs 1250 JPY. What is that in CAD?
The price is in JPY. The rate starts with CAD, the currency we want, so divide.
1250\div108=11.574\ldots   The lunch costs about $11.57 CAD.

Estimating Canadian Prices in Your Head

When you shop in the US, the price tags are in US dollars. You usually have no calculator out, but you still need to know what things really cost. With 1\text{ USD}\approx1.38\text{ CAD}, each US dollar costs a bit more than 1\tfrac{1}{3} Canadian dollars.

Key idea   Estimating a US price in CAD (when 1 USD is about 1.35 to 1.40 CAD)
Step 1. Round the US price to a friendly number.   Step 2. Find about a third of it (÷ 3).   Step 3. Add it on.
Or multiply by 1.4 in your head (× 1 plus × 0.4). This gives a slightly high estimate, which is safe for a budget.
★
Why it matters. Estimating lets you keep to your budget, tell whether a “sale” is really cheaper than at home, and avoid a surprise when your credit card bill arrives in Canadian dollars. It also helps you catch a calculator mistake. The same idea works for other currencies: with 1\text{ EUR}\approx1.55\text{ CAD}, add about half.
The × 1.4 way, for the $24.00 ball cap:  24\times1=24 and 24\times0.4=9.6, so about 24+9.6\approx\$34 CAD. The exact price is $33.12, so this estimate is a little high: safe for a budget.
Think In France, 1\text{ EUR}\approx1.55\text{ CAD}. A museum ticket costs 20 EUR. About how much is that in CAD?
Add about half: 20+10=30, so about $30 CAD. (Exact: 20\times1.55=\$31.00.)

Bigger or smaller? Catch a calculator mistake

A US dollar is worth more than a Canadian dollar, so a US price is always a bigger number in CAD. An estimate tells you right away if the calculator answer is wrong.

Example 5

Shopping in the US

Use 1\text{ USD}=1.38\text{ CAD}. Estimate first, then find the exact cost.

(a) A ball cap costs $24.00 USD.
Estimate: a third of 24 is 8;  24+8=32, about $32 CAD.
Exact: 24.00\times1.38=33.12, so $33.12 CAD.
The estimate is close.
(b) Running shoes cost $119.99 USD.
Estimate: 119.99\approx120; a third of 120 is 40;  120+40=160, about $160 CAD.
Exact: 119.99\times1.38=165.586\ldots, so $165.59 CAD.
(c) Kayden’s calculator says a $45.00 USD hoodie costs $32.61 CAD. Is that reasonable? What went wrong?
Estimate: 45+15=60, so it should be about $60 CAD. $32.61 is too small: the CAD number must be bigger than the US price.
Kayden divided (45\div1.38=32.61). He should multiply: 45.00\times1.38=62.10, so $62.10 CAD.
4

Buying and Selling Rates

A bank or currency exchange does not use one rate. It posts two rates, written from the bank’s point of view, in CAD for 1 unit of foreign money:

  • The selling rate (“we sell”) is what the bank charges when it sells you foreign cash, for example before a trip. It is the higher rate.
  • The buying rate (“we buy”) is what the bank pays when it buys foreign cash back from you, for example after a trip. It is the lower rate.
Example Bank: foreign cash (CAD for 1 unit)
CurrencyWe buyWe sell
US dollar (USD)1.341.42
euro (EUR)1.501.60
Mexican peso (MXN)0.0700.080

The gap between the two rates is how the bank makes money. So if you change money and then change it back, you get back less than you started with.

Key idea
Getting foreign cash (you buy, the bank sells): use the selling rate (higher).
Changing it back to CAD (you sell, the bank buys): use the buying rate (lower).
Either way the bank’s rate is the worse one for you: you pay more, and you get less.
Owen’s US cash at the selling rate: 600 × 1.42 ENTER shows 852. His sister’s: 500 ÷ 1.42 ENTER.
Think An airport counter posts We buy USD 1.31, We sell USD 1.45. Example Bank posts 1.34 and 1.42. Which has the bigger gap? Where would you rather change your money?
Airport gap: 1.45-1.31=0.14. Bank gap: 1.42-1.34=0.08. The bank is better both ways: you pay less for US cash (1.42 < 1.45) and get more back (1.34 > 1.31).

There and back: where does the money go?

Change money into US dollars and back again, and some of it stays with the bank: it sells high and buys low.

Example 6

Owen’s trip to Phoenix

Owen is going to Phoenix. Use the Example Bank board.

(a) Owen buys $600.00 USD in cash. Which rate is used? How much does it cost him in CAD?
The bank sells him USD: selling rate, 1\text{ USD}=1.42\text{ CAD}.
The amount is in USD and the rate starts with USD, so multiply:
600.00\times1.42=852.00   It costs $852.00 CAD.
(b) His sister has $500.00 CAD to change. How many US dollars does she get?
Selling rate again, 1\text{ USD}=1.42\text{ CAD}.
She has CAD and the rate starts with USD, so divide:
500.00\div1.42=352.112\ldots   She gets $352.11 USD.
(c) Owen comes home with $140.00 USD left and sells it back to the bank. How much CAD does he get?
The bank buys USD: buying rate, 1\text{ USD}=1.34\text{ CAD}.
140.00\times1.34=187.60   He gets $187.60 CAD.
(d) How much did those $140.00 USD cost him before the trip? How much did he lose by changing them back?
Cost: 140.00\times1.42=198.80, so $198.80 CAD.
Loss: 198.80-187.60=11.20.
He lost $11.20 because the bank sells high and buys low.
5

Fees, and Shopping Across the Border

Some exchange counters also charge a fee (a flat amount, such as $9.95) or a commission (a percent of the amount). A good-looking rate with a fee can cost more than a worse rate with no fee, so always compare the total cost.

When you buy from a US website with a credit card, the card company converts the price and usually adds a foreign transaction fee, often about 2.5%. To compare with a Canadian store, compare the total in CAD with the store’s price plus GST.

Key idea   Total cost in CAD = (price in foreign money × rate) + flat fee
or   (price × rate) × (1 + commission rate)
The calculator multiplies before it adds: 250 × 1.57 + 9.95 ENTER shows 402.45. A 4% commission: × 1.04.

Lucia’s euros for Spain

Lucia needs 250 EUR in cash for a trip to Spain. Complete the table to find the cheapest place to buy them.

The bank is cheapest at $400.00 CAD, even though its rate is the highest, because it charges no fee.
Example 8

Tyler’s hoodie: online or in Calgary?

Tyler wants a hoodie. A US website sells it for $52.00 USD plus $11.50 USD shipping. His credit card uses 1\text{ USD}=1.38\text{ CAD} and adds a 2.5% foreign transaction fee. A store in Calgary sells the same hoodie for $79.99 plus 5% GST. (Ignore any charges at the border.)

(a) What is the total cost of the online hoodie in CAD?
US total: 52.00+11.50=63.50 USD
In CAD: 63.50\times1.38=87.63
Add the 2.5% fee: 87.63\times1.025=89.82
Online total: $89.82 CAD
(b) What is the total cost at the Calgary store?
79.99\times1.05=83.9895
Store total: $83.99
(c) Which is the better deal? Give one reason that is not about price.
The Calgary store is cheaper by 89.82-83.99=\$5.83. Also, Tyler can try the hoodie on, take it home today and return it easily.

Flat fee or percent? It depends how much you change

A flat fee is the same for $50 or $5000, so it hurts most on a small amount. A percent grows with the amount. The cheapest place for Lucia’s 250 EUR is not always the cheapest for a bigger amount.

1 USD = 1.38 CAD means one US dollar costs $1.38 in Canadian money. A rate is a unit rate: scale it up. A number bigger than 1 after the = means the first currency is worth more.
