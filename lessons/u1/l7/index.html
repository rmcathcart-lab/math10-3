What Is a Percent?

Percent means “out of 100”. The sign % stands for “per hundred”. So 37% means 37 out of every 100.

A 10\times10 grid has 100 small squares, so each square is 1%. The whole grid is 100%. More than one whole grid is more than 100%.

Percent = per hundred. 37\%=\dfrac{37}{100}=0.37 The whole =100\%.
Example 1

Shaded grids

(a) What percent of Grid A is shaded? Write it three ways.
Percent 37% Fraction \dfrac{37}{100} Decimal 0.37
37 of the 100 squares are shaded: 37%, \dfrac{37}{100}, 0.37
(b) What percent of Grid A is not shaded?
100\%-37\%=\mathbf{63\%}
(c) Each grid in B is one whole. What percent is shaded?
One full grid (100 squares) and 25 more: 100+25=125 squares out of 100, so 125%.

Percents, Decimals and Fractions

A percent, a decimal and a fraction can all name the same amount. To switch between them, use these four rules.

Percent → decimal: divide by 100 (move the decimal point 2 places left).48\%\to0.48
Decimal → percent: multiply by 100 (move the decimal point 2 places right).0.3\to30\%
Fraction → percent: divide top by bottom, then multiply by 100.\tfrac34=3\div4=0.75=75\%
Percent → fraction: write it over 100, then simplify.60\%=\tfrac{60}{100}=\tfrac35

If there is no decimal point, it is at the end of the number: 7\%=7.\%\to0.07. Fill empty places with zeros.

!
7\% is 0.07, not 0.7. A one-digit percent needs a zero after the decimal point. Check: 7% is less than 10%, and 10\%=0.10, so the decimal must be less than 0.10.

Quick Percents in Your Head

Some percents are easy to find without a calculator. These are called benchmarks. Use them to estimate, and to check that a calculator answer makes sense.

100%the whole amount
10%divide by 10 (move the decimal 1 place left)
50%half: divide by 2
5%half of 10%
25%a quarter: divide by 4 (or half of 50%)
1%divide by 100 (move the decimal 2 places left)

Build others: 15\%=10\%+5\% 20\%=2\times10\% 30\%=3\times10\% 75\%=50\%+25\% 2\%=2\times1\%

Tipping

$
In Canada, people usually leave a tip of 15% to 20% of the bill at a sit-down restaurant, and often for haircuts, taxis and delivery. A tip is a percent of an amount.

You can build a tip from benchmarks: 15% = 10% + 5%, and 20% = 2 × 10%. Watch the tip calculator do it (Example 5).

Example 5

Supper out

Noor and her brother have supper at a restaurant. The bill is $47.60.

(a) Estimate a 15% tip in your head.
Round: \$47.60\approx\$48.
10% of $48 is $4.80. 5% is half: $2.40.
15\%\approx4.80+2.40=\$7.20
A 15% tip is about $7.20 (a little over $7).
(b) Find a 20% tip exactly, then the total they pay.
10% of $47.60 is $4.76.
20\%=2\times4.76=\$9.52
Total =47.60+9.52=\$57.12
They pay $57.12 with a 20% tip.

Think: why is the estimate in (a) a little more than the exact 15% tip? (We rounded the bill up to $48. The exact 15% tip is 4.76+2.38=\$7.14.)

Finding a Percent of an Amount

In math, “of” means multiply. To find a percent of an amount, change the percent to a decimal and multiply.

Percent of an amount: part = (percent as a decimal) \times amount 35\%\text{ of }260=0.35\times260=91

You can also use a proportion: \dfrac{35}{100}=\dfrac{x}{260}, then cross-multiply. Both ways give the same answer.

!
Estimate first so you can catch a misplaced decimal point. More than 100% of an amount is more than the amount (150% of 40 is 60). Less than 1% of an amount is very small (0.5% of 40 is 0.2).
On the calculator: 0.35×260ENTER. On the TI-30XIIS you can also type 35 2nd%, which divides by 100 for you, then ×260ENTER. Money: round to the cent only at the end.

What Percent Is One Number of Another?

To find what percent a part is of a whole, divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. The whole is the number that comes after “of”.

Percent =\dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}}\times100 18 is what percent of 72? 18\div72=0.25=25\%

If the part is bigger than the whole, the answer is more than 100%. If the division does not come out evenly, round the percent as asked (often to the nearest tenth of a percent).

Example 8

Tests and tips

(a) Priya got 38 out of 45 on her first test and 22 out of 25 on her second test. On which test did she do better?
Test 1: 38\div45=0.8444\ldots\approx84.4\%
Test 2: 22\div25=0.88=88\%
She did better on the second test (88% > 84.4%), even though she got fewer marks. Percents let us compare tests with different totals.
(b) Marco left a $12.00 tip on a $64.00 bill. What percent tip is that, to the nearest tenth of a percent? Is it more or less than 15%?
part = 12, whole = 64 (the bill)
12\div64=0.1875=18.75\%\approx18.8\%
Marco tipped about 18.8%, which is more than 15%.
(Check: 15% of $64 is 6.40+3.20=\$9.60, less than $12.)

Key ideas

Percent means “out of 100”. On a 10 by 10 grid, each square is 1% and the whole grid is 100%. 37\%=\tfrac{37}{100}=0.37.
Percent → decimal: 2 places left (7\%=0.07). Decimal → percent: 2 places right. Fraction → percent: top ÷ bottom, × 100. Percent → fraction: over 100, simplify.
Benchmarks: 10% (1 place left), 5% (half of 10%), 1% (2 places left), 50%, 25%. Build others: 15\%=10\%+5\%. Use them to estimate tips and to check answers.
Percent of: decimal × amount (0.35\times260=91). What percent: part ÷ whole × 100 (18\div72=25\%). The whole comes after “of”.
