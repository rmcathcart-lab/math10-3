Proportions: Finding the Missing Value

Two equal ratios make a proportion. When one number is missing, we can scale up or down, or cross-multiply, to find it. Recipes, maps, fuel mixes, medicine doses and photos all work this way.

I can…
  • decide whether two ratios form a proportion
  • find the missing number by scaling or by cross-multiplying
  • set up a proportion from a word problem, with units that match, and decide whether to round up or down
N1 Solve problems that involve unit pricing and currency exchange, using proportional reasoning.
1

What Is a Proportion?

Proportion: a statement that two ratios are equal. For example, 2:3=4:6 is a proportion. Written as fractions, it is \dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{4}{6}.

To decide if two ratios form a proportion, use one of these:

Simplify both ratios. If they simplify to the same ratio, they form a proportion.
4:6=2:3 and 2:3 is already simplest. Same!
Divide. Turn each ratio into a decimal (top \div bottom). If the decimals are the same, they form a proportion.
2\div3=0.667 and 4\div6=0.667. Same!
Example 1

Proportion or not?

Do the two ratios form a proportion? Show how you know.

(a) 4:6 and 10:15
4:6=2:3 (divide by 2)
10:15=2:3 (divide by 5)
Same ratio, so yes, it is a proportion.
(b) \dfrac{3}{8} and \dfrac{9}{16}
3\div8=0.375
9\div16=0.5625
Different, so no, not a proportion.
(c) 12:20 and 9:15
12:20=3:5 (divide by 4)
9:15=3:5 (divide by 3)
Same ratio, so yes.
Think Do 2:3 and 3:2 form a proportion? They use the same numbers.
No. 2\div3=0.667 but 3\div2=1.5. The order matters in a ratio: 2 scoops to 3 L of water is not the same drink as 3 scoops to 2 L.
Example 2

Fruit punch

Three friends make fruit punch from drink crystals and water. Jordan mixes 2 scoops with 3 L of water. Priya mixes 5 scoops with 7.5 L of water. Marco mixes 3 scoops with 5 L of water.

(a) Do Jordan’s and Priya’s punches taste the same strength?
Compare scoops : litres as decimals.
Jordan: 2\div3=0.667   Priya: 5\div7.5=0.667
\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{5}{7.5} is a proportion, so they are the same strength.
(b) Is Marco’s punch stronger or weaker than Jordan’s?
Marco: 3\div5=0.6
Marco uses 0.6 scoops per litre, less than Jordan’s 0.667, so Marco’s punch is weaker.

Solving by Scaling Up or Down

Often one number in a proportion is missing. We call it x. If a top or bottom number is an easy multiple of its partner, scale: find what the known pair was multiplied (or divided) by, then do the same to the other number.

1
Look at the pair with both numbers known (here 4 and 20). What do you multiply or divide by to get from one to the other? (4\times5=20)
2
Do the same thing to the other pair: x=3\times5=15.
Scaling rule. Multiply or divide the top and the bottom of a ratio by the same number and you get an equal ratio.
!
Only multiply or divide. Adding the same number to the top and bottom does not make an equal ratio: \frac{3}{4} and \frac{3+16}{4+16}=\frac{19}{20} are not equal.

Solve by scaling

Solve each proportion by scaling. Write the \times or \div you used.

(a) \dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{x}{20}
4\times5=20
so x=3\times5
\boldsymbol{x=15}

5\times7=35
so x=8\times7
\boldsymbol{x=56}
(c) \dfrac{18}{24}=\dfrac{x}{4}
24\div6=4
so x=18\div6
\boldsymbol{x=3}
(d) 6:x=30:45
30\div5=6
so x=45\div5
\boldsymbol{x=9}
Think Can you solve \dfrac{6}{11}=\dfrac{x}{25} by scaling? Why or why not?
Not easily. 25\div11=2.2727\dots, not a nice number, so there is no easy \times to see. For numbers like these we cross-multiply (next section).
Math Historyabout 2200 years ago · Alexandria, Egypt

Measuring the whole Earth with a shadow

Eratosthenes ran the great library in Alexandria. At noon on the longest day of the year, the sun was straight overhead in Syene: a stick there cast no shadow. At the same moment, a stick in Alexandria cast a shadow at an angle of 7.2°.

7.2^\circ is \tfrac{1}{50} of a full circle, and the cities were about 5000 stadia apart. His proportion: \tfrac{7.2}{360}=\tfrac{5000}{x}. Scale by \times50: x=\boldsymbol{250\,000} stadia around the Earth.

A city 2500 stadia from Syene, in the same direction. What angle would its shadow make?
\frac{7.2}{5000}=\frac{x}{2500}: 5000\div2=2500, so x=7.2\div2=\boldsymbol{3.6^\circ}. Half the distance, half the angle.

Solving by Cross-Multiplying

When the numbers do not scale nicely (like \frac{6}{11}=\frac{x}{25}), cross-multiply. In a proportion, the two numbers that sit diagonally across from each other multiply to the same answer. These answers are called the cross products.

Cross-multiplying. If \dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}, then a\times d=b\times c.

1
Multiply across both diagonals and write an equation.
2
Divide both sides by the number that is with x.
3
Check: put your answer in and find both cross products. They should be equal (or very close if you rounded).

For example, \dfrac{x}{12}=\dfrac{7}{8} gives 8\times x=12\times7, so 8x=84 and x=84\div8=10.5.
Check: 10.5\times8=84 and 12\times7=84 ✓

!
Multiply along the diagonals (top-left with bottom-right, bottom-left with top-right). Do not multiply the two tops together. It works the same way when x is on the bottom.
One line. Once you have 11x=6\times25: 6×25÷11ENTER gives 13.63636364. Round only at the end.

Why do the cross products match?

Give both fractions the same bottom by multiplying each one by the other’s bottom. The new tops are exactly the two cross products.

Example 4

Solve by cross-multiplying

Solve by cross-multiplying. Round to the nearest tenth where needed. Check part (b).

(a) \dfrac{9}{x}=\dfrac{15}{40}
9\times40=15\times x
360=15x
x=360\div15
\boldsymbol{x=24}
(b) \dfrac{6}{11}=\dfrac{x}{25}
6\times25=11\times x
150=11x
x=150\div11=13.636\dots
\boldsymbol{x\approx13.6}
Check: 6\times25=150; 11\times13.6=149.6\approx150 ✓
(c) \dfrac{2.5}{4}=\dfrac{7}{x}
2.5\times x=4\times7
2.5x=28
x=28\div2.5
\boldsymbol{x=11.2}

Setting Up a Proportion from a Word Problem

The hardest part of a word problem is writing the proportion. Write it in words first, then put in the numbers.

1
Decide which two things are being compared (for example, cups of flour and muffins).
2
Write a word proportion with the same thing in the same position on both sides.\dfrac{\text{flour}}{\text{muffins}}=\dfrac{\text{flour}}{\text{muffins}}
3
Put in the numbers and use x for the missing one. Units in the same position must match (both mL, or both L). Convert first if they don’t.
4
Solve by scaling or cross-multiplying.
5
Ask, “Does my answer make sense?” Then write a sentence answer with units.
Example 5

Muffins

A muffin recipe uses 2.5 cups of flour to make 12 muffins.

(a) How much flour is needed for 30 muffins? Estimate first.
Estimate: 30 muffins is a bit more than 2 batches of 12, so a bit more than 5 cups.
\dfrac{\text{flour}}{\text{muffins}}:  \dfrac{2.5}{12}=\dfrac{x}{30}
12x=2.5\times30=75, so x=75\div12=6.25
You need 6.25 cups of flour (about 6\frac{1}{4} cups). A bit more than 5, so it makes sense.
(b) Lena has only 4 cups of flour. How many whole muffins can she make?
\dfrac{2.5}{12}=\dfrac{4}{x}
2.5x=12\times4=48, so x=48\div2.5=19.2
She can’t bake 0.2 of a muffin, so she can make 19 muffins.
Example 6

A hiking map

On a hiking map, 2 cm represents 5 km of real distance.

(a) Two campsites are 7.4 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they really?
\dfrac{\text{map (cm)}}{\text{real (km)}}:  \dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{7.4}{x}
2x=5\times7.4=37
x=18.5
The campsites are 18.5 km apart.
(b) A trail is 12 km long. How long is it on the map?
\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{x}{12}
5x=2\times12=24
x=4.8
The trail is 4.8 cm long on the map.

Example 7

Fuel for a leaf blower

A leaf blower has a 2-stroke engine. Its fuel must be mixed with gas and oil in the ratio 50:1 (50 parts gas to 1 part oil).

(a) How many millilitres of oil should be mixed with 4 L of gas?
Units must match: 4\text{ L}=4000\text{ mL}.
\dfrac{\text{gas}}{\text{oil}}:  \dfrac{50}{1}=\dfrac{4000}{x}
50x=4000, so x=80
Mix 80 mL of oil with 4 L of gas.
(b) How much gas, in litres, goes with a full 250 mL bottle of oil?
\dfrac{50}{1}=\dfrac{x}{250}
Scale: 1\times250=250, so x=50\times250=12\,500 mL
12\,500\text{ mL}\div1000=12.5\text{ L}
A 250 mL bottle of oil goes with 12.5 L of gas.
!
Convert to the same units first. In part (a), writing \frac{50}{1}=\frac{4}{x} (litres on one side, millilitres on the other) gives x=0.08. But 0.08 mL of oil in 4 L of gas makes no sense: it is far too little.

Proportions at Work

Nurses, photo technicians and landscapers all use proportions. Set them up the same way. Then think about the answer:

Round up when part of a thing isn’t enough.
You can’t buy part of a bag: 3.56 bags means buy 4.
Round down when you can’t make part of a thing.
You can’t bake part of a muffin: 19.2 muffins means 19.
Read the whole answer before you round. 160÷45ENTER gives 3.555555556 bags. Then decide: up or down?

Round up or round down?

Think A school needs 13.4 buses for a field trip. How many buses should it book? You have enough flour for 13.4 batches of cookies. How many full batches can you make?
14 buses: 13 buses would leave some students behind, so round up. 13 batches: there isn’t enough flour for a 14th, so round down. The situation decides, not the decimal.
Example 8

A medicine dose

A nurse gives a child a medicine. The order says 1.5 mL for every 10 kg of body mass. The child has a mass of 24 kg. How many millilitres should the child get? (These numbers are made up for practice. Real doses always come from a doctor or pharmacist.)

\dfrac{\text{mL}}{\text{kg}}:  \dfrac{1.5}{10}=\dfrac{x}{24}    10x=1.5\times24=36, so x=3.6
The child should get 3.6 mL. (Check: 24 kg is a bit more than 2 groups of 10 kg, so a bit more than 2\times1.5=3 mL.)
Example 9

Enlarging a photo

A photo is 10 cm wide and 15 cm tall. To enlarge a photo without stretching it, the width and height must stay in the same ratio.

(a) It is enlarged to 24 cm wide. How tall is it?
\dfrac{\text{width}}{\text{height}}:  \dfrac{10}{15}=\dfrac{24}{x}
10x=15\times24=360, so x=36
The enlargement is 36 cm tall.
(b) Can the photo be enlarged to fill a 20 cm by 25 cm frame exactly, without cutting any off? Explain.
Photo: 10\div15=0.667
Frame: 20\div25=0.8
The ratios are not equal, so no. Some of the photo would have to be cut off (cropped).

Example 10

Grass seed

One bag of grass seed covers 45 m2 of lawn. Mateo’s new lawn is 160 m2. How many bags must he buy?

\dfrac{\text{bags}}{\text{m}^2}:  \dfrac{1}{45}=\dfrac{x}{160}    45x=160, so x=160\div45=3.56 bags
3 bags would not be enough, so round up. Mateo must buy 4 bags.
✓

Key ideas

A proportion is two equal ratios. To check, simplify both ratios or divide (top \div bottom) and compare.
Scaling: find the \times or \div for the known pair, then do the same to the other number. Never add.
Cross-multiplying: \frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d} means a\times d=b\times c. Divide by the number with x, then check.
Word problems: words first, same thing in the same place, units that match. Does it make sense? Round up or down to suit the job.
