The Stick and the Puck

At a sports store, a hockey stick and a puck cost $110 together.

The stick costs $100 more than the puck.

How much does the puck cost?

?
Got an answer? Check it: add the two prices.
The puck is $5, not $10! The stick is \$5+\$100=\$105, and \$105+\$5=\$110 ✓. If the puck were $10, the stick would be $110, and together they would cost $120. Most people say $10 first. A quick check catches it.

Unit Price: Finding the Best Buy

The big box or the small box? The 12-pack or “4 for $5”? Today we find the price of one unit, use it to compare sizes and deals, and decide what the best buy really is: for your wallet, and for you.

I can…
  • find a unit price: price ÷ quantity
  • compare sizes with the price per 100 g or per 100 mL, after changing to the same unit
  • find the price of one in a multi-buy deal, and include shipping
  • choose the best buy for cost and for other reasons
  • use a unit price to find the cost of any amount
N1 Solve problems that involve unit pricing and currency exchange, using proportional reasoning.
1

What Is a Unit Price?

Stores sell many things in packs, bags and bottles. The unit price is the price of one unit: one item, one kilogram, one litre, and so on. A unit price is a unit rate with money on top.

Key idea   Unit price = price ÷ quantity
8 batteries for $11.92:   \$11.92\div8=\$1.49 per battery
!
Watch out. Always divide the price by the quantity (money ÷ amount). If you divide the other way, you get a number that does not make sense. Estimate first: $11.92 for 8 is about $12 for 8, so a bit more than $1 each.
11.92 ÷ 8 ENTER shows 1.49. If the screen shows more decimals, round to the nearest cent: 0.74833… is about $0.75.

Which way do I divide?

Money ÷ amount gives dollars per item. Amount ÷ money gives items per dollar, and that is not a price. An estimate tells you right away which one makes sense.

Example 2

Priya’s salon

Priya owns a hair salon. She buys a case of 36 bottles of shampoo for $89.64. She sells each bottle to customers for $7.50.

(a) How much does one bottle cost Priya?
\$89.64\div36=\$2.49
One bottle costs Priya $2.49.
(b) How much profit (money she keeps) does she make on one bottle?
Profit = selling price − cost
\$7.50-\$2.49=\$5.01
She makes $5.01 per bottle.
(c) What is her profit if she sells the whole case?
36\times\$5.01=\$180.36
Her profit on the case is $180.36.

Comparing Sizes: Which Is the Best Buy?

To compare two or more sizes, find the unit price of each using the same unit. The size with the lowest unit price is the best buy (when cost is all that matters).

A price per gram or per millilitre is a tiny number, so stores often show the price per 100 g or per 100 mL. Find it by dividing the price by the number of hundreds: 525 g is 5.25 hundreds.

Key idea   Price per 100 g = price ÷ grams × 100
The same for mL: price ÷ mL × 100
3
Keep an extra decimal. When you compare unit prices, keep three decimals (to a tenth of a cent), for example $0.950 and $0.905. Two prices that round to the same cent can still be different.
4.99 ÷ 525 × 100 ENTER shows 0.950476…, so about $0.950 per 100 g.

Why price ÷ grams × 100? Count the hundreds

Cut the package into 100 g blocks. The price is shared over the blocks, so each block’s share is the price per 100 g.

Think A 12-pack of pop costs $7.99. A 24-pack costs $13.99. Without a calculator, which is cheaper per can?
Two 12-packs would cost about $16. The 24-pack gives the same 24 cans for $13.99, so the 24-pack is cheaper per can.
Example 3

Two boxes of cereal

A 525 g box of cereal costs $4.99. A 750 g box of the same cereal costs $6.79. Which is the better buy?

(a) Estimate the price per 100 g of each box.
525 g box: about $5 for 500 g, so about $1.00 per 100 g.
750 g box: about $6.80 for 7.5 hundreds, so about $0.90 per 100 g.
(b) Calculate the price per 100 g of each box, and answer the question.
525 g: 4.99\div525\times100=0.9505\approx\$0.950 per 100 g
750 g: 6.79\div750\times100=0.9053\approx\$0.905 per 100 g
The 750 g box is the better buy. It costs about 4.5¢ less per 100 g.

When the units do not match

Sometimes one size is in millilitres and another in litres, or one is in grams and another in kilograms. Change to the same unit first, then compare.

Key idea   1 L = 1000 mL   ·   1 kg = 1000 g
For example: 1.5 L = 1500 mL (× 1000)  ·  650 g = 0.65 kg (÷ 1000)
Example 5

Same unit first

(a) A 740 mL bottle of dish soap costs $3.97. A 1.5 L bottle of the same soap costs $7.49. Which is the better buy?
Change 1.5 L to mL: 1.5\times1000=1500 mL
740 mL: 3.97\div740\times100\approx\$0.536 per 100 mL
1500 mL: 7.49\div1500\times100\approx\$0.499 per 100 mL
The 1.5 L bottle is the better buy.
(b) At the meat counter, lean ground beef is $11.00 per kg. A 650 g package in the cooler is $7.49. Which is the better buy?
Change 650 g to kg: 650\div1000=0.65 kg
Package: \$7.49\div0.65\approx\$11.52 per kg
Counter: $11.00 per kg
The meat counter is the better buy.
(Or: 0.65 kg at the counter costs 0.65\times\$11.00=\$7.15, less than $7.49.)

Multi-Buy Deals and Extra Costs

A multi-buy deal gives a price for a group, like “4 for $5.00”. To find the price of one, divide: \$5.00\div4=\$1.25 each. Read the sign: some stores give the deal price even if you buy just one, but some say “must buy 4”.

Think The sign says “4 for $5.00” and you only want 1 cup. Is the deal good for you?
Only if the store gives the deal price for one ($1.25). If the sign says must buy 4, you would pay $5.00 for cups you don’t want.
Example 6

Yogurt cups

At a grocery store, a single cup of yogurt costs $1.49. The sign also says “4 for $5.00”. A 12-pack of the same cups costs $13.99.

(a) Find the price of one cup each way. Which is the best buy?
Single: $1.49 per cup
4 for $5.00: \$5.00\div4=\$1.25 per cup
12-pack: \$13.99\div12\approx\$1.166 per cup
The 12-pack is the best buy.
(b) How much do you save by buying the 12-pack instead of 12 single cups?
12 single cups: 12\times\$1.49=\$17.88
Savings: \$17.88-\$13.99=\$3.89
You save $3.89 with the 12-pack.

Extra costs: shipping and delivery

If you must pay extra to get the items (shipping, delivery), add it to the price before you divide.

Key idea   Unit price with extra costs = (price + shipping or delivery) ÷ quantity
Use brackets so the calculator adds the shipping first: ( 62.40 + 9.99 ) ÷ 24 ENTER
Example 7

Water bottles for the soccer club

Coach Okafor is ordering water bottles for her soccer club online.

Store A: 24 bottles for $62.40, plus $9.99 shipping.
Store B: 30 bottles for $84.00, with free shipping.
(a) Find the price per bottle at Store A without shipping.
\$62.40\div24=\$2.60 per bottle
(It looks cheaper than Store B, but shipping is not counted yet.)
(b) Find the real price per bottle at each store, with shipping. Which store is the better buy?
Store A: \$62.40+\$9.99=\$72.39
\$72.39\div24\approx\$3.016 per bottle
Store B: \$84.00\div30=\$2.80 per bottle
Store B is the better buy, by about $0.22 per bottle.

The Best Buy Is Not Always the Cheapest

The lowest unit price only tells you about cost. The best buy for you also depends on other things:

Will it spoil?Food you throw out is money wasted.
Do you need that much?A big pack is not a deal if you only use a little.
Room to store it?Is there space in the fridge or cupboard?
QualityIs it the same product, brand and quality?
Money todayCan you afford the higher total price right now?
Example 8

Jasmine’s spinach

Jasmine lives on her own. She eats about 100 g of spinach a week. Fresh spinach stays good for about 5 days. Her store sells a 142 g box for $3.49 and a 1 kg tub for $10.99.

(a) Find the price per 100 g of each size.
142 g: 3.49\div142\times100\approx\$2.458 per 100 g
1 kg = 1000 g: 10.99\div1000\times100\approx\$1.099 per 100 g  (less than half the price per 100 g)
(b) Which is the better buy for Jasmine? Finish the sentence.

The better buy for Jasmine is the 142 g box because she would only eat about 100 g before the 1 kg tub spoiled; she would throw out about 900 g and pay $10.99 for 100 g of spinach.

The 142 g box. The tub would spoil before she could eat it.
(c) Give another reason (not price) that could change which size is the best buy for someone.
For example: a family of five eats spinach every day, so the tub would not spoil; or there is no room in the fridge for the big tub.

Using a Unit Price to Find a Cost

Once you know the unit price, you can find the cost of any amount:

Key idea   Cost = unit price × amount
The amount must be in the same unit as the unit price.

This is the same as solving a proportion. For $2.36 per 100 g, the cost of 350 g is \dfrac{\$2.36}{100\text{ g}}=\dfrac{x}{350\text{ g}}, so x=3.5\times\$2.36=\$8.26.

350 g is 3.5 hundreds: 3.5 × 2.36 ENTER

(b) A 400 g bag of trail mix costs $5.60. At the same unit price, how much would 1.5 kg cost?
Unit price: \$5.60\div400\times100=\$1.40 per 100 g
1.5 kg = 1500 g = 15 hundreds
15\times\$1.40=\$21.00
1.5 kg would cost $21.00.

(c) A box of laminate flooring covers 2.4 m² (square metres) and costs $67.20. Malik’s bedroom floor is a rectangle 3 m by 4 m.

(i) Find the price per m².
\$67.20\div2.4=\$28.00 per m²
(ii) Find the area of the floor.
Area = length × width
3\times4=12 m²
(iii) How many boxes does Malik need, and what do they cost?
12\div2.4=5 boxes
5\times\$67.20=\$336.00
(Check: 12\times\$28.00=\$336.00.)

Malik’s floor, box by box

Lay the boxes on the floor one at a time. Each box covers 2.4 m², so the number of boxes is the area ÷ 2.4.

✓

Key ideas

Unit price = price ÷ quantity (money ÷ amount). Estimate first, then round to the nearest cent.
Compare in the same unit: change L to mL or kg to g first. Price per 100 g = price ÷ grams × 100. Keep three decimals. The lowest unit price is the best buy for cost.
Deals: “4 for $5.00” is $5.00 ÷ 4 each. Shipping or delivery: add it to the price before you divide.
The best buy for you also depends on spoiling, how much you need, storage, quality and money today. Cost = unit price × amount.
