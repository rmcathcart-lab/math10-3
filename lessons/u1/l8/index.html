The Guitar Flip

Maya buys a used guitar for $60. She sells it to a friend for $70.

Later she buys it back for $80, and then sells it again for $90.

Did Maya make money or lose money? How much?

?
Got an answer? Tell the person beside you how you got it.
She made $20. She paid \$60+\$80=\$140 in all, and she got \$70+\$90=\$160 in all. \$160-\$140=\$20. Another way: the first deal made $10 and the second deal made $10. Many people say $10, because the $70 → $80 part feels like a loss.

Setting a Price: Markup and Sales Tax

A store pays a supplier for a helmet. How does it decide what to put on the price tag? And why do you pay more than the tag at the till? Today we follow a price from the cost to the tag to the total.

I can…
  • find the markup and the retail price from the cost and the markup rate
  • use the shortcut: retail price = cost × (1 + markup rate)
  • find a markup percent (a percent increase)
  • find GST, PST or HST and the total cost
  • find what a customer pays after markup, then tax, and complete a receipt
N1 Solve problems that involve unit pricing and currency exchange, using proportional reasoning.
1

Cost, Markup and Retail Price

A store does not make the things it sells. It buys them from a supplier, called a wholesaler. The price the store pays is the wholesale price (or cost).

The store then sells the item for more, so it can pay rent and wages and make a profit. The extra amount it adds is the markup. The price on the tag is the retail price (or selling price).

The markup is usually a percent of the cost, called the markup rate. A markup of 40% means the store adds 40% of what it paid.

Key idea   Markup and retail price
markup = cost × markup rate
retail price = cost + markup
Shortcut: retail price = cost × (1 + markup rate)
For example, a 40% markup: cost × 1.40
!
Watch out. Change the percent to a decimal first: 40% = 0.40,  5% = 0.05 (not 0.5),  180% = 1.80. A markup can be more than 100%. Then the retail price is more than double the cost.

The shortcut works because the store charges 100% of the cost plus 40% more. That is 140% of the cost, and 140% = 1.40.

The shortcut in one line: 48 × 1.40 ENTER shows 67.2, so the retail price is $67.20.

Percent to a decimal: move the point two places

Percent means “out of 100”, so 40% = 40 ÷ 100. Dividing by 100 moves the decimal point two places left. Then add 1 (the 100%) for the shortcut.

Think A store marks up a $10.00 item by 100%. What is the retail price?
100% of $10.00 is $10.00, so the retail price is \$10.00+\$10.00=\$20.00. A 100% markup doubles the price (× 2).
Example 1

A bike helmet

A bike shop pays $48.00 for a helmet. It uses a markup of 40%.

(a) Find the markup in dollars.
40% = 0.40
markup = \$48.00\times0.40=\$19.20
The markup is $19.20.
(b) Find the retail price. Check with the shortcut.
retail = \$48.00+\$19.20=\$67.20
Check: \$48.00\times1.40=\$67.20 ✓
The helmet sells for $67.20.
Example 2

The phone case and the granola bar

(a) A phone accessory kiosk pays $6.50 for a phone case. Its markup is 180%. Find the markup and the retail price.
180% = 1.80
markup = \$6.50\times1.80=\$11.70
retail = \$6.50+\$11.70=\$18.20
(Shortcut: \$6.50\times2.80=\$18.20)
The case sells for $18.20.
(b) A school café buys a box of 24 granola bars for $21.60. It marks them up 150%. What does it charge for one bar?
Cost of one bar: \$21.60\div24=\$0.90
1+1.50=2.50
retail = \$0.90\times2.50=\$2.25
One bar costs $2.25.
2

Finding the Markup Percent

Sometimes you know what the store paid and what it charges, and you want the markup rate. The markup is an increase from the cost, so this is a percent increase: compare the increase with the starting amount.

Key idea
Percent increase = \dfrac{\text{new}-\text{original}}{\text{original}}\times100
Markup percent = \dfrac{\text{retail}-\text{cost}}{\text{cost}}\times100
!
Watch out. Always divide by the original amount (for markup, the cost), not by the new price.
For a cost of $32 and a retail price of $56: ( 56 − 32 ) ÷ 32 × 100 ENTER gives 75. Use the brackets so the subtraction is done first.

Why divide by the original? Check both ways

A percent increase is “how much more, compared with what it was”. Test each answer: the original plus that percent must give the new price.

Think In Example 4(b), the pizza went from $17.50 to $18.90. Use the percent increase formula to check that the increase is 8%.
increase = \$18.90-\$17.50=\$1.40;  \$1.40\div\$17.50=0.08;  0.08\times100=8\% ✓
Example 3

A skateboard deck

A skate shop buys a skateboard deck for $32.00 and sells it for $56.00. What is the markup percent?

Step 1: markup = \$56.00-\$32.00=\$24.00  ·  Step 2: \$24.00\div\$32.00=0.75  ·  Step 3: 0.75\times100=75\%
The markup is 75%.
Example 4

Prices go up

Prices go up for many reasons, not only markup.

(a) A mechanic raises her hourly rate from $96 to $108. What is the percent increase?
increase = \$108-\$96=\$12
\$12\div\$96=0.125
0.125\times100=12.5\%
Her rate went up 12.5%.
(b) A pizza place raises the price of a $17.50 pizza by 8%. What is the new price?
1+0.08=1.08
new price = \$17.50\times1.08=\$18.90
(Or: 8% of $17.50 = $1.40; \$17.50+\$1.40=\$18.90)
The pizza now costs $18.90.

Sales Tax: GST, PST and HST

When you buy something, the government adds sales tax to the retail price. There are three kinds in Canada:

GSTGoods and Services Tax5%, charged by the federal government everywhere in Canada.
PSTProvincial Sales TaxAdded by some provinces on top of the GST.
HSTHarmonized Sales TaxIn some provinces, the GST and the provincial tax are joined into one tax.

Alberta has GST only, so in Calgary you pay 5%. Some things, like most basic groceries (milk, bread, fresh vegetables), have no GST at all.

ProvinceTaxTotalMultiply by
Alberta, territories5% GST5%1.05
British Columbia5% GST + 7% PST12%1.12
Saskatchewan5% GST + 6% PST11%1.11
Ontario13% HST13%1.13
Nova Scotia14% HST14%1.14

Complete the table. The “multiply by” number works just like the markup shortcut: 100% of the price plus the tax.

Rates as of 2026. Tax rates can change, so check before you use them in real life.

Key idea   Sales tax
tax = retail price × tax rate     total = retail price + tax
Shortcut in Alberta: total = retail price × 1.05  ·  Round each tax amount and each total to the nearest cent.
Think A $20.00 phone case in Calgary. Without a calculator, what is the total with GST?
10% of $20.00 is $2.00, so 5% is $1.00. The total is \$20.00+\$1.00=\$21.00.

Estimating the GST

≈
Estimate. To estimate 5%, find 10% (move the decimal one place left), then take half. 10% of $54.99 is about $5.50, so the GST is about $2.75.
The GST, then the total with the shortcut: 54.99 × 0.05 ENTER shows 2.7495; 54.99 × 1.05 ENTER shows 57.7395. Round both to the cent.
Example 5

A hoodie in Calgary

A hoodie at a Calgary store is $54.99. Estimate the GST, then find the GST and the total cost.

(a) Estimate the GST.
10% of $55 is $5.50.
Half of that is about $2.75.
(b) Find the GST.
\$54.99\times0.05=\$2.7495
\approx\$2.75
(c) Find the total cost.
\$54.99+\$2.75=\$57.74
(Shortcut: \$54.99\times1.05 =\$57.7395\approx\$57.74)
The hoodie costs $57.74 with GST.

Two taxes: each one on its own line

In a province with GST and PST, find each tax on its own, then add both to the price. Check with the shortcut.

?
Why work out each tax? A store must keep track of how much GST and how much PST it collects, so receipts show each tax on its own line. The shortcut is quicker when you only need the total. Once in a while the two ways differ by 1 cent because of rounding. That is fine.
Example 6

Headphones in Kamloops

Liam buys headphones for $129.99 in Kamloops, BC (5% GST and 7% PST). Find each tax and the total.

GST = \$129.99\times0.05=\$6.4995\approx\$6.50  ·  PST = \$129.99\times0.07=\$9.0993\approx\$9.10
total = \$129.99+\$6.50+\$9.10=\$145.59  ·  Check with the shortcut: \$129.99\times1.12=\$145.5888\approx\$145.59 ✓
Liam pays $145.59.
Example 7

Calgary or Toronto?

A game console costs $499.99 in Calgary and $499.99 in Toronto (13% HST). How much more does it cost in Toronto after tax?

Calgary: \$499.99\times1.05=\$524.9895\approx\$524.99  ·  Toronto: \$499.99\times1.13=\$564.9887\approx\$564.99
difference = \$564.99-\$524.99=\$40.00
The console costs $40.00 more in Toronto.

Markup, Then Tax

Many problems put the two ideas together. Do them in the order they happen in a store:

Step 1Find the retail price (cost + markup). This is the price on the tag, so round it to the cent.
Step 2Find the tax on the retail price.
Step 3Add to get the total the customer pays.
!
Watch out. The tax is on the retail price, not on the cost. The customer never sees the cost.
After ENTER, an operator keeps going from the answer: 85 × 1.60 ENTER shows 136, then × 1.05 ENTER shows 142.8.

Tax the tag, not the cost

What goes wrong if you find the tax on the cost? The store would collect too little tax, and the total would be wrong.

Think Why does the store round the retail price to the cent before it finds the tax?
The retail price is printed on the tag, so it must be a real amount of money. The tax is then found on that tag price, the price the customer sees.
Example 8

Steel-toed boots

A work-wear store in Red Deer buys steel-toed boots for $85.00 a pair. It marks them up 60%. What does a customer pay, with GST?

Step 1: the retail price
retail = \$85.00\times1.60
=\$136.00
Step 2: the GST
GST = \$136.00\times0.05
=\$6.80
Step 3: the total
total = \$136.00+\$6.80
=\$142.80
The customer pays $142.80 for the boots.

Completing a receipt

A receipt does it all in order: quantity × price for each line, add for the subtotal, find the GST on the subtotal, add for the total, then subtract to find what is left on the gift card.

Key ideas

Markup = cost × markup rate. Retail price = cost + markup, or the shortcut: cost × (1 + rate). Change the percent to a decimal first (5% = 0.05).
Markup percent (a percent increase) = (retail − cost) ÷ cost × 100. Always divide by the original amount.
Sales tax: GST 5% everywhere (Alberta: GST only, × 1.05). Some provinces add a PST; some join them into one HST. Estimate 5%: find 10%, then take half.
Markup, then tax: retail price first (round to the cent), then the tax on the retail price, then add. On a receipt, the GST is on the subtotal.
